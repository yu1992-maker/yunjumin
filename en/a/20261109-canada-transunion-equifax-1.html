Getting TransUnion and Equifax Canada reports without a Canadian phone or address

Nov 9, 20263 min readYunjumin Forum editors

When online verification fails, request free reports by mail with two pieces of ID, or by phone.

Without a Canadian phone number or address, online verification will probably fail. The alternative listed by the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC) is to download the Equifax or TransUnion request form, enclose copies of two pieces of valid identification and mail it; the report is free. You may also call and answer identity questions.

We found no official page describing a process specifically for people abroad. Below, what official sources state and what only third parties claim are marked separately.

Why online requests fail

Both bureaus generally match your name, address and date of birth against their own files and then ask questions only you should be able to answer. We found no official explanation of specific failures; the following are inferences:

  • Your file contains no Canadian address or phone to match.
  • Your file is thin or does not exist yet.
  • The code is sent to a Canadian phone you cannot use.

Option 1: by mail (official route)

FCAC states that you may fill in the bureau's form and mail it with copies of two pieces of valid ID, and that the mailed report is free. Equifax and TransUnion have separate forms, linked from FCAC's page.

  1. Download and print each form from the bureau or FCAC (TransUnion's needs Adobe Acrobat Reader to print).
  2. Complete it. Each applicant needs a separate form on a separate sheet, even in the same envelope.
  3. Copy both sides of two valid pieces of ID.
  4. Mail to the address on the official site and keep proof of posting.

Which ID is accepted

FCAC says only "two pieces of valid identification". We found no complete official list; see the instructions on each form rather than guessing.

Option 2: by phone

FCAC says to call the bureau, answer the identity questions and follow the prompts.

  • Equifax Canada: 1-800-465-7166
  • TransUnion Canada: 1-800-663-9980

These numbers are from FCAC. We found no official statement on whether the 1-800 numbers work, or are free, from the United States; check the bureaus' contact pages. If your file lacks the relevant details, you may be unable to answer the questions by phone either.

Option 3: other routes

  • In person: TransUnion's site mentions a walk-in centre in Burlington; third parties give weekday hours of 9:00 to 16:00 Eastern. An option if you are in Canada.
  • Fax: several third-party guides list an Equifax fax number, 514-355-8502, which we did not find on an official Equifax page. Verify at equifax.ca before faxing copies of ID.

Before you post

  • Return address: the report goes to the address on the form. We found no official statement on mailing outside Canada. If the form accepts a U.S. address you may try it; otherwise call.
  • Privacy: copy only the documents requested.
  • Send separately: the two bureaus process requests independently and do not forward them.
  • The file may be empty: if you have never used credit in Canada, "no file" is a normal result.

FAQ

Does a mailed request cost anything?

FCAC says the mailed report is free, and TransUnion says the Consumer Disclosure is free. Paid scores and monitoring are separate from the free statutory report.

How long does it take?

FCAC's page gives no processing time. Equifax mentions about 5 to 10 days for delivery. We found no figure for TransUnion; international mail will take longer, so apply early.

What ID can I use without a Canadian address?

Official pages say only "two pieces of valid identification" and do not say whether foreign documents are accepted from abroad. Call or write to the bureau.

The bureaus' and FCAC's official pages prevail; addresses, numbers and forms may change. For immigration, tax or credit disputes, consult a licensed CPA or lawyer.
