How Soon Can You Use a UK eVisa to Open a Bank Account?

Nov 9, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

A new UK eVisa is usually viewable and able to generate share codes within 24 hours, but whether a bank accepts it, and whether it wants proof of address, varies by bank. Ask first.

Short answer: you usually do not have to wait long after receiving an eVisa. According to GOV.UK, once your eVisa is linked to your UKVI account it is normally available straight away, taking up to about 24 hours. But being able to see your eVisa and having a bank accept it are two different things. The government page does not mention banks, banks and sources differ, and it is best to check before you apply.

1. How quickly the eVisa account works

According to the GOV.UK eVisa page:

  • You receive an eVisa once a visa or other immigration permission is granted.
  • You need a free UKVI account to view it.
  • Once linked to your account it is usually available immediately, though it can take up to 24 hours.
  • A vignette (visa sticker) remains valid until it expires.

Wise's guide also says an expired BRP can still be used to create a UKVI account or obtain a share code for up to 18 months after expiry (a third-party source; rely on official guidance).

2. What a share code is

According to the GOV.UK share code page:

  • A share code is valid for 90 days, can be reused until it expires and can be regenerated whenever needed.
  • The page's examples are employers, landlords and travel.
  • It does not mention banks.

The3million notes that without a UKVI account you can generate share codes only for right to work or right to rent; with an account you can generate codes for "other purposes." If a bank asks for a share code and you can offer only a work or rent code, the group suggests giving your BRP instead (for those who still hold one).

4. Steps before opening an account

  1. Confirm your eVisa is active and that you can log in to your UKVI account.
  2. Generate a share code from the account and choose the purpose carefully; if a bank wants an "other purposes" code, you need an account to create one.
  3. Have your passport, a UK address (tenancy agreement, temporary address and so on, depending on the bank) and your eVisa details ready.
  4. Check each bank's website or customer service for its requirements for newcomers and visa holders before choosing.
  5. If refused, ask why (address, identity, or the system not recognising your eVisa), then try another bank or add documents.
  6. As a fallback, look at basic bank accounts; some sources say HSBC offers one, subject to the bank's own conditions.

5. Common pitfalls

  • Expired share codes: they last 90 days, so do not generate one months in advance.
  • Staff unfamiliar with eVisas: some branches still expect a physical card; bring a printout of your account page and the official guidance.
  • Address problems: the most common sticking point for new arrivals without a settled address; sorting housing first usually makes opening an account smoother.
  • Relying on second-hand guides: many "same-day account" claims come from blogs or affiliate sites, so check the bank's own website.

FAQ

1. Can I open an account on the day my eVisa arrives?

The eVisa is generally viewable the same day or within 24 hours, but same-day opening depends on the bank's identity and address checks; there is no official standard timeline.

2. Does a bank always need a share code?

No. The government page does not mention banks; some banks may need only your passport and address, while others will check your status. Follow the bank's website.

3. Can I open an account without a UKVI account?

That depends on the bank. But according to The3million, without an account you can generate only work or rent share codes, so setting one up first, which is free, is advisable.
