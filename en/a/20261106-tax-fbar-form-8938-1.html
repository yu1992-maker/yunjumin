Green-card holder with savings and rental income in China: FBAR and Form 8938?

Nov 6, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

FBAR applies above $10,000 in aggregate; Form 8938 starts at $50,000/$75,000. The property itself is not reported, but rent is taxable.

Green-card holders are US tax residents. If your Chinese bank accounts together exceed $10,000 at any point in the year, you must file an FBAR. If those deposits plus other specified foreign financial assets exceed the Form 8938 threshold (single, living in the US: above $50,000 at year-end or $75,000 at any time), you also attach Form 8938 to your return. A property in China you own directly goes on neither form, but the rent must be reported on Form 1040.

Why green-card holders must report

The IRS says US tax residents report worldwide income, including interest, dividends, wages and rent, wherever earned. Green-card holders are generally tax residents, and FBAR covers US residents. The IRS FBAR page does not itself define "resident"; see FinCEN's FBAR materials or a professional.

FBAR (FinCEN Form 114)

  • Threshold: a financial interest in, or signature or other authority over, foreign financial accounts whose aggregate value exceeded $10,000 at any time during the year.
  • Aggregation: three banks with $4,000 each total $12,000, so you file.
  • Deadline: 15 April of the following year, with an automatic extension to 15 October.
  • Filing: online through FinCEN's BSA E-Filing system, not with your tax return. The IRS does not accept the old TD F 90-22.1 or printed Form 114; paper filing requires a FinCEN exemption.

Form 8938 (FATCA)

Form 8938 goes to the IRS with Form 1040 and covers specified foreign financial assets. IRS thresholds:

Filing statusLiving in the US: year-end / any timeLiving abroad: year-end / any time
Single$50,000 / $75,000$200,000 / $300,000
Married filing jointly$100,000 / $150,000$400,000 / $600,000

The IRS defines living abroad as having a tax home abroad and being outside the US for at least 330 days in a 12-month period, for citizens. The page does not clearly say whether green-card holders can use this tier; ask a CPA. If you are not required to file an income tax return, you do not file Form 8938.

FBAR versus Form 8938

ItemFBARForm 8938
Filed withFinCEN (online)IRS (with Form 1040)
ThresholdAggregate above $10,000See table above; much higher
Deadline15 April, auto-extended to 15 OctoberSame as Form 1040, including extensions
Non-account foreign assetsNot reportedReported

The IRS states that the two forms have different rules and definitions, some accounts appear on only one, and filing Form 8938 does not replace the FBAR.

Your case: deposits plus rent

  1. Chinese bank deposits: current, fixed-term and savings accounts count; for Alipay or WeChat balances held with financial institutions, judge by account type and keep statements. File FBAR if the aggregate exceeds $10,000.
  2. Form 8938: total all specified foreign financial assets, including bank accounts, and compare with the thresholds.
  3. The property itself: IRS Form 8938 FAQs say directly held foreign real estate, whether lived in or rented out, is not a specified foreign financial asset. If held through a company or other entity, your interest in the entity is reportable. Directly held property is not on the FBAR either.
  4. Rent: worldwide income, reported on Form 1040, usually via Schedule E. Report deposit interest too.
  5. Values: monthly statements can be used to estimate maximum account values. Convert to dollars using the rates specified in IRS and FinCEN instructions.

Tax already paid in China

US rules generally allow a foreign tax credit (Form 1116) for Chinese tax on rent and similar income. Online sources say each income category needs its own form, the credit is limited and unused amounts can be carried over; some preparers note an exemption from Form 1116 when foreign tax is under $300 (single) or $600 (joint) and other conditions are met. These details come from preparer blogs, not IRS text; check the Form 1116 instructions. Whether treaty positions require Form 8833 is disputed online; ask a CPA.

Penalties

  • Form 8938: $10,000 for failure to file; up to a further $50,000 if you continue after IRS notice; plus a possible 40% penalty on underpayments tied to undisclosed assets. Reasonable cause can excuse penalties.
  • FBAR: the IRS says civil or criminal penalties may apply, adjusted for inflation each year. Secondary sources put the 2025 non-wilful maximum at about $16,536; in Bittner (2023) the Supreme Court held non-wilful penalties apply per report, not per account. The wilful maximum is the greater of $165,353 or 50% of the balance, though sources differ on which year these figures belong to; check FinCEN's current penalty table.

Catching up

  1. Compile past maximum balances, interest, rent and Chinese tax paid.
  2. Do not decide on your own whether the failure was non-wilful.
  3. Have a CPA or lawyer assess voluntary disclosure, streamlined procedures or straightforward amended filings.
  4. Set an annual reminder to handle both forms at tax time.

FAQ

I only have $8,000 in China. Do I file?

If your foreign accounts never together exceeded $10,000 at any time, no FBAR is due, and Form 8938 is unlikely. Exceeding it for a single day is enough to trigger filing.

My property rose in value. Does it go on Form 8938?

Directly held foreign real estate is not reportable on Form 8938, but gains on sale are reportable as capital gains.

If I file an FBAR, do I still need Form 8938?

Assess each separately. The IRS is explicit that neither replaces the other.
