How to request your full credit file in Canada under PIPEDA

Nov 9, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Channels, documents, a sample letter, the 30-day limit and how to complain.

The simplest way to see your Canadian credit file is to ask Equifax or TransUnion directly, online, by phone or by mail, at no cost. If you want everything a bureau holds about you, including inquiry and correction records, send a written PIPEDA access request; a reply is due in principle within 30 days.

Credit report or PIPEDA request

TypeFor whomHow
Standard reportMost people, checking errors or preparing for a loanOnline, phone, mail or in person; free
PIPEDA access requestThose wanting a fuller file, or following up a refusal or anomalyIn writing, stating what you want

The steps below come from the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (OPC) guidance for businesses in general; it does not address credit bureaus specifically, so the bureau's reply governs. Some provinces have their own credit reporting law, which we did not check one by one.

Route one: the standard report

According to the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC):

  • Online (free): both bureaus allow download or printing, updated monthly.
  • Phone (free): Equifax 1-800-465-7166; TransUnion 1-800-663-9980, with identity questions.
  • Mail (free): the bureau's form with copies of two valid pieces of identification. Use the address on the form.
  • In person: in some provinces, with at least two documents; places are limited.

Checking your own report does not affect your score. FCAC also warns of fake "free report" sites; confirm an official https address.

How long by mail? Sources differ

One third party says about 5 to 10 days for Equifax, others two to three weeks; FCAC gives none. Allow three weeks and keep proof of posting.

Route two: a PIPEDA request

OPC says the request must be in writing (letter or email), sent to the privacy contact usually found in the site footer or privacy policy.

  1. Identify yourself: full name, address, former names and addresses, daytime phone or email.
  2. State what you want: a specific scope is usually answered faster; for everything, write "all personal information you hold about me".
  3. Attach identification: OPC names no documents; bureaus usually ask for two. Mask unrelated numbers.
  4. Mention cost: ask for an estimate. OPC says only a minimal fee may be charged, with notice and your agreement.
  5. Ask for an alternative format if you need one because of a disability.
  6. Keep copies: tracked mail or sent email, with the date.

Sample letter

Subject: Request for access to personal information under PIPEDA
To the privacy contact:
I (name, date of birth, current and former addresses) request access under the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act to all personal information you hold about me, including my credit file, inquiry records, disclosures to third parties and any correction or dispute records. Copies of identification are enclosed. If a fee applies, please tell me the approximate amount first. Please reply within the statutory period to (phone or email).
Yours sincerely, name, date

How soon they must reply

SituationRule (OPC)
Ordinary replyWithin 30 calendar days
No information heldMust also say so within 30 days
ExtensionUp to 30 more days in certain cases, such as unreasonable interference with operations or format conversion; written notice with reasons within the first 30 days

OPC adds that access does not always mean a paper copy; you may be invited to view the file.

Errors or refusals

  1. Ask for correction with evidence. If you disagree, your objection can be recorded, and third parties that received the information should be told.
  2. Talk first: OPC suggests direct resolution, as many cases are misunderstandings.
  3. Complain to OPC: 1-800-282-1376; 30 Victoria Street, Gatineau, QC K1A 1H3.

A British Columbia source mentions six months to complain after a refusal. We could not verify this with OPC; ask them.

Points to note

  • You generally see only your own information; third-party content may be redacted and other exceptions apply.
  • FCAC says Equifax scores are free nationwide online, by phone or mail, while TransUnion's are free online only for Quebec and Ontario residents. Third parties differ; check the bureaus.
  • Credit Karma and Borrowell are not bureaus. For the full file, go to Equifax or TransUnion.

Questions

1. Does requesting my report affect my score?

No. FCAC says checking your own report or score has no effect.

2. Do I need a PIPEDA letter?

Not for the report alone. Use it for a fuller file or an uncooperative bureau.

3. No reply after 30 days?

Check for an extension notice (30 more days at most). Otherwise keep your evidence, remind them, then complain to OPC.

Reminder: official pages prevail and rules may change. For identity theft, litigation or complex cases, consult a lawyer or CPA.
