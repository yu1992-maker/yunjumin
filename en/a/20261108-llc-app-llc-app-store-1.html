Does a Small LLC Need a Privacy Policy? App Store Review Requirements

Nov 8, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

If your app or site collects user data, you almost certainly need one. The legal basis, Apple and Google rules, and a launch checklist.

If your app or website collects personal information, including email addresses, accounts, device IDs or analytics, write a privacy policy however small the LLC. For the App Store it is a hard requirement: without a working privacy policy link, approval is unlikely.

1. What the law requires

No single US privacy statute covers every state, so take it in layers:

LawWho is coveredWhat it means for a small LLC
CalOPPACommercial sites and apps that collect personal information from California residents, wherever the operator isCompliance firms such as Termly say any California users trigger a public policy; with wide distribution it is hard to avoid
CCPAFor-profit businesses in California meeting any one test: annual revenue above $25 million; buying, selling or sharing data of 100,000 or more California residents or households; or 50% or more of revenue from selling personal informationA new LLC usually falls below; reassess as data volumes grow
Florida Digital Bill of RightsGlobal revenue above $1 billion plus other tests, such as 50% of revenue from advertising or an app store with 250,000 or more appsGenerally not applicable. A law-firm summary says some provisions, such as child protection, have no revenue test; accounts differ on the sale of sensitive data, and we have not checked the statute
COPPASites and apps directed to children, and potentially general audiences (typically under 13)The FTC stresses verifiable parental consent. Children's products carry the highest risk

So even below the CCPA and Florida thresholds, CalOPPA and the store rules make a privacy policy the default.

2. What App Store review requires

From section 5.1 of Apple's App Store Review Guidelines:

  1. 5.1.1(i) Privacy policies: every app must link to one in App Store Connect metadata and in an easily accessible place in the app.
  2. Contents: what data is collected, how, and every use; that third parties receiving it (analytics, ad networks, SDKs) give equal protection; retention and deletion; and how users withdraw consent or request deletion.
  3. 5.1.1(ii) Consent: obtain consent to collect user or usage data, even if considered anonymous, and offer an easy way to withdraw it.
  4. 5.1.1(iii) Data minimization: request only data relevant to core functionality.
  5. 5.1.1(v) Accounts: if the app supports account creation, offer deletion inside the app; do not force login without significant account features.
  6. 5.1.2 Use and sharing: no use or sharing of personal data without permission; sharing with third parties, including third-party AI, needs clear disclosure and explicit permission; tracking needs App Tracking Transparency.
  7. 5.1.4 Kids: children's apps should in principle carry no third-party analytics or ads; any app that can collect minors' personal information needs a policy and must follow children's privacy law.

App Privacy Details

Apple requires these "nutrition labels" for new apps and updates: what is collected, why, whether it is linked to identity and whether it is used for tracking, including the practices of third-party SDKs. A public privacy policy URL is mandatory on the product page; a privacy choices URL is optional.

3. Google Play is similar

Google Play's help center requires the policy to explain how the app accesses, collects, uses and shares user data, with a link in Play Console and a completed Data safety form. Secondary sources say apps that collect nothing must still submit both, and that apps only on an internal testing track are exempt from the form. Follow the current Play Console page.

4. Pre-launch checklist

  • Inventory what your code and every SDK (analytics, crash logs, ads, login) collects.
  • Host the policy at a public URL that needs no login and will not break, ideally the LLC's site.
  • Put the same link in the app's settings.
  • State data types, purposes, third parties, retention, deletion requests and a contact email.
  • If there are accounts, build in-app deletion.
  • Make the App Privacy and Data safety answers match the policy and the code.
  • For California users, explain how you treat Do Not Track signals, as CalOPPA requires.
  • If you avoid children, say the app is not directed to under-13s and do not design it to attract them.

5. Where to get a policy

  • Write it yourself: cheapest, but it must match the data you actually collect.
  • Generators such as Termly or iubenda: convenient, but check the output.
  • Attorney review: worthwhile for health, finance, children or location data.

The largest risk is a policy that does not match practice, such as claiming "no collection" with an ad SDK in the code. It invites rejection and is what regulators look for.

FAQ

My app collects nothing. Do I still need a policy?

Under Apple's guidelines all apps need a link. Secondary sources say the same of Google Play. A short statement that no personal information is collected will do.

If I am below the CCPA thresholds, am I clear?

No. They decide only whether the CCPA applies. CalOPPA, store rules and, where relevant, COPPA do not depend on revenue.

Must I honor deletion requests?

Apple requires the policy to explain how to ask and account-based apps to offer in-app deletion. Whether state-law duties apply depends on your facts; ask an attorney.

Official sources prevail. Consult a CPA or attorney for complex cases involving children, health, finance, location or advertising. This is not legal advice.
