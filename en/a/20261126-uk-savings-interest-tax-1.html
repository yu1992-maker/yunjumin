UK tax on savings interest: the £5,000 starting rate and the end-of-November check

Nov 26, 20265 min readYunjumin Forum editors

How three allowances stack, the cliff at £50,270 where the Personal Savings Allowance halves, when fixed-term bond interest is taxed, and the 24%/44%/49% rates from April 2027.

For 2026-27, UK savings interest can be sheltered by three stacked allowances: the £12,570 Personal Allowance, a starting rate for savings worth up to £5,000 (only if other taxable income is under £17,570), and the Personal Savings Allowance of £1,000, £500 or £0 depending on your band. With no other income, up to £18,570 of interest is tax-free. HMRC collects anything above that after the event, and GOV.UK says that if you owe tax and have not received a calculation by the end of November, you must contact HMRC. From April 6, 2027, savings rates rise to 24%, 44% and 49%.

Sources: GOV.UK's Tax on savings interest guide, GOV.UK Income Tax rates and Personal Allowances, HMRC's Tax-free savings newsletter 24 (October 2026), HM Treasury's Budget 2026 overview of tax legislation and rates, and HMRC manual SAIM2440. Checked November 26, 2026. Figures are for taxpayers in England, Wales and Northern Ireland; Scotland's non-savings bands differ.

1. How the three layers stack

AllowanceAmount (2026-27)Conditions and taper
Personal Allowance£12,570Used first by wages, pension and other income; only the remainder covers interest
Starting rate for savingsUp to £5,000Only if other taxable income (excluding interest and dividends) is below £17,570; reduced £1 for every £1 of other income above the Personal Allowance
Personal Savings AllowanceBasic rate £1,000; higher £500; additional £0Band is set by other income plus all interest
ISA interestExcludedUses none of the above

Two details decide outcomes. The starting rate looks only at other income, so interest itself does not taper it. The PSA band, by contrast, includes interest, so interest alone can push you into the higher-rate band.

GOV.UK's example: tax on 2025-26 interest is calculated during 2026-27 and usually collected through the 2027-28 tax code, while the 2026-27 and 2027-28 codes may each also carry an estimate based on 2025-26 interest. One year's interest can leave traces in three tax codes.

4. When interest is taxed: the fixed-term bond question

HMRC's SAIM2440 says interest arises when it is received or made available, including when credited to an account you are free to draw on. For fixed-term bonds the manual distinguishes two cases: if early access is allowed with a penalty, interest is taxed each year as credited; if there is no access until maturity, it is all taxed at maturity.

That matters in 2027. A two-year bond paying all interest at a maturity after April 6, 2027 is taxed entirely at 24%/44%/49% and bunched into one year, making it more likely to breach the PSA band and the higher-rate threshold. Annual interest uses two years of PSA.

5. Pro tips

  • Choose the payment schedule, not just the rate. Annual or monthly interest spreads income across years; pay-at-maturity products cost more when rates rise and income is near a threshold.
  • Watch £50,270. If you are close, move some savings into an ISA or shift a maturity date so interest does not halve your PSA.
  • Allocate savings between spouses. Joint account interest is split equally by default; holding savings in the lower earner's name can use their starting rate and larger PSA, provided the money genuinely belongs to them.
  • Correct the estimate in your tax code. If rates fell or you withdrew savings, HMRC's estimate from last year will be too high. GOV.UK suggests multiplying average monthly interest by 12 and contacting HMRC; overpaid tax comes back through payroll or pension.
  • Low earners can reclaim. If total income was below the Personal Allowance, claim within four years of the end of the tax year, using form R40 if you do not file Self Assessment.

6. Common mistakes

  • "No letter from HMRC means nothing to pay." GOV.UK says you must contact HMRC if you owe tax and have no letter by the end of November.
  • "My PSA depends on my salary." The band includes all interest.
  • "Bond interest only counts at maturity." Only where there is no access before maturity.
  • "Joint interest follows who contributed." It is split equally unless you contact HMRC.
  • "Everyone gets the £5,000 starting rate." It is zero once other taxable income reaches £17,570.

FAQ

Do the same allowances cover interest from overseas banks?

GOV.UK says foreign savings follow different rules. New arrivals using the four-year FIG regime or transitional remittance arrangements should settle their status first.

Will the PSA or starting rate change in April 2027?

HMRC's newsletter 24 says both stay the same; only the rates change, to 24%, 44% and 49%.

Does ISA interest count toward £50,270?

No. ISA interest is tax-free and does not affect your PSA band.
