Canada's $10 NSF cap: the larger cost is the merchant's $20 to $40

Nov 23, 20266 min readYunjumin Forum editors

NSF fees are capped at $10 from March 12, 2026. Merchant fees are not, and newcomers can ask for a $0 account in their first year.

Since March 12, 2026, federally regulated banks in Canada may charge no more than $10 per non-sufficient funds (NSF) event on a personal account, no more than once in two business days per account, and nothing when the unauthorized overdraft is under $10. Yet most of the cost of a failed payment lies elsewhere: according to the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC), merchants, landlords and utilities typically charge a further $20 to $40, outside the cap. For an occasional shortfall, a $5 overdraft fee is often cheaper than a $10 NSF fee, and newcomers may ask for a $0 account in their first year.

This article relies on section 10.1 of the Financial Consumer Protection Framework Regulations, the NSF and overdraft chapters of FCAC's Review of banking fees, the Commitment on Low-Cost and No-Cost Accounts, and the Department of Finance release of March 11, 2026. Checked on November 23, 2026. Amounts are in Canadian dollars unless stated.

1. What section 10.1 says

ItemBefore March 12, 2026Since
Fee per eventAbout $45 to $48, up to $50No more than $10
Repeat feesSome banks charged on every retryAt most once in two business days per personal deposit account
Small shortfallsCharged even if $5 shortNone if the unauthorized overdraft is under $10
AccountsPersonal deposit accounts of natural persons; not business accounts
InstitutionsFederally regulated banks and federal credit unions

Three details matter. The two-day limit attaches to the account, not the payment: a phone bill returned on Tuesday and an insurance premium on Wednesday produce one fee if drawn on the same account, but separate accounts are counted separately. FCAC says the limit holds however many times a merchant retries the same payment; a retry after the window can be charged again. And provincially regulated credit unions fall outside this federal rule, so check their own fee schedules.

FCAC estimates NSF fees exceeded $1 billion in 2025, the largest consumer cost among the account charges for which it collected data. The Department of Finance expects the cap to save consumers more than $600 million a year.

2. Worked example: FCAC's $80, $10 and $5

In FCAC's scenario, Nadine has $30 in her account when a $50 phone payment falls due; later a $495 vehicle payment arrives before her $500 paycheque. Overdraft interest is 22% a year, each negative balance lasts five days, and the merchant fee is a midpoint $30:

ItemNo protectionMonthly plan ($5/month)Pay-per-use ($5 each)
Bank NSF fees$20NoneNone
Merchant fees$60NoneNone
Overdraft feesNone$5$10
InterestNone$0.11$0.15
Total$80$5.11$10.15

Under the new rule the bank accounts for only $20 of the $80; three-quarters goes to merchants. The break-even points follow:

  • A monthly plan charged regardless of use costs $60 a year. At $5 each, pay-per-use is cheaper or equal up to 12 uses a year.
  • One failure a year costs about $10 + $30 = $40 without protection, against about $5 plus a few cents of interest with pay-per-use.
  • If the bank waives the monthly fee in unused months, the monthly plan is never dearer than pay-per-use.

FCAC notes that practice varies: some banks waive the monthly fee when unused, others do not; some charge pay-per-use per item, others once a day; student, senior and premium accounts are often exempt. Most banks require repayment within a set period, for example 89 days, and charge about 19% to 22% a year.

3. Why overdraft costs exactly $5

This is not industry custom. FCAC explains that federal law treats overdraft fees as interest, and section 347 of the Criminal Code makes an annual rate above 35% criminal, which effectively limits the charge to $5. If a bank's schedule shows more, ask in writing for the basis.

4. Newcomers: the $0 first year and the 90-day cheque trap

The Commitment took effect on December 1, 2025 and has 14 federally regulated signatories, including the six largest banks. All Canadians may have an account costing at most $4 a month with at least 18 debit transactions and no minimum balance. Signatories must offer the same at $0 to seniors receiving the GIS, RDSP beneficiaries, youth aged 18 and under, students, and newcomers in their first year in Canada.

"Newcomer" is broader than many assume: permanent residents, including those with approval in principle from IRCC; refugees (protected persons); and temporary residents, including holders of study, work or temporary resident permits. Documents are required.

Sources differ: the Department of Finance release of March 11, 2026 lists youth, students, GIS seniors and RDSP beneficiaries for $0 accounts and omits newcomers, while FCAC's Commitment and consumer page both list them as a mandatory group. Quote the Commitment when opening the account.

A related trap is the cheque hold. FCAC says an eligible cheque deposited in person gives immediate access to the first $100, with the rest held for up to four to eight business days. If the account is less than 90 days old, the bank may withhold even the $100 and extend the hold. A new arrival who deposits a first pay cheque, sees the balance rise and schedules the rent can find the payment returned. The Department of Finance says Bill C-15 raises the $100 to $250; on the date checked, FCAC's page still said $100.

5. Advanced moves

  • Raise the alert threshold. Banks must alert you when the balance falls below $100, and you can change the figure. Setting it at your largest automatic payment plus $50 makes it useful. The alert must say how to avoid a fee and how long you have.
  • Move due dates to two business days after payday. Ask landlords, telecoms and utilities whether they allow it; a free change beats any overdraft plan.
  • Price linked-account protection at $5 a transfer. Using your own savings avoids interest, not the fee.
  • Count the whole year. FCAC's example account costs $12 a month unless you hold $2,000, and one day at $1,900 triggers the fee. Missing it all year costs $144 against at most $48 for a low-cost account: a $96 difference, without tying up $2,000.
  • Switch a month before the first year ends. The $0 status lasts for the first year in Canada. The Commitment bars switching fees with the same signatory, so ask what the second year will cost and move to a $4 account if needed; those still studying may qualify for $0 as students.
  • Cite the provision. If overcharged, refer to "SOR/2021-181, s. 10.1" with the dates of both fees. If unresolved, contact FCAC at 1-866-461-3222.

6. Common misconceptions

  • "A failed payment now costs only $10." Merchant fees, service interruption and late charges are outside the cap.
  • "Overdraft protection is automatic." FCAC says the bank must approve it; it is short-term credit.
  • "A small shortfall is free." The exemption covers overdrafts below $10 only.
  • "A $4 account is crippled." The Commitment requires at least 18 debit transactions and free digital statements, plus cheque writing and joint accounts where these are offered to other retail customers.

FAQ

Do fintech accounts charge NSF fees?

FCAC reports that most do not; they decline the transaction. The merchant may still charge its own fee.

Can a work-permit holder ask for a $0 account?

The Commitment's definition includes work-permit holders, for the first year in Canada and subject to documents. A bank's own newcomer package may be better or come with conditions; compare the two separately.

Does the $10 cap apply in the United States?

No. This is a Canadian federal regulation. FCAC cites a CFPB estimate that by 2023, 97% of NSF fee revenue among banks with over US$10 billion in assets had been eliminated.

Reminder: verify with FCAC and your bank. For contract disputes or debt problems, consult a lawyer or licensed credit counsellor.
