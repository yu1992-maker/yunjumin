Canada Child Benefit for newcomers: the 18-month rule and pre-arrival income

Nov 25, 20266 min readYunjumin Forum editors

PRs qualify the next month; permit holders must re-apply in month 19; foreign income counts toward AFNI.

From July 2026 to June 2027 the Canada Child Benefit (CCB) pays up to $8,157 per child under 6 and $6,883 per child aged 6 to 17, tax-free. Permanent residents and protected persons can be paid from the month after they become eligible, before filing a first return. Temporary residents on study or work permits need 18 months in Canada and a valid permit in the 19th month. The amount depends on family net income, foreign income earned before arrival is included, and an early application by a temporary resident does not convert into a CCB claim by itself.

Sources: CRA booklet T4114 (Rev. 26), the CRA newcomers page, its CCB eligibility and application pages, the RC66SCH form page, the July 20, 2026 release from Employment and Social Development Canada, and sections 122.6, 122.61 and 122.62 of the Income Tax Act. Amounts are Canadian dollars. Checked November 25, 2026.

One drafting detail is often missed. Section 122.6 requires the applicant to live with the child and be a Canadian tax resident, but the status test is met by the applicant or the cohabiting spouse. If one spouse is already a permanent resident, the other, newly arrived on a study permit, does not wait 18 months.

The 18 months are tested at the start of each month. Section 122.6 asks whether the person was resident in Canada throughout the preceding 18 months. For someone who landed on June 10, 2025, the window on December 1, 2026 reaches back to June 1, a few days short; January 1, 2027 is the first date that works, so January 2027 should be the first payment. This is our reading of the statute; the CRA decides individual cases.

2. The amount: two-stage reduction

T4114 gives the 2026 to 2027 formula, based on 2025 adjusted family net income (AFNI). Up to $38,237 the benefit is paid in full; $38,237 to $82,847 is the first band; above $82,847 is the second.

Use the latter for the current year.

3. Why income before arrival counts

Section 122.61(3) says that anyone who was non-resident at any time in the year is treated as having been resident throughout for this calculation. T4114 says the same: family net income includes income from all sources, inside and outside Canada, for any part of the year either spouse was non-resident, measured by Canadian rules.

Scenario: a family of four lands as permanent residents in September 2026, with children as above and 2025 net income in China equal to $90,000. From October 2026 the 2025 base year gives about $718 a month. If 2026 income, including the pre-arrival months, falls to $55,000, the benefit is recalculated from July 2027; on this year's formula that is about $12,777 a year, or $1,065 a month. A low first year after a drop in income is how the system works, not an error.

Those who filed no Canadian return for 2025 report income on Form RC66SCH; a spouse still abroad requires Form CTB9. T4114 asks newcomers who became residents within the last two years to attach RC66SCH to RC66. Omitted foreign income is recovered on recalculation from later CCB, tax refunds or CGEB.

4. Advanced techniques

  • Re-apply in month 19. T4114 says an application made before 18 months registers the child only for CGEB and other programs. You must apply again for the CCB once you qualify. Put the date in your calendar.
  • Eleven months and ten years. Section 122.62 requires the application within 11 months after the end of the month claimed; the Minister may extend up to 10 years. Claims going back more than 11 months need proof of status, residence (at least 3 documents), the child's birth, and primary care (at least 3 documents) for the whole period.
  • An RRSP deduction pays twice. It lowers net income. With two children and AFNI between $38,237 and $82,847, a $5,000 deduction adds $675 of CCB (13.5%) on top of the tax saving; with three children, $950. To affect base year 2026, the CRA contribution deadline is March 1, 2027. Newcomers usually have no room in their first year, so plan from the second.
  • File even with no income. T4114 requires both spouses to file every year. If one does not, payments can stop in July.
  • One application, several programs. The CRA uses it for provincial benefits: for example, up to $1,750 for a first child under the BC family benefit, and up to $5,882 in Alberta across the base and working components.

5. Common mistakes

  • "Either parent can apply." The law presumes the female parent living with the child is primarily responsible. If the father applies, attach her signed letter. One payment is made per household.
  • "No SIN, no application." If Service Canada will not issue one, attach an explanation and identity document. The CRA newcomers page says a temporary tax number (TTN) can be used for benefits.
  • "Couples living in two countries can file as single." Separation for work or study is involuntary; you still have a spouse and the foreign income counts.
  • "The CCB is taxable." The CRA says it is not taxable and is not reported on the return.
  • "Shared custody is split by days." Between 40% and 60% is shared custody; each parent receives 50% of the amount computed on their own AFNI. No other ratio is used.

Frequently asked questions

When is it paid?

Generally on the 20th, or the last business day before a weekend or federal holiday. Amounts under $20 a month are paid as a lump sum for the year.

What if my permit is being renewed?

The CRA newcomers page treats people who applied to extend before expiry as temporary residents, and asks you to send the renewed permit so payments continue.

What is needed for a child born abroad?

Proof of birth and a document from the immigration department, such as a PR card or confirmation of permanent residence. Documents not in English or French need an acceptable translation.

Reminder: rely on the CRA website and T4114. For residence questions, multi-year claims or cross-border income, consult a CPA or lawyer.
