UK eVisa share codes: how to prove your status to a bank or landlord

Nov 6, 20263 min readYunjumin Forum editors

A share code is a temporary code proving UK immigration status, valid for 90 days. How to generate one, which type to choose for renting versus banking, and what to do if a bank refuses it.

A share code is a temporary code the UK government issues to eVisa holders so they can prove their immigration status to a third party without showing a physical document. The recipient uses the code and your date of birth to check your status on an official website. The government says a code is valid for 90 days and can be reused during that time.

It works for both renting and opening a bank account, but choose the right type: a right-to-rent code for a landlord, and a "something else" code for a bank.

What a share code is

  • GOV.UK defines it as a temporary code that lets you prove your immigration status without showing your eVisa itself.
  • The checker also needs your date of birth.
  • Codes are valid for 90 days and can be used more than once; you can generate a new one after expiry.
  • When generating a code, the system tells you which personal details the recipient will see.

How to generate one

  1. Sign in to your UKVI account and open the "View and prove your immigration status" service.
  2. Have one of these ready: passport, national identity card, biometric residence card (valid or expired), expired biometric residence permit, or UKVI customer number.
  3. Make sure you can receive the security code by text or email linked to the account.
  4. Choose the purpose and the system generates the code.
  5. Send the code and your date of birth to the recipient; they do not need to see your eVisa.

If you cannot access the account, GOV.UK suggests first checking that your UKVI account is fully set up, or proving your right to work or rent another way.

Renting

  • GOV.UK's right-to-rent page says non-UK, non-Irish citizens may prove their right to rent with a share code or original immigration documents, at their choice.
  • A landlord must not refuse you simply because you offer an acceptable immigration document rather than a share code.
  • If you cannot prove your right to rent, the landlord can ask the Home Office for a check.
  • The rental page does not give step-by-step instructions or a validity period; the 90 days above applies.

Opening a bank account

GOV.UK's share code page gives examples for employers, landlords and travel, but does not mention banks. The banking detail below comes from the citizens' group the3million.

  1. In the View and prove service, choose "prove your status".
  2. Select "something else" as the reason.
  3. According to the3million, these codes begin with the letter S and last 90 days.
  4. Give the code and your date of birth to the bank, which checks it on the government verification page.
Note: the3million warns that a "something else" code cannot be used for right-to-work or right-to-rent checks. Generate separate codes for renting and banking.

If the bank will not accept it

  • According to the3million, some banks and building societies refuse share codes.
  • Point them to the government guidance "Residence documents: information for financial providers", which says an eVisa can evidence identity and immigration status.
  • If you feel unfairly treated, complain to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and you can also report it to the3million.

Proof of address

Newcomers opening accounts at traditional banks are often asked for proof of a UK address. UCL's guidance notes that most banks do not accept hotels or hostels as an address. Several university and charity guides say digital banks such as Monzo, Starling, Revolut and Monese are more flexible, but requirements change and accounts differ; check each bank's current document list before applying.

FAQ

What if my share code has expired?

Generate a new one; the government says you can get a new code at any time.

Can one code serve both a landlord and a bank?

Not recommended. Per the3million, a "something else" code cannot be used for renting or work checks, so generate codes by purpose.

What if the bank insists on a physical document?

Ask the bank to use the official checking service and show them the government guidance for financial providers. If still refused, consider the Financial Ombudsman or another bank.
