Canada's Home Buyers' Plan: $60,000, the 89-day rule and repayment relief to 2028

Nov 26, 20265 min readYunjumin Forum editors

The five eligibility tests for RRSP first-home withdrawals, the 89-day non-deductibility trap, a repayment timeline starting in 2031 and stacking with the FHSA.

The Home Buyers' Plan (HBP) lets first-time buyers withdraw up to $60,000 tax-free from their own RRSPs and repay it over 15 years. Three timing rules cause most of the trouble: RRSP contributions made in the 89 days before a withdrawal become permanently non-deductible to the extent the account's value after the withdrawal falls below them; the home must be bought or built before October 1 of the year after the first withdrawal; and for first withdrawals between January 1, 2022 and December 31, 2028, repayment starts only in the fifth year after the withdrawal year.

Sources: the Canada Revenue Agency's pages on participating in, withdrawing under and repaying the HBP, the CRA's FHSA withdrawal page and its 2025 housing tax tips, with TaxTips.ca as a secondary reference. Checked November 26, 2026.

For newcomers, the first-time buyer test asks whether you lived in a home you owned; the CRA's wording does not limit this to homes in Canada. Anyone who lived in their own home abroad before landing should confirm the four-year test with the CRA or a tax adviser. Newcomers also typically have no RRSP room in their landing year, so what the HBP can deliver depends on what is actually in the RRSP.

2. The 89-day rule: same $50,000, very different outcome

The CRA states that contributions made to an RRSP in the 89 days before an HBP withdrawal are not deductible for any year to the extent they exceed the RRSP's fair market value after the withdrawal. The calculation is done per account and per withdrawal.

ScenarioBalance before the windowNew money in 89 daysHBP withdrawalValue afterNon-deductible
A: last-minute deposit$10,000$50,000$60,000$0$50,000
B: deposited 90+ days earlier$60,000$0$60,000$0$0
C: leave a cushion$60,000$20,000$60,000$20,000$0

At an assumed 30% marginal rate, Scenario A forfeits roughly $15,000 of tax savings. Scenario C shows the alternative: if the account's post-withdrawal value is at least the new contributions, they remain fully deductible. The same rule applies to spousal RRSP contributions; where both spouses contributed in the window, the CRA treats the earliest contributions as non-deductible first.

On the scope of the relief, the CRA repayment page's main text says first withdrawals from January 1, 2022 to December 31, 2028, while an example heading on the same page still reads January 1, 2022 to December 31, 2026. Withdrawals made in 2026 are covered either way; anyone planning a withdrawal in 2027 or later should rely on the CRA's guidance at the time and their annual HBP statement.

4. Pro tips

  • Stack with the FHSA for the same home. The CRA's FHSA withdrawal page confirms you can make an HBP withdrawal and a qualifying FHSA withdrawal for the same qualifying home, provided each set of conditions is met.
  • Couples can each take $60,000. The CRA's housing tax tips confirm both spouses or common-law partners can participate for the same home, up to $120,000 combined.
  • Repayments do not use RRSP room. The CRA says you can contribute and designate repayments even with zero deduction room, though designated amounts are not deductible. In tight years, repay the HBP and keep fresh room for deductible contributions.
  • Repay early to reuse the plan. Once your balance is zero on January 1 you can participate again, and repaying above the minimum lowers later minimums.

5. Common mistakes

  • "A pre-approval is enough." The CRA says it is not a written agreement.
  • "Contributions to my spouse's RRSP count as repayments." They do not, nor do direct transfers from an FHSA or another RRSP.
  • "Take $65,000 and sort out the excess later." Anything above $60,000 is income in the year received, with tax withheld at source.
  • "I can repay slowly after moving abroad." If you become non-resident after buying, repay the balance by the earlier of filing your return for the departure year or 60 days after becoming non-resident, or it is added to income.
  • "Any RRSP will do." Locked-in and group RRSPs generally do not allow HBP withdrawals.

FAQ

Can I buy first and withdraw later?

The general CRA rule is that the home cannot be acquired more than 30 days before the HBP withdrawal, with limited exceptions such as buying out a separated spouse's share.

What do I file each year?

Give Form T1036 to your RRSP issuer for each withdrawal, then designate repayments on line 24600 of Schedule 7 each year until the balance is repaid or included in income.

What if the purchase falls through?

Cancel your participation with Form RC471 and return the funds to your RRSP within the CRA's deadlines, or the withdrawal becomes income.
