Can a UK Switching Bonus Be Clawed Back if You Close the Account Early?

Nov 11, 20266 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Whether UK banks reclaim switching bonuses after early closure: the clawback scenarios, when each bank pays, and how to check your own terms.

not every UK switching bonus carries a clawback clause. Many offers do, however, require the account to be open when the bonus is paid, and some require it to stay open afterwards. Whether closing early or withdrawing money puts the bonus at risk depends on the terms and conditions of your particular offer. Online claims vary, so there is no blanket answer.

Of the material we found, Barclays' official terms are the clearest. Most of the rest comes from comparison blogs and forums. We separate what we can source from what we cannot, and say so where it matters.

What a clawback is, and the common UK scenarios

A clawback means the bank takes back a bonus it has already paid. In practice there are three distinct situations:

  1. Closing before the bonus is paid: you do not receive it. This is not a clawback; you simply failed the conditions.
  2. A requirement to keep the account open for a period after payment: binding only if written into the terms, and early closure may then trigger a clawback.
  3. Bonuses that require ongoing qualification (for example, monthly payments): miss a month and you lose that month's payment, but amounts already paid are usually unaffected.

Barclays: the clearest example

According to Barclays' current switching offer page:

  • £150 switching bonus: you must hold an open Barclays personal account on the payment date, complete a full CASS switch moving at least 3 active Direct Debits, and pay in at least £2,000 of salary within 30 days of opening. The bonus is paid within 28 working days of meeting the conditions.
  • £30 a month, up to 5 times: available only after the £150 has been earned. Each qualifying month needs at least 10 eligible debit card payments and at least £2,000 of salary paid in. The account must be active on the payment date.
  • Consequence of closure: if Barclays learns that the new account has been or is about to be closed, you will not receive the CASS bonus.
  • Will paid bonuses be reclaimed? The page does not say. We found no more detailed terms, so we cannot say yes or no; read the full T&Cs or ask the bank.

Higher-threshold offers: maintaining a balance

  • HSBC Premier £500: requires annual salary of £100,000 or more paid in, or £100,000 or more of savings or investments moved across, with that balance maintained for 3 months. This is where withdrawals carry the most risk: moving the money out early may disqualify you.
  • NatWest Premier Select / Reward £500: pay in £15,000 within 90 days of opening and log in to the mobile app.

These offers are about reaching and holding a condition, not simply collecting the money and leaving.

Where sources disagree

  • A 2026 switching roundup on FreedomIsntFree says most banks now require the account to remain open and active for 3 to 6 months after payment, with early closure triggering a clawback. It names no banks and gives no bank-by-bank periods.
  • Another blog gives a range of 6 to 12 months, and some say Halifax requires at least 3 months.
  • A bonus-hunting site says it has never seen a switching offer with a clawback clause.
  • On the Be Clever With Your Cash page, an October 2021 reply from the author says that if the terms do not require you to keep the account, there is no penalty. That is a years-old comment, not current bank policy. Posts on the MoneySavingExpert forum make a similar point: if the terms do not mention a clawback, the bank generally cannot reclaim the bonus, and most bonuses are paid only after the qualifying period. These are users' views, not legal conclusions.

Conclusion: these claims contradict one another, and we cannot verify that "most banks have a 3–6 month clawback period". Only one point is reliable: the terms of your specific offer govern.

How to check, step by step

  1. Before opening, save the offer page and T&Cs as a PDF or screenshot, with the date. Offers are often withdrawn or amended.
  2. Search the terms for: open, active, remain, close, withdraw, reclaim, recover, deduct.
  3. Confirm two dates: when the bonus is paid, and whether you must keep the account or balance after that.
  4. If part of the bonus is paid monthly (such as Barclays' £30), do not close the account before the final payment.
  5. Before closing, check that the bonus has been credited, then ask the bank in writing whether any clawback applies and keep the reply.

Do not trip over the switching rules themselves

  • Most bonuses require a full CASS switch and closure of the old account. Which? notes that partial switches are not covered by the Current Account Switch Guarantee and often do not earn the top bonus.
  • Many offers exclude people who have previously received a bonus from the same bank. NatWest Group offers, for example, exclude anyone who has had a switching bonus from NatWest, RBS or Ulster Bank, and Lloyds excludes anyone who has received a Lloyds, Halifax or Bank of Scotland switching bonus since 1 January 2023.
  • TSB's website states that applicants must be 18 or over and UK resident. If you are not resident in the UK, find out whether you can open an account at all before relying on CASS.

FAQ

Q1: The bonus has been paid. Will it definitely be reclaimed if I close the account now?

Not necessarily. The risk exists only if the terms require the account to stay open after payment. If they do not, users generally take the view that the bank has no basis to reclaim it. Barclays' page does not say that paid bonuses will be reclaimed, but it does require the account to be open on the payment date.

Q2: What if I withdraw the money?

If the terms only require a one-off deposit, withdrawing usually does not affect the bonus. For offers that require a maintained balance, such as HSBC Premier's 3 months, withdrawing may cost you eligibility or trigger a clawback.

Q3: What if a bonus is reclaimed and the account does not have enough money?

We found no official statement from UK banks on this. Banks generally debit the account, and a shortfall may become an overdraft, depending on the account agreement. If you dispute it, complain to the bank first and then, if unsatisfied, to the Financial Ombudsman Service.

Official terms prevail. Bonus amounts and conditions change frequently. For complex cases involving tax, residence status or large sums, consult a CPA or lawyer.
