UK pension carry forward: unused 2023-24 allowance expires on 5 April 2027

Nov 26, 20265 min readYunjumin Forum editors

The £60,000 annual allowance can be carried forward three years, up to £240,000 this year; the tax-relief cap, MPAA and taper limits, with a £120,000 salary example.

The UK pension annual allowance for 2026-27 is £60,000, and unused allowance from the previous three tax years can be carried forward. 2023-24, 2024-25 and 2025-26 each carried a £60,000 allowance, so up to £240,000 of pension saving this year can avoid an annual allowance charge. Whatever remains of 2023-24 disappears after 5 April 2027. Carry forward raises the allowance, not the cap on tax relief for personal contributions, which stays at the higher of 100% of UK taxable earnings or £3,600.

Sources: GOV.UK, Tax on your private pension contributions; HMRC guidance on unused annual allowances and the tapered allowance; HMRC Pension schemes rates; and Pensions Tax Manual PTM055100, consulted on 26 November 2026.

1. The framework: allowance, order of use, eligibility

  • What counts. The allowance covers all private pensions: in defined contribution schemes, every contribution by you, your employer or a third party; in defined benefit schemes, the increase in promised benefits.
  • Order of use. The current year's allowance is used first, then carried-forward allowance from the earliest year (HMRC guidance; PTM055100). 2023-24 is therefore consumed first and expires first.
  • Membership. HMRC states you cannot carry forward from any tax year in which you were not a member of a UK registered pension scheme or a qualifying overseas scheme. Someone who arrived in the UK and was auto-enrolled only in 2025 has no 2023-24 or 2024-25 allowance to use.
  • No claim needed. Using carry forward does not need to be reported to HMRC; only pension savings above the total available allowance go on the Self Assessment return.

3. Worked example: £120,000 salary, £10,000 saved in each of three years

An English taxpayer earns £120,000 in 2026-27, saved £10,000 a year into a pension in each of the past three years, was a member throughout, has no other income and no extra employer contribution this year.

  • Available allowance: £60,000 this year plus £50,000 unused from each prior year, £210,000 in total.
  • Relief cap: tax relief on personal contributions is capped at this year's UK earnings of £120,000, so the allowance cannot be fully used personally.
  • Action: a net contribution of £60,000 to a relief-at-source personal pension, grossed up by £15,000 of basic-rate relief to £75,000. This uses this year's £60,000 plus £15,000 from 2023-24; the remaining £35,000 of 2023-24 lapses on 5 April 2027.
  • Tax comparison: without the contribution, the Personal Allowance is reduced by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income above £100,000 (GOV.UK, Income Tax rates and Personal Allowances), leaving £2,570 and income tax of about £39,432. With a £75,000 gross contribution, the basic-rate band is extended by £75,000, adjusted net income falls to £45,000, the full £12,570 allowance returns, and income tax is about £21,486.
  • Result: roughly £17,946 back through Self Assessment plus £15,000 added by the provider: about £32,946 of relief on £75,000, or 43.9%. Net personal cost is about £42,054.

The example ignores National Insurance (personal contributions do not reduce it) and Scottish rates; it illustrates the structure only.

4. Pro tips

  1. Route large sums through the employer. Relief on personal contributions is capped by your earnings; employer contributions are not subject to that cap but still count against the allowance. Company directors and employees with salary sacrifice can use carry forward beyond their salary this way. Corporation tax deductibility depends on separate rules; check with an accountant.
  2. Get historic pension savings statements. For defined benefit members, "savings" means benefit growth, not contributions; a promotion or pay rise can absorb much of a year's allowance.
  3. Tapered years carry forward at the reduced amount. Per PTM055100, if a prior year was tapered, only the unused part of the reduced allowance carries forward. The taper cuts £1 for every £2 of adjusted income above £260,000, to a minimum of £10,000, and applies only if threshold income exceeds £200,000.
  4. Allow time before 5 April. Defined contribution savings count when paid; bank transfers and provider processing can lag near year end, so pay at least two weeks early.
  5. Combine with the £100,000 trap. Between £100,000 and £125,140, contributions also restore the Personal Allowance, giving the highest marginal relief. That band is where carry forward works hardest.

5. Common mistakes

  1. "I can pay in £240,000 and get full relief." Relief is capped at the higher of 100% of UK taxable earnings or £3,600 (HMRC Pension schemes rates). On a £50,000 salary, only £50,000 gross of personal contributions attracts relief.
  2. "I can still carry forward after drawing my pension." Flexible access triggers the £10,000 money purchase annual allowance. HMRC states unused MPAA cannot be carried forward; only the alternative annual allowance can.
  3. "I am new to the UK, so I have three years' allowance." Years without membership do not count. A foreign pension does not create membership unless it is a qualifying overseas scheme.
  4. "My provider will stop me if I go over." A scheme only sends a statement if you exceed the allowance within that scheme. Combined excess across schemes is your responsibility to report on Self Assessment, taxed at your marginal rate.

FAQ

Can a non-earning spouse use carry forward?

The allowance carries forward, but relief is capped at £3,600 gross (£2,880 net), so carry forward adds little for non-earners.

Do I have to apply to HMRC to carry forward?

No. HMRC guidance says no report is needed unless savings exceed the total available allowance.

What happens to unused 2023-24 allowance?

It lapses after 5 April 2027 and cannot be carried forward further.
