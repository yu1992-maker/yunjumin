Amex Pay Over Time: Is It Revolving Debt, and Does It Hurt Your Credit Score?

Nov 10, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

How Amex Pay Over Time works, how interest is charged, whether it is reported to the bureaus, its effect on credit scores, and reports that it cannot be turned off from February 2027.

Amex Pay Over Time (POT) lets certain Amex charge cards (Green, Gold, Platinum and others) behave like credit cards, carrying eligible purchases into the next month with interest. Functionally it is revolving debt. On credit scores, Amex says the POT limit is not reported, but third-party sources disagree; what matters is what your own credit report shows.

What Pay Over Time is

  • Charge cards normally require payment in full each month. With POT on, you can pay the minimum or any amount on eligible purchases and carry the rest with interest.
  • Amex says you can choose whether and when to carry a balance, up to your Pay Over Time Limit, which is separate from your overall spending power.
  • Experian's explainer: you pay interest only on purchases you choose to defer, and there is no payoff deadline as long as the minimum is paid on time.
  • An older Doctor of Credit article (2016) said only purchases over $100 were eligible and that enrolment involved no hard pull. Check your account for the current threshold.

Is it revolving debt?

In practice, yes. Amounts carried forward accrue interest just like a credit card balance. How credit scoring treats it, however, is disputed (see below).

How interest works

  • The APR is variable and tied to the Prime Rate. The Amex Business Platinum cardmember agreement, for example, sets it at Prime + 10.99% to Prime + 19.99%, depending on creditworthiness at application. Check your own agreement for personal cards.
  • The agreement states that POT purchases accrue interest from the transaction date.
  • The Business Platinum agreement includes a penalty APR of Prime + 25.99% if you twice fail to pay the minimum by the closing date within 12 billing periods. Other cards may differ.
  • Plan It is a separate feature that splits large purchases into fixed monthly payments with a fixed monthly fee, also within the POT limit, rather than APR-based interest.

Does it affect your credit score?

How to check for yourself

  1. Pull your free reports from Experian, Equifax and TransUnion (annualcreditreport.com).
  2. Find your Amex tradeline and check for a credit limit or "High Credit" field and whether it is listed as Charge or Revolving.
  3. After a statement closes with a POT balance, compare the balance and limit fields for changes.

Reports that POT cannot be disabled from February 2027

  • Media citing Doctor of Credit report that Amex has told cardmembers POT will stay on permanently from 1 February 2027.
  • We could not open the original Doctor of Credit article to verify, and The Points Guy's guide does not mention it. Rely on any notice you receive from Amex and on its website.
  • For now, according to Your Mile May Vary (March 2026), you can turn it off by logging in, searching for Pay Over Time and setting it to Inactive, with no phone call needed.

Should you enable it?

  • Suitable for: a one-off large expense or short-term cash-flow gap, with a clear view of the APR cost.
  • Not suitable for: habitually carrying a balance. The Points Guy warns that carrying balances can cost you points and significant interest.
  • To avoid accidental use: with POT on, eligible purchases may flow into the POT balance automatically (YMMV), so check each statement whether you are paying in full or in part.
  • Effect on new applications: early reports suggested POT could affect new approvals; the YMMV author says recent data show little effect.

FAQ

Q1: Does enabling POT trigger a hard inquiry?

Doctor of Credit's 2016 article said no, and Amex says carrying a balance requires no additional credit review. The article is old; check current terms.

Q2: Will using POT definitely lower my score?

Not necessarily. Amex says the limit is not reported and does not affect scores, though some sources say balances or limits may count towards utilization. Your own credit report is the test.

Q3: If I pay in full, is there any interest?

Interest applies only to purchases you defer; per Amex, paying in full means no interest. Note that the agreement says POT purchases accrue interest from the transaction date, so read your statement carefully.
