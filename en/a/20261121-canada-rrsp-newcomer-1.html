RRSP for Newcomers to Canada: Zero Room in Year One and the $2,000 Cushion

Nov 21, 20265 min readYunjumin Forum editors

RRSP room is 18% of last year’s income, so most newcomers start at zero. A $70,000 example, the 1% penalty and HBP plus FHSA.

RRSP room is 18% of the previous year's Canadian earned income, capped at $33,810 for 2026, so most newcomers have zero room in their landing year, the opposite of the TFSA, which grants a full year's room on arrival. You can park up to $2,000 in the landing year without penalty; anything beyond that is taxed at 1% a month, and real room only starts to build in year two.

Sources: Canada Revenue Agency pages on the RRSP deduction limit, excess contributions and annual limits, the Home Buyers' Plan pages, the FHSA contribution page and Guide T4040; checked on November 21, 2026.

1. The formula, and why year one is zero

The CRA calculates the deduction limit as unused room at the end of the prior year, plus the lesser of 18% of the previous year's earned income and the annual RRSP limit, minus the pension adjustment (PA), plus any pension adjustment reversal (PAR), minus any net past service pension adjustment (PSPA).

The operative word is previous. Room in the landing year depends on Canadian earned income in the year before arrival, which for most newcomers is nil. A footnote in Guide T4040 notes that certain income earned while a non-resident can qualify; the CRA asks people to confirm the types with its international enquiries line rather than assume.

3. Worked example: lands March 2026, earns $70,000

Assume a newcomer who arrives in March 2026, earns $70,000 of employment income in 2026, has no employer pension plan and no qualifying 2025 earned income.

  • 2026 room: $0. A $2,000 contribution attracts no penalty but cannot be deducted that year.
  • Contribute $10,000 in June 2026 instead: the $8,000 above the cushion is taxed at 1% a month, or $80. June through December is seven months, $560 in total, and a T1-OVP is due by March 31, 2027. The excess disappears only when new room arrives on January 1, 2027.
  • 2027 room: 18% × $70,000 = $12,600, below the $35,390 cap. The $2,000 already contributed can be deducted in 2027 or later; it does not need to be contributed again.
  • 2028 room: with 2027 income of $80,000, a further $14,400 is added, plus any 2027 room left unused.

The CRA notes that withdrawing an excess before the end of the month in which it was made avoids that month's 1% tax, but taking the excess out through the Home Buyers' Plan or Lifelong Learning Plan may still leave you liable for the full 1% tax.

Other HBP conditions: a written agreement to buy or build; Canadian residence from the first withdrawal until the purchase; and acquisition or completion before October 1 of the year after the first withdrawal. The CRA has extended temporary repayment relief to first withdrawals made between January 1, 2026 and December 31, 2028: the 15-year repayment period starts in the fifth year after the first withdrawal, so a 2026 withdrawal means a first repayment year of 2031. Any required repayment not made is added to income for that year.

For a couple who both qualify, the theoretical ceiling is $60,000 of HBP plus $40,000 of FHSA each, or $200,000; in practice the FHSA takes years to fill and the RRSP must hold the money first, so the usable amount in the early years is far lower.

5. Pro tips

  • Contribute now, deduct later. The CRA lets you deduct unused contributions from earlier years. In low-income early years you can contribute without deducting, then claim the deduction in a higher-bracket year.
  • Use the $2,000 cushion. Even with zero room, an adult can start $2,000 compounding inside the shelter.
  • The first 60 days. Contributions from January 1 to March 1, 2027 can be applied to 2026, useful for anyone who knows the year's income only at year-end.
  • Spousal RRSP. Contributions to a spouse's or common-law partner's RRSP use the contributor's own room, which can even out retirement income in households with uneven earnings.
  • Early HBP repayment does not shorten the term. The CRA says paying early leaves the 15-year period unchanged but reduces the amount due in the first repayment year.

6. Common misconceptions

  • RRSP room arrives with you, like TFSA room. The TFSA grants room from the year you become resident; the RRSP looks at last year's earned income.
  • My room equals $33,810. That is a cap. Room is the lesser of 18% of prior-year income and the cap, less any PA.
  • This year's salary sets this year's room. This year's income sets next year's room.
  • An HBP withdrawal is tax-free cash. If you are not a first-time buyer, it is a regular RRSP withdrawal and fully taxable; the CRA's own example rules out anyone who lived in a home they owned within the past four years.
  • My spouse's home does not affect me. The CRA says living in a home owned by your current spouse or common-law partner also disqualifies you.

FAQ

Where do I find my exact room?

Your notice of assessment or CRA online account shows the RRSP deduction limit. In the landing year there is usually no notice yet, so estimate with the formula above.

What if I am also a U.S. taxpayer?

RRSPs have separate U.S. reporting rules; see our article on reporting RRSPs and TFSAs on a U.S. return.

Does withdrawing the excess fix everything?

It stops the tax for future months, but months already in excess must still be reported on a T1-OVP and paid.
