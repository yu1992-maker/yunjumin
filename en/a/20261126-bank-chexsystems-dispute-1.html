Denied by ChexSystems: the 5-year record and the 30-day dispute clock

Nov 26, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Most checking-account denials trace to ChexSystems or EWS. Get a free report within 60 days, dispute errors on a 30-day clock, and expect accurate records to last about five years.

If a US bank refuses to open a checking account for you, the cause is usually a checking-account report from ChexSystems or Early Warning Services (EWS), not your credit score. The bank must tell you in writing which company supplied the information; you can get a free copy within 60 days of that notice, and once you dispute an error the company generally has 30 days to investigate. Accurate negative items cannot be disputed away. The OCC says they generally stay for five years; the legal ceiling is seven.

Sources: CFPB "Consumer guide to checking account denials", OCC HelpWithMyBank, the Fair Credit Reporting Act (15 U.S.C. §§1681c, 1681i, 1681j) and the Bank On National Account Standards 2025–2026. Checked 26 November 2026.

1. Two databases: why a 780 score can still be declined

Equifax, Experian and TransUnion track borrowing. ChexSystems and EWS track deposit-account behaviour. The CFPB lists typical negatives as unpaid overdrafts or fees, involuntary account closure, suspected fraud related to a checking account, and those problems on a joint account held with someone else.

The CFPB notes that an involuntary closure can stay for up to seven years depending on the company, even if the overdraft is later repaid. Repayment only changes the status to paid.

2. Scenario: five years for a $480 overdraft

In March 2022 a student leaves the US with an account that runs to $480 in overdraft and fees; the bank closes it in May and reports it. In November 2026 he returns on an H-1B and is declined at a different bank.

  1. Ask for the written adverse-action notice and identify whether ChexSystems or EWS was used.
  2. Within 60 days (by late January 2027) request the free report and check the amount, dates and reporting bank.
  3. If anything is wrong, dispute with both the reporting company and the original bank. If it is accurate, repay the original bank and get written confirmation.
  4. On the OCC's "generally five years" basis the item should age off between March and May 2027. Until then, apply for a lower-risk account.

3. Pro tips

  • Know the dispute clocks. Under FCRA §1681i the agency must finish its reinvestigation within 30 days, extendable by up to 15 days if you send more information during that period, and must send results within 5 business days of finishing. Information that cannot be verified must be deleted or corrected.
  • Dispute on both sides. The CFPB recommends disputing with the reporting company and with the bank that furnished the data; furnishers also have a duty to investigate. The CFPB publishes sample letters for each.
  • Look for Bank On certified accounts. The 2025–2026 standards require an opening deposit of $25 or less, a non-waivable monthly fee of $5 or less, and no overdraft or NSF fees. They strongly recommend denying applicants only for actual past fraud and accepting ITINs and consular IDs as primary identification, which helps newcomers without an SSN.
  • Freeze against fraudulent openings. The ChexSystems consumer portal offers a security freeze and alerts. Remember to lift the freeze temporarily before you open an account yourself.
  • Pulling your own report is harmless. It is a consumer request, not a bank's account-opening inquiry.

4. Common mistakes

  • "Paying it off deletes it." No. The status changes to paid; early removal depends on whether the bank agrees to update it, and it has no legal duty to do so.
  • "A dispute can remove an accurate record." The FCRA only requires removal of inaccurate or unverifiable information.
  • "Closing my own account gets me blacklisted." The CFPB's list covers involuntary closures, unpaid balances and fraud, not ordinary voluntary closures.
  • "One denial means every bank will say no." The CFPB says institutions' policies vary widely; try another.

FAQ

How do I contact ChexSystems and EWS?

The CFPB guide lists ChexSystems at (800) 428-9623 and EWS at (800) 325-7775. Check mailing addresses against the latest CFPB List of Consumer Reporting Companies.

What if the record comes from identity theft?

The CFPB suggests checking all three credit reports and including an FTC identity theft affidavit with your dispute.

Does a denial hurt my credit score?

Checking-account reports are a separate system, but some banks also pull a credit report at account opening. Check the bank's disclosures.
