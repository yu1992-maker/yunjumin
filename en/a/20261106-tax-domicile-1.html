Florida tax domicile: what evidence actually proves it

Nov 6, 20265 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Florida has no state income tax; the hard part is whether your former state will audit you. A checklist of domicile evidence, the role of the Declaration of Domicile, and New York's Hoff case.

Florida has no single domicile evidence package and no day-count threshold of its own. The audience you must convince is your former state, such as New York or California, which needs to believe you have genuinely left. A Declaration of Domicile is one piece of evidence; it cannot carry the case on its own.

Three concepts to keep apart

ConceptWhat it turns onWho decides
DomicileWhere you intend to live permanently, judged on all facts and intentState tax departments, especially your former state
Statutory residentA permanent place of abode plus day counts, such as New York's 183 daysYour former state
Federal tax residency (non-citizens)Green card or the Substantial Presence TestIRS

The federal test is separate. The IRS says the Substantial Presence Test requires at least 31 days in the US in the current year and at least 183 weighted days over three years: all days this year, plus one-third of last year's days, plus one-sixth of the days two years ago. The IRS example of 120 days in each of three years gives 120+40+20=180, which fails. People who meet the test may still be treated as non-residents under the closer connection exception; see the IRS page. Green card holders are generally residents under the green card test, which that page does not cover; see Publication 519.

Declaration of Domicile: useful, not sufficient

The basis is Florida Statutes §222.17:

  • You file with the Clerk of the Circuit Court in your county of residence and sign under oath before an officer authorised to administer oaths.
  • It states that you reside in that county and intend to remain permanently; if you keep a home elsewhere, the Florida home must be your predominant and principal home; and it lists your former city, county and state and any other residences.
  • The Clerk charges fees under s. 28.24, which vary by county. Broward County, for example, lists a $10 recording fee (filed after notarisation).
  • The statute expressly says it does not replace other ways of proving domicile.

A separate form exists for people domiciled elsewhere who maintain a Florida residence, such as seasonal residents, to declare that they keep their out-of-state domicile.

Sources differ: some form websites say a copy must also be filed with the Florida Department of Revenue. County clerk sites and the statute mention only the Clerk; we found no official basis for a DOR filing.

Evidence checklist

Alper Law groups the evidence into four categories:

  1. Formal records: Florida driver's licence, voter registration, Declaration of Domicile, homestead application; addresses changed to Florida with banks, brokers, card issuers, insurers, professional licensing bodies, your employer's HR, the IRS (Form 8822) and Social Security.
  2. Housing records: a Florida purchase or lease, homestead, year-round utility bills, renovation and furnishing receipts.
  3. Where you actually are: a contemporaneous daily location log (which the article calls the most persuasive evidence), backed by card receipts, tolls, itineraries and phone bills.
  4. Cutting ties with the old state: selling the former home is cleanest; if you keep it, use it less, insure it as a vacation or second home, and ideally rent it at market rates. Move valuables and family to Florida, and stop running a business remotely from the old state.

When you leave, file a final resident or part-year return stating the date domicile changed. If you simply stop filing, the old state's statute of limitations may never start to run, as WOH notes.

A real case: appearances are not enough

Matter of Hoff (New York Division of Tax Appeals, DTA No. 850209, 9 October 2025)

A couple bought a condominium in Naples, Florida in 2014 but kept their New York home, and claimed a Florida domicile from October 2018. New York assessed $59,648 in tax and interest for 2018 and 2019. The tribunal accepted that they intended to move to Florida but found they had not shown the move was complete during the audit period.

2018186131

Other adverse facts:

  • Both New York country club memberships were kept; only one Florida club was joined.
  • Only one of four cars was registered in Florida during the audit period.
  • New York bank and brokerage accounts were kept and no Florida bank account was opened.
  • The wife's income came solely from a New York business.
  • Three of four major holidays were spent in New York.
  • Many Florida formalities came before or after the audit period; a hunting and fishing licence, for example, only in 2020.

The author stresses that day counts are only one factor and not necessarily decisive; here the days combined with everything else.

How former states audit

  • New York: the burden is on the taxpayer, to a clear and convincing standard. Auditors start with five primary factors: home, active business involvement, time, family location and “near and dear” items, then look at other factors if needed. New York also has statutory residency: keep a permanent place of abode and spend more than 183 days in the state and you are a resident regardless of domicile.
  • California: sources differ. Some cite a presumption of non-residence for those domiciled elsewhere who spend under 183 days, some cite nine months, and others the 546-day safe harbour in R&TC §17016. All agree the facts and circumstances dominate. Check the FTB's own guidance.
  • Auditors typically request returns, residence and employment history, leases or mortgages, calendars and logs, insurance, driver's licence, voter and vehicle registrations, card statements, tolls and phone records. New York response windows are often about three weeks; audits can exceed a year.
  • Risk peaks in the first two or three years after a move; keep records until you have at least three complete tax years of Florida domicile.

Steps

  1. Start the file and a daily location log before you move.
  2. Rent or buy a Florida home; apply for homestead if you buy.
  3. File a Declaration of Domicile with the Clerk.
  4. Switch driver's licence, voter and vehicle registration; update every account and government record.
  5. Move your doctor, lawyer, CPA, clubs and place of worship to Florida.
  6. Deal with the old home and file the final-year return as required.
  7. Review day counts every year and stay under the old state's statutory threshold.

FAQ

Is a Declaration of Domicile enough?

No. The statute itself says it does not replace other evidence, and the Hoff couple lost despite similar formalities.

How many days must I spend in Florida?

We found no Florida day-count threshold in official sources. Day counts come from your former state, such as New York's 183 days, and federally from the IRS Substantial Presence Test.

Does a green card holder need the Substantial Presence Test?

Green card holders are generally federal tax residents under the green card test; the IRS Substantial Presence Test page does not address this, so rely on Publication 519. State domicile is a separate question.

Reminder: official sources govern, and state rules and case law change; consult a CPA or lawyer for complex situations.
