UK Marriage Allowance: £252 a Year, Backdatable Four Years

Nov 23, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

A partner earning under £12,570 can transfer £1,260 of allowance to a basic-rate spouse, backdated to 2022/23, worth up to £1,260 in total.

Marriage Allowance lets the partner earning below the Personal Allowance (usually £12,570) transfer £1,260 of it to a basic-rate taxpaying spouse or civil partner, cutting the couple's tax by up to £252 a year. Claims can be backdated to April 6, 2022; with 2026/27 included, that is up to five tax years and £1,260 in total. The transfer renews automatically each year and must be cancelled if circumstances change.

Sources: GOV.UK's Marriage Allowance guide (How it works, How to apply, If your circumstances change), Income Tax rates and Personal Allowances, and the Personal Allowance page of Tax on your UK income if you live abroad. Retrieved November 23, 2026. Rates are for England, Wales and Northern Ireland; Scotland is noted separately.

1. Eligibility: all three must hold

ConditionGOV.UK rule
RelationshipMarried or in a civil partnership; cohabiting couples cannot claim
Transferring (lower) earnerPays no Income Tax, or income below the Personal Allowance (usually £12,570)
Recipient (England, Wales, NI)Basic-rate taxpayer, usually income £12,571–£50,270 before the allowance
Recipient (Scotland)Starter, basic or intermediate rate, usually £12,571–£43,662
Living abroadNo bar, as long as you get a Personal Allowance
Born before April 6, 1935Married Couple's Allowance may be better; you cannot have both

For 2026/27 (April 6, 2026 to April 5, 2027), the 20% basic rate applies to £12,571–£50,270 and 40% from £50,271. A recipient in the 40% band is ineligible.

2. Worked examples: the gain depends on how far the transferor is below £12,570

Scenario A: One partner earns £6,000 part-time, the other £35,000. After transfer, the lower earner's allowance is £11,310, still above income, so no tax; the other's becomes £13,830, saving £1,260 x 20% = £252.

Scenario B (GOV.UK's example): Incomes of £11,500 and £20,000. The transferor now pays tax on £190, while the recipient's taxable income falls from £7,430 to £6,170. Combined taxable income drops from £7,430 to £6,360, a net saving of £214.

Scenario C: The transferor earns £12,300. After transfer they pay tax on £990, i.e. £198, against the recipient's £252 saving: a net £54. The closer to £12,570, the thinner the benefit. If the lower earner also has savings or dividend income, GOV.UK suggests calling the Income Tax helpline to work out who should claim.

GOV.UK currently allows backdating to April 6, 2022, i.e. the four full tax years before the current one. On that pattern, 2022/23 should drop out once the current tax year ends on April 5, 2027; check GOV.UK at the time. Backdated years use each year's Personal Allowance, which was £12,570 throughout.

4. Pro tips

  1. British couples living in the US can qualify. GOV.UK says British citizens, EEA citizens, and those covered by a relevant tax treaty keep a Personal Allowance while living abroad; non-residents claim it on form R43 after each tax year with UK income. Households where one partner has UK rental income and the other little UK income should check.
  2. Mind the filing order in Self Assessment. The transferor completes the Marriage Allowance section and the recipient leaves it blank; the transferor should file at least 3 days before the recipient.
  3. Check your tax codes. Once granted, the recipient's code ends in M and the transferor's in N. A code change can take up to 2 months.
  4. Bereavement. If a partner died after April 5, 2022, you can still claim by phone; the estate is treated as having the increased allowance.

5. Common mistakes

  • "One claim covers one year." The reverse: it renews until cancelled. Cancel if the transferor's income rises above £12,570 or the recipient moves into the 40% band. A cancellation for an income change runs to the end of the tax year (April 5).
  • "We can just switch it off after divorce." If the relationship ends, the change may be backdated to April 6, possibly leaving tax underpaid for that year.
  • "The higher earner can use it." No. The recipient must be a basic-rate taxpayer (starter, basic or intermediate in Scotland).
  • "The transferor never pays tax." Scenarios B and C show they may; the test is whether the couple pays less overall.
  • "Living together is enough." Unmarried cohabitants are not eligible.

FAQ

Who applies?

If both partners have only wages, the lower earner applies, online or through Self Assessment; backdated claims follow the route on GOV.UK's How to apply page.

How is backdated relief paid?

The recipient's tax for each backdated year is recalculated with the higher allowance; the current year is reflected through the tax code or Self Assessment. The method of refund follows HMRC's processing.

Do Scottish taxpayers save less?

The transfer is the same £1,260; the saving depends on which Scottish band that £1,260 falls in. GOV.UK provides a calculator.

Note: Verify on GOV.UK and HMRC. For non-residency, cross-border income or divorce, consult a UK chartered tax adviser or solicitor.
