Capital One Venture X: is the $395 annual fee worth it after credits?

Nov 7, 20263 min readYunjumin Forum editors

What Venture X really costs after the $300 travel credit and 10,000 anniversary miles, plus the lounge rule changes from February 2026.

Capital One Venture X carries a $395 annual fee. If you use the full $300 travel credit each year (bookings through Capital One Travel only) and count the 10,000 anniversary miles from year two (valued by Capital One at $100), the fee roughly nets to zero, which is why it is often seen as strong value among entry premium travel cards. If you do not book travel through Capital One's portal, or rely on it to bring family into lounges, the 2026 rules make it less compelling.

Net cost

  1. $395 fee minus $300 credit = $95 (as calculated by Thrifty Traveler and TPG).
  2. Subtract anniversary miles at Capital One's 1 cent per mile, $100: net about -$5, essentially breakeven.
  3. Transferring miles to airline partners can be worth more. Estimates differ: TPG values them at $185; an OMAAT writer at about 1.7 cents, roughly $170. These are personal valuations, not guarantees.
  4. The Global Entry/TSA credit (up to $120, not every year) is extra.
Note: the travel credit applies per cardmember year, including the first; anniversary miles start only at the first anniversary. OMAAT does not specify reset dates; check your account.

Everyday earning

  • Hotels and rental cars via Capital One Travel: 10X
  • Flights and vacation rentals via Capital One Travel: 5X
  • Capital One Entertainment tickets: 5X
  • Everything else: 2X

The official site also lists up to $100 experience credit on Premier Collection and up to $50 on Lifestyle Collection hotel bookings via Capital One Travel, per booking rather than per year.

Lounge rules from February 1, 2026

According to View from the Wing, from February 1, 2026:

  • Authorized users pay $125 each for lounge access; the authorized user card itself remains free if you skip lounges.
  • Guests at Capital One lounges are no longer free: $45 per adult, $25 under 18, free under 2.
  • Spending $75,000 in a calendar year restores free guests: 2 at Capital One Lounges, 1 at Landings, for that year and the next.
  • Priority Pass: complimentary guests are eliminated on the personal Venture X, even at $75,000, and restaurant credits no longer apply.

A $35 Priority Pass guest fee mentioned elsewhere refers to Venture X Business; check official terms for the personal card. TPG currently lists five Capital One Lounges (DFW, DEN, IAD, LAS, JFK) and two Landings (LGA, DCA); CLT is planned with no firm date.

Who it suits

  • Good fit: at least $300 a year of flights, hotels or cars you are willing to book via Capital One Travel; you want a simple card with non-expiring miles; you travel abroad often.
  • Poor fit: rare travelers; people who book direct with airlines or hotels; families expecting free lounge access (now $125 per authorized user).

Checklist before applying

  1. Compare Capital One Travel prices on your usual routes and hotels against Google Flights and hotel sites.
  2. Confirm you can use the $300 within the cardmember year, with margin.
  3. Price lounge access for family at $125 per person.
  4. Decide whether to redeem at 1 cent for travel or transfer to partners.
  5. Pay for Global Entry with this card if you need it.

FAQ

Can the $300 credit be used for bookings elsewhere?

No. NerdWallet and TPG both stress that only Capital One Travel bookings qualify, which is its main limitation.

Are the 10,000 anniversary miles given in year one?

No. Capital One says they start at the first anniversary; OMAAT says they post within one or two statements after the fee is charged. The travel credit is usable from the first year.

Is there a fee for authorized users?

The official site does not state an amount; View from the Wing and TPG say the card is free and only lounge access costs $125 per person.

Note: terms change. Verify on the official site and your cardholder agreement; for tax or complex financial situations, consult a CPA or attorney.
