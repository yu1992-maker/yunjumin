The 0% capital gains bracket: harvesting gains below $49,450 of taxable income in 2026

Nov 26, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Sell and rebuy within the 0% bracket to raise cost basis tax-free: 2026 thresholds, two worked examples, using 401(k) deferrals to widen the room, and where the wash-sale rule stops.

For tax year 2026, long-term capital gains and qualified dividends are taxed at 0% to the extent they fall within taxable income of $49,450 for single filers and $98,900 for married couples filing jointly. Selling appreciated positions held for more than a year below that line, and buying them straight back, resets cost basis at no federal income tax cost. This is tax-gain harvesting. The wash-sale rule restricts losses, not gains, so there is no 30-day waiting period.

Sources: the IRS announcement of 2026 inflation adjustments and Revenue Procedure 2025-32, IRS Topic No. 409, Publications 550 and 519, and the IRS Net Investment Income Tax page, as consulted on November 26, 2026.

1. The threshold is taxable income, not gross income

The 2026 long-term capital gains brackets under Rev. Proc. 2025-32 are below. The test is taxable income after the standard or itemized deduction, and the gains themselves count toward it.

The 2026 standard deduction is $16,100 single, $32,200 joint and $24,150 head of household. A single wage earner taking the standard deduction therefore keeps some 0% room up to roughly $65,550 of gross income; a joint couple up to roughly $131,100.

Ordinary income fills the brackets first and long-term gains stack on top. Only the slice of gains that lands below the 0% ceiling is tax-free. Gains never push wages into a higher ordinary rate, but they do consume the 0% room.

2. Worked examples

Scenario A: single, $60,000 in W-2 wages

  • Taxable ordinary income: $60,000 − $16,100 = $43,900.
  • Remaining 0% room: $49,450 − $43,900 = $5,550.
  • Realising $10,000 of long-term gains: $5,550 at 0%, $4,450 at 15%, federal tax $667.50.
  • Adding $5,000 of pre-tax traditional 401(k) deferrals from the final December paycheck cuts taxable ordinary income to $38,900 and widens the room to $10,550, so the whole $10,000 is taxed at 0%. The $5,000 would otherwise have been taxed in the 12% bracket ($12,400–$50,400 for single filers in 2026), saving another $600. Total saving: $1,267.50.

Scenario B: married filing jointly, $110,000 in wages

  • Taxable ordinary income: $110,000 − $32,200 = $77,800.
  • 0% room: $98,900 − $77,800 = $21,100. An index fund bought for $30,000 and now worth $51,100 can be sold and repurchased, lifting basis to $51,100 with no federal tax.
  • A later sale at $60,000 then produces a taxable gain of $8,900 instead of $30,000, a future saving of $3,165 at 15%.

3. Pro tips

  1. Repurchase immediately. Under IRS Publication 550 the wash-sale rule disallows losses; it does not apply to gains. Buying back the same fund keeps your allocation intact while resetting basis; the new holding period starts the day after purchase.
  2. Estimate the full year first. December mutual fund distributions, year-end bonuses and interest all eat into the room. Trade after funds publish distribution estimates in mid-December and leave a $1,000–$2,000 cushion.
  3. Use specific lot identification. To get the most basis step-up per dollar of room, sell the lots with the largest embedded gains.
  4. Trade date governs the year. Per Publication 550, cash-method taxpayers recognise gains on the trade date, so a sale executed on December 31 that settles in January belongs to 2026.
  5. Low-income years are the window. The year you move from student to employee, take parental leave, lose a job mid-year, or retire before Social Security and RMDs begin.

4. Immigrants and new residents

  • Nonresident aliens: According to Publication 519, a nonresident alien present in the US for fewer than 183 days in the year generally owes no US tax on non-business capital gains; one present 183 days or more is taxed at 30% (or a treaty rate) on net US-source gains, and the 0%/15%/20% brackets do not apply.
  • Resident aliens, including green card holders, are taxed on worldwide income, so gains on foreign shares and funds count, and can use the 0% bracket.
  • No step-up on arrival. Unlike Canada, which resets cost basis to market value on immigration, the US generally uses original cost for assets bought before you became resident. Positions that rose sharply before the move can be realised gradually within the 0% band in low-income years. Seek a cross-border CPA for complex cases.

5. Common mistakes

  1. "My income must be under $49,450." The limit applies to taxable income; a single filer with the standard deduction still has room at about $65,550 of gross income.
  2. "Short-term gains qualify too." Per IRS Topic No. 409, only gains on assets held more than one year get the preferential rates.
  3. "A loss carryforward makes harvesting better." Carried-over capital losses must offset current gains first; you cannot elect to save them. If the gains would have been taxed at 0% anyway, the carryforward is wasted. Use it against 15%-bracket gains or the $3,000 ordinary-income offset instead.
  4. "0% federal means tax-free." State income tax is separate and varies by state, and higher AGI can affect income-tested benefits. Higher earners should also watch the 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax, whose statutory thresholds the IRS lists as $200,000 single and $250,000 joint.

FAQ

Is selling and immediately rebuying an abusive transaction?

No. The wash-sale rule only bars deducting a loss when substantially identical securities are bought within 30 days. A gain is simply reported on Form 8949 and Schedule D at 0%.

Do qualified dividends use up the 0% room?

Yes. Qualified dividends and long-term gains share the same brackets.

Does this work inside a 401(k) or IRA?

No. Trades inside tax-deferred accounts do not create current taxable gains; harvesting is only relevant in taxable brokerage accounts.
