Wrong Address on a Canadian Credit File: Equifax and TransUnion Documents

Nov 8, 20263 min readYunjumin Forum editors

How to correct an address with Equifax Canada and TransUnion Canada: documents, timelines and next steps if refused.

A wrong address on a Canadian credit file can be corrected directly and free of charge with Equifax Canada and TransUnion Canada. They hold separate files, so each must be fixed separately. Equifax's site lists two valid government-issued documents (both sides) plus one proof of address dated within 90 days. TransUnion accepts requests online, by phone or by mail.

Why the address is wrong in the first place

Most addresses are reported by creditors such as banks, card issuers and phone companies, not entered by you. Correcting the bureau alone is not enough: update your address with every bank and card issuer as well, or the next report may restore the old one. Credit Karma also notes that a bureau usually checks with the creditor before changing a record.

Step 1: Pull both reports

  1. Request the free report from each bureau (one free a year from each). The TransUnion report can also be viewed free on Credit Karma.
  2. Identify the wrong address and note the Equifax reference number if there is one; Equifax says it helps.
  3. The two files may differ, so do not check only one.

Step 2: Prepare documents

Equifax Canada (official requirements)

ItemRequirement
IdentificationTwo valid government-issued documents, both sides, showing legal name, date of birth and/or current address (driver's licence, passport, birth certificate)
Proof of addressOne document dated within 90 days with name and current address (utility bill, financial statement)
AdditionalIf your current address does not appear on the report, further documents are needed. That sentence was cut off on the page we retrieved; follow the form

TransUnion Canada

We could not open TransUnion's page (403), so the following is third-party. People's Law School says a written request needs two pieces of identification and that the letter and identification together must show name, current address, date of birth and signature. Borrowell says official proof of the new address may be required. TransUnion's own form governs.

If you have lived at your current address for less than two years, include the previous address (required in TransUnion's online request and advised by People's Law School).

Step 3: Submit

Equifax

  1. Open the Equifax Dispute page and choose Personal Information, which Borrowell says covers name, address, date of birth, SIN and phone.
  2. Submit online (up to three files; several pages may be merged into one PDF), or print and mail the form.
  3. Mailing address (official): Equifax Canada Co., National Consumer Relations, Box 190, Montreal, Quebec H1S 2Z2.

TransUnion

  1. Use its credit report disputes page, or download and mail the form.
  2. Phone: Borrowell lists 1-800-663-9980 (English) and 1-877-713-3393 (French). These are third-party listings; check the official site first.

How long it takes

Sources differ:

  • Equifax: about 15 to 20 days online, 20 to 25 by mail.
  • Borrowell: Equifax takes about 10 to 20 business days.
  • TransUnion: up to 30 days according to Borrowell; People's Law School, writing for BC, says bureaus generally have 30 days to respond.

Equifax will contact you if it needs more, and sends results by email (online) or letter (mail). Keep copies.

If the correction is refused

  1. Ask the creditor that reported the wrong address to correct it first.
  2. If it will not, you may add a consumer statement of up to 100 words (People's Law School, for BC), sent with two pieces of identification to the bureau's consumer relations department.
  3. In BC, People's Law School says you may ask the privacy commissioner in writing for a review within 30 business days of refusal. Other provinces differ.
If the wrong address comes with unfamiliar accounts or inquiries, it may be identity theft. Contact both bureaus at once and ask about a fraud alert.

FAQ

Can I correct only one bureau?

No. Each keeps its own file.

I have just arrived and have no bill from the last 90 days. What can I do?

Equifax asks for a document dated within 90 days showing name and current address, such as a utility bill or financial statement. Have a bank or carrier issue one to the new address first. For unusual cases, such as a file that lacks your current address, ask Equifax.

Does changing an address affect the score?

The sources we checked do not say, so we make no claim.
