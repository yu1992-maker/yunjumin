Do the Self-Employed Owe Quarterly Tax? Form 1040-ES, Deadlines and Penalties

Nov 11, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

If you expect to owe $1,000 or more at filing, quarterly estimated tax is generally due. How 1040-ES works, the 2026 dates, safe harbors and penalties.

Yes. Sole proprietors, partners, ride-share and delivery drivers and freelancers who expect to owe $1,000 or more when they file generally must pay estimated tax each quarter, calculated and paid with Form 1040-ES. Underpayment triggers a penalty computed like interest on the amount and the days outstanding; the IRS underpayment rate for the fourth quarter of 2026 is 7% a year, compounded daily.

As of November 11, 2026, the last (fourth) installment for tax year 2026 is due January 15, 2027, so there is still time to catch up.

Who must pay estimated tax

  • Per the IRS, individuals (including sole proprietors, partners and S corporation shareholders) generally must pay if they expect to owe $1,000 or more when the return is filed.
  • Estimated tax covers income tax, self-employment tax and alternative minimum tax.
  • You do not have to pay if you had no tax liability for the prior year, were a U.S. citizen or resident for the whole year, and the prior tax year covered 12 months.
  • If you also have wages, you can ask your employer to withhold more on Form W-4 instead.

What self-employment tax is

According to the IRS, the self-employment tax rate is 15.3%: 12.4% for Social Security and 2.9% for Medicare. In 2026 the Social Security portion applies only to the first $184,500 of combined wages and net self-employment earnings; above that, only the 2.9% Medicare tax remains. Net self-employment earnings of $400 or more require SE tax and Schedule SE. The employer-equivalent portion of SE tax is deductible in figuring AGI.

Note: the IRS page does not mention the common net earnings × 92.35% calculation; see the Schedule SE instructions.

The periods are not equal quarters. A due date falling on a weekend or legal holiday moves to the next business day; for mail, the U.S. postmark date counts. These dates come from Form 1040-ES and several secondary sources; the IRS estimated-tax page itself does not list them, so rely on the form.

How to work through 1040-ES

  1. Estimate this year's income and deductions to arrive at projected AGI and taxable income.
  2. Compute the projected income tax, then add self-employment tax.
  3. Subtract expected credits and any withholding (for example from a W-2 job).
  4. The result is the year's expected tax, usually split into four equal payments.
  5. Use the worksheet in Form 1040-ES; last year's return is a useful starting point.

If income is uneven (for instance, concentrated late in the year), the annualized installment method can reduce or eliminate the penalty; it is claimed on Schedule AI of Form 2210.

How to pay

  • Mail the payment voucher included with Form 1040-ES.
  • Pay online, by phone or through the IRS2Go app.
  • Pay through your IRS online account at IRS.gov/account.

Safe harbors

Per the IRS, you generally avoid the underpayment penalty if either applies:

  • You owe less than $1,000 after withholding and refundable credits; or
  • Withholding plus estimated payments equal at least 90% of this year's tax or 100% of the tax on last year's return, whichever is smaller.

High earners: the IRS page says only that special rules apply to certain higher-income taxpayers, without figures. Several secondary sources (not the IRS page) state that if prior-year AGI exceeded $150,000 ($75,000 married filing separately), the prior-year threshold rises to 110%. We could not confirm this on the IRS page; rely on the 1040-ES instructions.

What underpaying costs

  • The penalty is figured as amount underpaid × days late × rate, separately for each installment, so missing the first installment and catching up at year-end still produces a charge.
  • The IRS underpayment rate for Q4 2026 (October 1–December 31) is 7% a year, compounded daily. We did not find the rates for earlier quarters; see the Form 2210 instructions.
  • Compute the penalty on Form 2210, or let the IRS calculate it and bill you.

Waivers may apply for casualty, disaster or other unusual circumstances, or if you retired after reaching 62 or became disabled in the current or prior year and the underpayment was due to reasonable cause rather than willful neglect.

What to do now (November)

  1. Total what you have paid this year and compare it with the safe harbors.
  2. If short, make the fourth payment by January 15, 2027, ideally in full, to limit the penalty.
  3. If you have W-2 wages, ask your employer to withhold more now. Some say withholding is treated as paid evenly through the year even if taken late; we did not confirm this on the IRS page, so check with a CPA.

FAQ

I started freelancing this year. Must I pay in year one?

It depends on whether you expect to owe $1,000. If last year's tax was zero and the conditions are met, you need not; otherwise apply the 90%-of-current-year test.

I skipped the first two installments. Can I catch up with the last one?

Yes, the payment will be accepted, but the penalty for the earlier installments still accrues daily; the sooner you pay, the smaller it is.

Can the penalty be waived?

In specific cases, such as disaster, casualty, retirement after 62 or disability. Explain the reason on Form 2210.

Verify on the IRS site. For complex situations (multi-state income, nonresident or newly arrived status), consult a CPA or attorney.
