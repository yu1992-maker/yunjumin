Amex First-Year Fee Waiver Didn't Apply? What to Check When You Apply

Nov 6, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

When an Amex first-year fee waiver fails, the cause is usually the link, the product or what the page displayed. A pre- and post-application checklist with remedies.

When an Amex “first year free” offer does not take effect, the cause usually falls into one of three groups: the link or page you used never stated a waiver; the card's fee is permanent rather than waived for year one; or what the page displayed did not match the actual terms. Before applying, find wording such as “$0 intro annual fee” on the final confirmation page and in the terms. If you cannot find it, assume it does not exist. If you have already been charged, ask for a refund by secure message or phone.

A caveat: Amex has no official page explaining why a fee waiver failed. What follows draws on reporting by Doctor of Credit, NerdWallet and AwardWallet, plus individual cases on the myFICO forums. Some cases are old; treat them as context and rely on the page and terms shown when you apply.

First, distinguish “waived for year one” from “no annual fee”

This is the most common confusion. According to NerdWallet and similar reviews, the Blue Cash Everyday card carries an ongoing $0 annual fee, while the “$0 first year, then $95” structure belongs to its sibling, Blue Cash Preferred. Mixing the two up makes it look as though a promotion failed.

The same card may carry different terms at different times and through different links, so always read the page in front of you.

Before applying: five checks

  1. Apply through the exact link that showed the waiver. One reader could not find the waiver on the Amex site and ended up with a different offer: a $250 bonus and no waiver.
  2. Screenshot the final confirmation page, including the fee, the first-year terms and the welcome bonus. You will need it if you dispute a charge.
  3. If the waiver is missing, reload or try another browser. In one case the “$0 intro annual fee for the first year, then $95” line appeared only after switching browsers. That is anecdotal; it shows the display can be inconsistent, not that the offer is guaranteed.
  4. Watch for the eligibility pop-up. AwardWallet explains that Amex checks welcome-bonus eligibility during the online application. If you are ineligible, a notice may appear and you can stop without affecting your credit; no notice usually means you qualify. The notice is not guaranteed to appear and does not give reasons.
  5. Be sure you can live with the fee. One blogger's advice: decide in advance whether you would keep the card if the waiver did not apply.

Common reasons the waiver “didn't work”

  • The link or offer never included it: public links, CardMatch and targeted offers can carry different terms.
  • The wrong product: see the table above.
  • Upgrades and product changes: a myFICO thread asked whether an upgrade also earns a first-year waiver; no one confirmed it. Do not assume it applies.
  • Display errors: a December 2016 Doctor of Credit post noted that some people who applied for Blue Cash Preferred in late August 2016 had the $95 first-year fee waived; the author suspected the site was not displaying the fee correctly (the fee rose from $75 to $95 on 3 August 2016). Display and execution can diverge, in your favour or not.
  • No clear pattern: one blogger says there is no consistent explanation for why some applicants are charged and others are not.

After approval: how to confirm whether you'll be charged

  1. Log in and read the online cardmember agreement to see whether a first-year fee applies, as one blogger recommends.
  2. Compare it with your application screenshots.
  3. Check the first statement for an annual-fee line.

If you've already been charged

  1. Send a Secure Message with your screenshots, application date and link source.
  2. Or call and explain that the application page showed a first-year waiver.
  3. Keep records and follow up if needed.

Two forum cases: one cardholder charged $95 received a refund after a secure-message complaint; in another, Amex acknowledged a billing error, said the first year should be free and reversed the fee. These are individual cases, not guarantees.

How this relates to the welcome bonus

The fee waiver and the welcome bonus are separate. Several outlets note that Amex bonuses are generally once per lifetime, with records said to last about seven years (a media estimate, not an official figure). Terms often state that you may be ineligible if Amex determines you currently hold an Amex card account. Wording varies by offer: some exclude anyone who has or has had the product, others do not. CardMatch does not trigger a hard inquiry, but outlets report it often shows cards you already hold, and there is no reliable explanation for why a given offer does not appear.

FAQ

1. The page showed a waiver. Why was I charged?

Possible causes are a display error, the wrong product, a different link, or no identifiable reason. Send a secure message with screenshots and ask Amex to verify and refund.

2. If no eligibility notice appeared, am I guaranteed the bonus?

No. AwardWallet says the notice “should” appear but is not guaranteed, and you still have to meet the spending requirement.

3. Can an upgrade or product change earn a first-year waiver?

No reliable source confirms it. Ask before you act and keep a record of the answer.
