Can You Get a CIBC Credit Card With No Canadian Credit History? A Newcomer's Start

Nov 10, 20263 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Newcomers without Canadian credit history can apply for a CIBC credit card through its newcomer offer. Eligibility, documents, steps and how to build credit.

Yes. CIBC has a newcomer offer, and according to third-party reports, newcomers do not need Canadian credit history or a deposit to apply for a credit card. You typically need permanent resident, work permit or study permit documents and open a newcomer bank account at the same time. Limits and terms should be confirmed on CIBC's own pages; sources disagree on details.

Who qualifies

According to Savvy New Canadians (third party, not CIBC), the broad criteria are:

  • Permanent residents (landed immigrants) within the past 5 years;
  • Foreign workers with a work permit valid for 12 months or more;
  • International students, who have dedicated student cards and accounts.

Another source says visitors generally cannot get an unsecured card from a big bank and usually need a secured card with a deposit.

What to bring

  • Newcomers and foreign workers: PR card or landing papers, work permit (IMM 1442), plus government-issued photo ID such as a passport or driver's licence;
  • International students: valid student ID, study permit, foreign passport or Canadian driver's licence.

You can book an appointment on CIBC's website and complete it at a branch.

Another source mentions newcomers can get the CIBC Dividend Visa or CIBC Adapta Mastercard. Sources also name the program differently, as Newcomer to Canada or Welcome to Canada Plan. Check CIBC's current page before applying.

Newcomer account and sign-up bonus

According to Savvy New Canadians:

  • The Smart Account for Newcomers costs up to $16.95 a month, waived for the first 12 months (another source says two years; accounts differ);
  • Complete and maintain any two of the following within 2 months of opening for a bonus of up to $350: one recurring payroll direct deposit, two recurring pre-authorized debits, or two online bill payments of at least $50 each;
  • The bonus is paid within 7 months of meeting the conditions;
  • Students can open a Smart for Students account with no monthly fee, reportedly before arriving in Canada.

Bonus terms change often; confirm with CIBC in person.

Getting started

  1. Open the account first: bring your documents, book a branch visit and open the newcomer account;
  2. Apply for one basic card: choose a no-fee or fee-waived card and avoid applying for several at once, since multiple inquiries in a short span can hurt your score;
  3. Put routine spending on the card: charge part of your everyday expenses and pay in full after the statement date;
  4. Set up autopay: at least the minimum, ideally the full balance;
  5. Confirm the card reports to the bureaus: most sources say it should report to both Equifax Canada and TransUnion Canada;
  6. Consider a second card or a higher limit after 12 to 24 months.

How fast does a score build

Money.ca cites Equifax Canada saying newcomers' scores rise by more than 10 points on average in their first two years; we could not locate Equifax's original report. One blog says 12 to 24 months of on-time payments can reach 700+; that is a single-source claim, not a guarantee.

Equifax reportedly launched a Global Consumer Credit File in 2024 to help lenders extend credit to newcomers. Whether it applies to you, and which lenders use it, should be confirmed with Equifax or your bank.

FAQ

Can I be approved without proof of income?

According to Savvy New Canadians, newcomers need not provide income or credit history. The bank decides; it may decline or offer a low limit.

Can I apply on a visitor visa?

One source says big banks do not issue unsecured cards to visitors. A secured card is an option for building a record.

What limit will I get?

Sources cite a $10,000 maximum and a $500 to $7,500 range; accounts differ. The bank sets your limit; newcomers usually start low and request increases after a good track record.
