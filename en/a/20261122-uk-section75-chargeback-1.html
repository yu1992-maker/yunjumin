UK Section 75 vs Chargeback: the £100 Threshold, Full-Price Cover and the 120-Day Clock

Nov 22, 20265 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Pay even a deposit by credit card on an item priced £100–£30,000 and Section 75 covers the full price; debit cards get only a chargeback, typically within about 120 days.

In the UK, when a single item or service with a cash price over £100 and up to £30,000 is paid for by credit card, section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act 1974 makes the card provider jointly and severally liable with the merchant for breach of contract and misrepresentation, and paying only part of the price on the card is enough to cover the whole amount. Debit cards, charge cards and payments routed through intermediaries such as PayPal do not carry this statutory right. They rely on the card networks' chargeback scheme, which typically runs for about 120 days and is not a legal obligation.

Sources: the current text of sections 75 and 75A of the Consumer Credit Act 1974 on legislation.gov.uk (up to date to 20 November 2026), section 22 of the Consumer Rights Act 2015, the Financial Ombudsman Service (FOS) guide to Section 75 and chargeback, and Visa UK's purchase-disputes page, checked on 22 November 2026. Which? is cited as a secondary source.

They are not mutually exclusive. The FOS suggests asking your provider to consider both chargeback and Section 75 and let it decide the route.

2. Four details in the statute

  • Cash price, not the amount charged. s.75(3)(b) refers to the cash price the supplier attached to a single item. The FOS confirms Section 75 applies even if only part of the payment was made on credit.
  • Per item, not per order. Five items at £40 each total £200, but none exceeds £100, so s.75 does not apply.
  • Exceeding your limit does not matter. s.75(4) says the section applies even if you exceeded the credit limit or breached another term of the agreement.
  • Charge cards are excluded. s.75(3)(c) excludes running-account credit that must be repaid in full each period, the legal basis for the FOS statement that charge cards are not covered.

Above £30,000: section 75A

For a "linked credit agreement" that finances specific goods or services, where the cash price exceeds £30,000 and the credit does not exceed £60,260, s.75A lets you pursue the lender if the supplier cannot be traced, does not respond, is insolvent, or you have taken reasonable steps (not necessarily litigation) without obtaining satisfaction. It does not apply to agreements secured on land or credit taken mainly for business.

5. Pro tips

  • Watch the chargeback window while a Section 75 claim runs. A Section 75 review can take weeks while the roughly 120-day chargeback window closes. Ask in writing for chargeback to be considered at the same time; the FOS looks at whether a chargeback should have been raised.
  • Avoid intermediaries on big purchases. The FOS notes that Section 75 needs a debtor-creditor-supplier relationship and that how you paid and who was involved can affect it. Third-party payment platforms or agents can break the chain; paying the actual supplier directly by credit card is safer.
  • Argue from the Consumer Rights Act. The FOS says providers should consider the CRA 2015: goods must be of satisfactory quality and services performed with reasonable care and skill. Citing those standards is more persuasive than saying you are unhappy.
  • Assemble evidence once. The FOS lists receipts, contracts and terms, marketing material or screenshots, correspondence, photos or video and, where needed, independent reports.

6. Common misconceptions

  • "The card payment must exceed £100." The test is the cash price of a single item, not the amount on the card.
  • "Debit cards have Section 75 too." The FOS is explicit: debit cards, charge cards, overdrafts, general-purpose bank loans, cash, credit card cheques and bank transfers are not covered.
  • "Banks must raise a chargeback." The FOS says a bank does not have to, though doing so where valid reasons exist can be good practice.
  • "Section 75 refunds only the card portion." Joint liability attaches to the claim for breach or misrepresentation, not the amount charged.
  • "The 120 days run from the purchase date." The FOS refers to the expected delivery date or receipt of defective goods; Which? says typically 120 days from processing or expected delivery. Network rules vary.

FAQ

Is there a deadline for a Section 75 claim?

Section 75 sets none, but evidence gets harder to assemble over time. General limitation rules for contract claims are complex; take legal advice if needed.

Does it cover overseas websites paid with a UK credit card?

The section does not exclude foreign suppliers, but establishing the three-party relationship can be harder. The provider's assessment and FOS case decisions govern.

What about buy-now-pay-later such as Klarna?

It depends on whether the product is a regulated consumer credit agreement, and the regulatory regime is changing. We draw no conclusion; check each provider's terms.

Reminder: this is general information, not legal advice. Network rules and provider processes change; verify with official sources and the FOS, and consult a lawyer for large sums or litigation.
