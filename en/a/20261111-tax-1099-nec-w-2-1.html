Got a 1099-NEC but No W-2? Filing Freelance Income Step by Step

Nov 11, 20263 min readYunjumin Forum editors

A 1099-NEC means self-employment income, reported on Schedule C and Schedule SE. The 15.3% SE tax, estimated payments, deductions and common questions.

A 1099-NEC means the payer treated you as an independent contractor, not an employee. Report the income and expenses on Schedule C attached to Form 1040, and compute self-employment tax on Schedule SE. With no W-2 and no withholding, you calculate and pay the tax yourself.

1099-NEC versus W-2

ItemW-2 (employee)1099-NEC (self-employed)
WithholdingEmployer withholdsUsually none; you pay
Social Security and MedicareSplit between employer and employeeYou pay both halves (SE tax)
FormsStraight onto Form 1040Schedule C + Schedule SE
Expense deductionsEssentially noneBusiness expenses reduce income

The IRS describes Form 1099-NEC simply as the form for reporting nonemployee compensation.

Filing steps

  1. Add up all income. Total every 1099-NEC, and include income for which no form arrived. The IRS pages we reviewed do not say whether income without a form is exempt, but self-employment income is reported on what you actually received, so keep bank records and invoices.
  2. Complete Schedule C (Form 1040). The IRS says it reports profit or loss from a sole proprietorship or gig work. Income minus business expenses gives net profit.
  3. Complete Schedule SE. Use the Schedule C result to compute Social Security and Medicare tax.
  4. Deduct half of SE tax. The IRS allows the employer-equivalent portion to be deducted in figuring AGI. This reduces income tax, not the SE tax itself.
  5. Check the QBI deduction. Sole proprietors may qualify for up to 20% of qualified business income on Form 8995 or 8995-A.
  6. Plan estimated tax for future quarters. See below.

How SE tax works

  • The rate is 15.3%: 12.4% Social Security and 2.9% Medicare (IRS).
  • For 2026 the Social Security portion applies to the first $184,500. The page we used does not list the 2025 cap.
  • Net self-employment earnings of $400 or more require SE tax and Schedule SE.
  • High earners also owe the 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax above $200,000 (single/head of household), $250,000 (married filing jointly) or $125,000 (married filing separately).

Deductible expenses

Business expenses are subtracted from business income to reach net profit. A net loss can generally offset other income, though some losses are limited. Common items:

  • Software, equipment and website costs used for the work
  • Professional fees (accounting, legal)
  • Home office deduction, which the IRS says both homeowners and renters may claim

For which items qualify and how to compute them, see IRS Publication 334 (Tax Guide for Small Business). Keep receipts and records.

Estimated tax: nobody withholds, so you pay quarterly

  • If you expect to owe $1,000 or more when filing, estimated tax is generally required (IRS).
  • Use Form 1040-ES to estimate; pay online, by phone, via the IRS app or by check.
  • There are four payment periods, each with its own due date. The IRS page does not list the dates; see Form 1040-ES.
  • Safe harbors: owe less than $1,000, or pay at least 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's, whichever is smaller.
  • High earners face special rules; the widely cited 110% rule is not on that IRS page, which refers readers to Publication 505.
  • Underpaying can be penalized even if you end up with a refund. Use Form 2210.

If the 1099-NEC amount is wrong

  1. Ask the payer to correct it and issue a new form.
  2. If they will not, report your actual income anyway and keep documentation.
  3. With several forms, or duplicates from one client, check each one so nothing is counted twice.

We did not find the 1099-NEC reporting threshold (historically $600) on the IRS pages consulted this time, nor confirmation of any change from 2026. The threshold affects only whether the payer must issue a form; it does not affect whether you must report the income. See the IRS instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC.

FAQ

I have one small 1099-NEC. Do I still need Schedule C?

Net self-employment earnings of $400 trigger reporting and SE tax. Whether smaller amounts must be reported depends on your other income.

Can I just put the 1099-NEC income on the other income line?

Not advisable. You lose expense deductions and may miss SE tax. The IRS approach is Schedule C plus Schedule SE.

What if I did not pay estimated tax?

You may be penalized even if a refund is due. Compute it on Form 2210, or catch up early using the safe harbors above.

Verify on the official sites. For complex cases (multi-state income, nonresident status, forming an LLC or S corp), consult a CPA or attorney.
