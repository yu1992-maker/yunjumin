The Chase 2/30 Rule: How Many Cards Can You Open in 30 Days?

Nov 8, 20263 min readYunjumin Forum editors

2/30 is an unofficial rule of thumb: Chase usually approves at most two cards in 30 days. How to count, whether business cards count, and what to do if denied.

2/30 means Chase will generally approve no more than two new cards in 30 days, and a third application is likely to be declined. Chase has not published it; it is inferred from applicants' data points, so "generally" and "likely" are not guarantees. Leave at least 30 days between applications, or three to four months to be conservative.

What the rule is and is not

  • Origin: Chase has never published it. The Points Guy, discussing 5/24, also notes that Chase has not formally announced such policies and that they are inferred from data points, some of them isolated.
  • Content: Most sources agree that in a rolling 30 days Chase generally approves no more than two new cards. The Points Guy cites data points suggesting the same limit.
  • Nature: It is a rule of thumb. Some report more approvals, but those cases are not a template.

A simple example

Travel Update's example: two cards approved on April 1, then a third application on April 21 is declined. On a rolling 30 days, wait until after May 1.

RuleContentStatus

5/24 covers personal cards from all banks, even closed ones, and authorized-user cards on your report. Business cards from Discover and TD Bank, and most Capital One small-business cards, count too. See The Points Guy for detail.

Applying in practice

  1. Count first: list approved Chase cards in the last 30 days and six months, business cards included.
  2. Check 5/24: most popular Chase cards are subject to it.
  3. Leave room: to apply for two, make sure there are no other approvals in the 30 days.
  4. Space applications: The Points Guy suggests at least three to four months, which reduces the risk of denial and closure.
  5. Consider a product change: it is not a new application.
  6. Track it: sites such as PointsNav log approvals and flag conflicts.

If you are declined

  • Read the stated reason; do not reapply on a guess.
  • You can call Chase reconsideration. One source says requests are possible within 30 days of applying, but that is not a Chase commitment.
  • Wait before applying for another Chase card.

The other risk of applying too often

Travel Update warns that too many applications in a short time may lead Chase to close existing cards, even with good credit, and forum users report one or more closures. These are mostly individual cases without official explanation, but they suggest that treating 2/30 as an allowance to be used in full is not safe.

FAQ

Do the 30 days run from application or approval?

Sources do not agree. Travel Update's example runs from approval. To be safe, wait 30 days from the latest approval plus a few more.

Do business cards count?

PointsNav says yes, Cloud9Club is stricter, Travel Update is silent. No consensus; plan as if they do.

Can I apply for three at once?

Not advisable. The third will probably be declined, leaves a hard inquiry and may draw attention to your accounts.

A caution

2/30, 4/6 and 5/24 are not published by Chase, sites differ in data and dates, and Chase may change course at any time. Check Chase's website and application pages, and consult a CPA or attorney for complex credit, tax or business-account questions.
