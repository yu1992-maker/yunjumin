Name Misspelled on Your Canadian Credit Report? Disputing With TransUnion and Equifax

Nov 11, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

How to file separate disputes with TransUnion and Equifax when your name is misspelled on a Canadian credit report, what to prepare and how long it takes.

If your name is misspelled on a Canadian credit report, file disputes with TransUnion and Equifax separately; they do not share investigation results. Official guidance says corrections are free, and each bureau has its own online form, with mail as an alternative. Prepare identity documents and supporting evidence, and expect results in roughly 30 days.

This guide draws on the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC) and public information from TransUnion and Equifax. TransUnion's site was unavailable when we checked and some Equifax pages could not be read, so certain details are marked "sources differ" or "unverified." Confirm on the bureaus' sites before you submit.

First: a misspelling or a name change?

  • Misspelling (wrong order of pinyin names, a missing letter, an extra middle name): use the dispute/correction process below.
  • Legal name change or married name: one source says TransUnion offers a name-change form requiring a change-of-name certificate or marriage certificate. We could not verify this form on TransUnion's site.

The FCAC states that errors in personal information, such as address or date of birth, can be disputed, but it does not describe a specific process for name updates, so check each bureau's site.

Checklist before you file

  1. Download your latest TransUnion and Equifax reports and mark every name issue, including the main file and any "also known as" entries.
  2. Prepare documents proving the correct spelling: PR card, passport, driver's licence, birth certificate. Use the spelling that matches your passport or PR card.
  3. Combine multi-page documents into one PDF. One Equifax page notes the online form accepts at most three attachments.
  4. State clearly which field is wrong, what it shows, what it should show, and which document proves it. Guides suggest this because evidence-backed disputes are more likely to be accepted.

Filing with TransUnion Canada

  1. Go to the "Credit Report Disputes" page on TransUnion Canada's site (linked from the FCAC page).
  2. Complete the correction form (Hardbacon calls it the Credit Investigation Request Form), describe the error and upload evidence.
  3. Wait for the investigation. TransUnion typically completes it within 30 days of receipt (Hardbacon).
  4. A dispute does not guarantee a change. If the bureau finds the data accurate, you may need to go back to the lender that reported it.

Mailing address: one guide lists a PO box in Hamilton, but we could not verify it on TransUnion's site, so we do not reproduce it. Use the bureau's contact page.

Filing with Equifax Canada

  1. Go to the "Dispute Credit Report" page on Equifax Canada's site (linked from the FCAC page).
  2. Complete the Consumer Credit Report Update Form online or by mail.
  3. Upload supporting documents, again within the three-file limit.
  4. By mail, one Equifax page lists Equifax Canada Co., National Consumer Relations, Box 190, Montreal, Quebec H1S 2Z2. The same page carried a Canada Post strike notice, so confirm mail service and that the address is current.

Contact the lender too

If a bank or card issuer reported the wrong spelling, the FCAC suggests contacting it directly to check its records and send corrected data to the bureaus, which can be faster. Otherwise the lender may report the old spelling again next month after the bureau corrects it.

If you are not satisfied

  • Ask for a higher-level review at the bureau or lender.
  • Federally regulated financial institutions must have a complaints process and an external complaints body; the FCAC has guidance.
  • Add a free consumer statement to your report. For TransUnion, Hardbacon says it is generally limited to 100 words (200 in Saskatchewan) and requires a written request, two accepted pieces of ID, your name, current address, date of birth and signature. The FCAC says only that bureaus set the length and it may vary by province.

FAQ

Do I need to dispute with both bureaus?

Yes. Official and third-party sources agree that each keeps its own file and investigation, and neither updates the other.

Does a dispute cost anything?

No. The FCAC states bureaus must correct errors free of charge, and consumer statements are free. Fraud alerts may carry a fee.

Does a misspelled name hurt my score?

Not necessarily by itself, but it can split or wrongly merge files or cause identity-matching failures. There have been Canadian news cases of TransUnion mixing up people with similar names and lowering scores, so check early.

Rely on the official TransUnion, Equifax and FCAC pages. For identity theft, wrongly merged files, or a rejected dispute you want to pursue, consult a licensed lawyer or qualified professional.
