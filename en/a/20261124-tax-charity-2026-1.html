Charitable deductions in 2026: $1,000 without itemizing, a 0.5% floor with it

Nov 24, 20265 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Nonresidents on Form 1040-NR qualify; bunching two years adds $7,200 of deductions; the 37% bracket is capped at 35%.

From tax year 2026, people who do not itemize can deduct up to $1,000 of cash gifts ($2,000 on a joint return), and IRS Publication 6196 names nonresidents filing Form 1040-NR among those eligible. Itemizers face three new constraints: gifts count only above 0.5% of AGI, the value of the deduction is capped at about 35% for those in the 37% bracket, and amounts lost to the 0.5% floor usually disappear. For many middle-income households, giving two years' worth in one year saves more than giving evenly.

Sources: IRS news release IR-2026-135 (November 10, 2026), Publication 6196 (October 2026), Publication 526, Topic 506, sections 68, 164 and 170 of the Internal Revenue Code, and the draft 2026 Form 1040, Form 1040-NR and Schedule A. Two points are easily misread. The draft places line 12f after AGI (line 11b), so the deduction lowers taxable income but not AGI and will not bring you under thresholds that depend on AGI. And it stacks on the standard deduction. The IRS figures for 2026 are $16,100 single, $24,150 head of household and $32,200 joint, so a joint return can reach $34,200.

2. Nonresidents and ITIN holders: an unusual explicit benefit

Nonresidents generally have no standard deduction (the draft form excepts certain residents of India), which shuts them out of many breaks. Publication 6196 is clear this time: taxpayers qualify if they do not itemize, and they "may take the standard deduction but are not required to do so", with nonresidents and separate filers whose spouse itemizes given as examples. Section 170(p) lists no SSN requirement, unlike the tip and overtime deductions, which need a work-eligible SSN.

Example: an F-1 student in Florida earns $30,000 in wages in 2026 and charges a $600 gift to a qualified U.S. charity, with no state income tax. Itemizing gives $600 less $150 (0.5% of AGI), or $450. Line 12b gives $600, worth $72 at 12%. In California with $1,200 of state tax, itemizing totals $1,650 and still wins. State income tax decides the track.

3. Three new hurdles for itemizers

  • The 0.5% floor. With AGI of $200,000, the first $1,000 of the year's gifts does not count. The floor applies to the annual total, not to each gift.
  • The 35% cap. Section 68 reduces itemized deductions by 2/37 of the lesser of total itemized deductions or the amount by which taxable income, with those deductions added back, exceeds the start of the 37% bracket: $640,600 single and $768,700 joint for 2026. Take a couple with $900,000 after the add-back and $100,000 of deductions. The excess is $131,300, so the lesser is $100,000, and 2/37 of it is $5,405. The remaining $94,595 at 37% saves $35,000, exactly 35%.
  • Floored amounts rarely carry over. Under section 170(d)(1)(C), they join a carryover only in a year when a percentage limit is exceeded. Most households never give 60% of AGI, so the amount is lost.

State tax is the other moving part. Section 164 sets the 2026 SALT cap at $40,400, phased down above $505,000 of modified AGI to no less than $10,000. The higher cap pushes more homeowners back above the standard deduction, so the choice of track needs to be recalculated.

4. Worked example: $20,000 over two years

A joint couple has AGI of $200,000, $14,000 of mortgage interest, $12,000 of state and local tax, and plans to give $10,000 a year. For comparison, assume the 2027 standard deduction equals 2026's.

Plan2026 deduction2027 deductionTotal
A: $10,000 each year$26,000 + $9,000 = $35,000$35,000$70,000
B: $20,000 in 2026, none in 2027$26,000 + $19,000 = $45,000$32,200$77,200
C: $18,000 in 2026, $2,000 cash in 2027$26,000 + $17,000 = $43,000$32,200 + $2,000 = $34,200$77,200

B and C deduct $7,200 more than A, about $1,584 at 22%. C spreads the cash but matches B, because the second-year $2,000 is free of the 0.5% floor. The gain comes from paying the floor once and using one full standard deduction.

5. Advanced techniques

  • Bunch through a DAF, but not in a non-itemizing year. Fund it in the itemizing year, deduct then, and grant over time. To use the $1,000 or $2,000 in other years you must give directly to a charity; IR-2026-135 excludes DAF contributions.
  • At 70½ or older, look at a QCD first. Notice 2025-67 sets the 2026 limit at $111,000 from an IRA. A qualifying distribution is not income, so it never meets the 0.5% floor or the 35% cap. It cannot also be deducted.
  • Appreciated stock in itemizing years, cash in the others. Listed stock held more than a year is generally deductible at market value and avoids capital gains, within a 30%-of-AGI limit. Property does not qualify under section 170(p). Reverse the order and the deduction is zero.
  • December 31 timing. Publication 526: a check counts when mailed and a credit card gift when charged, even if paid next year. A stock certificate counts when mailed or delivered, or when transferred on the company's books if sent to the issuer. A promissory note is not a gift until paid.
  • Get the $250 acknowledgment before filing. Any single gift of $250 or more needs a written acknowledgment stating whether goods or services were provided, in hand by the earlier of the filing date or the due date including extensions. Weekly $25 gifts are not combined.

6. Common mistakes

  • "Crowdfunding or helping relatives counts." Topic 506 says gifts to individuals are not deductible. Check the organization under Pub. 78 Data in the IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search.
  • "Gifts to charities in China or elsewhere count." Publication 526 generally excludes foreign organizations. Treaty exceptions exist for Canada, Mexico and Israel, usually requiring income from that country.
  • "Separate filers get $2,000." Section 170(p) gives $2,000 only to joint returns; separate filers and heads of household get $1,000.
  • "Clothing and furniture fit in the $1,000." IR-2026-135 limits it to cash, which under Publication 526 includes checks, electronic transfers, and debit and credit cards.
  • "A gala ticket is fully deductible." Only the amount above the fair value of what you receive.

Frequently asked questions

Do 2025 gifts qualify?

No. IR-2026-135 says the deduction begins with tax year 2026, on returns filed in 2027.

Does it expire?

The IRS calls it permanent. Section 170(p) contains no end date and no inflation adjustment.

Can the unused part be carried forward?

No. Section 170(p) is computed without the carryover rule in section 170(d)(1). A single non-itemizer who gives $1,500 loses the extra $500.
