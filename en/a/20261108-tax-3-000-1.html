How Much Tax Can Stock Losses Offset? The $3,000 Cap and Carryovers

Nov 8, 20263 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Losses offset capital gains first, then up to $3,000 of other income; the rest carries forward. With a table, filing steps and the wash-sale rule.

Capital losses first offset the year's capital gains. Any excess offsets up to $3,000 of other income a year ($1,500 if married filing separately). What remains is not lost; it carries to later years. This summary follows IRS Topic 409 and Publication 550.

How the offset works

  1. Capital gains come first. Losses are netted against gains on stocks, funds and other assets sold that year.
  2. A net loss then offsets ordinary income. Under Topic 409 the deduction is the lesser of $3,000 ($1,500 married filing separately) and the total net loss.
  3. The excess carries over. The IRS says a net loss above the annual limit carries to later years.

Short-term and long-term positions are computed separately. One year or less is short-term; more than one year is long-term. A net short-term loss is short-term losses, including last year's carryover, less short-term gains; the long-term figure works the same way.

Example: a $10,000 loss

SituationUsable this yearCarried forward
No capital gains, $10,000 loss$3,000 against wages and other income$7,000
$4,000 gain, $10,000 loss$4,000 against gains, then $3,000 of the $6,000 net loss$3,000
$12,000 gain, $10,000 lossNet gain of $2,000; no loss deduction$0

The $3,000 cap applies to the net loss used against ordinary income, not to total losses. Losses used against gains are not subject to it.

Filing in four steps

  1. Get Form 1099-B from your broker, showing cost and proceeds for each sale.
  2. Report each sale on Form 8949; most transactions go through it.
  3. Carry the totals to Schedule D (Form 1040) to find the net gain or loss.
  4. Enter the deductible loss on Form 1040, line 7a, from Schedule D, line 16.

If Schedule D is not required, the IRS notes that you check the box on line 7b.

Using a carryover

  • Compute it with the Capital Loss Carryover Worksheet, in Publication 550 (Worksheet 4-1) or the Schedule D instructions.
  • Next year it is treated as a new short- or long-term loss: against gains first, then up to $3,000 of other income.
  • The short- or long-term character generally survives. That point comes from third-party software guidance; Publication 550 has a section titled "Use short-term losses first", which you should check for detail.
  • Keep each year's Schedule D and worksheet, and bring the figure across when you change software.

The wash-sale rule

If you buy substantially identical stock within 30 days before or after selling at a loss, the loss is not deductible that year. The window is 61 days including the sale date. Publication 550 lists these triggers:

  • buying substantially identical stock or securities;
  • acquiring them in a taxable exchange;
  • acquiring a contract or option to buy them;
  • buying them for an IRA or Roth IRA;
  • a purchase by your spouse or a corporation you control.

A disallowed loss is added to the basis of the new shares. In Publication 550's example, a disallowed $250 is added to an $800 purchase for a basis of $1,050, and the holding period includes that of the old shares. For purchases in an IRA or Roth IRA, the publication says the basis adjustment does not apply, so the loss may never be usable.

On Form 8949, enter W in column (f) and the disallowed amount, as a positive number, in column (g). Brokers usually mark it on the 1099-B. Whether securities are substantially identical depends on the facts; stock of different companies is generally said not to be, though that rests on informal IRS views. Ask a CPA if in doubt.

Where people go wrong

  • Losses on personal-use property are not deductible, including your home and car.
  • Married filing separately halves the cap to $1,500 each.
  • The window crosses year-end. A late-December sale and an early-January purchase can be a wash sale.
  • Related-party losses are not deductible, according to Publication 550.

FAQ

Does a carryover expire?

The IRS page says only that it carries to later years and states no limit. Recompute it each year with the worksheet.

Can I use losses with no gains this year?

Yes, up to $3,000 of other income ($1,500 married filing separately); the rest carries forward.

What if I sell and rebuy the same stock in a Roth IRA?

The loss is disallowed. Publication 550 treats it as a wash sale, and the loss is not added to basis.
