How Newcomers to Canada Get Their First Credit Card: Documents, Steps and Building Credit

Nov 20, 20263 min readYunjumin Forum editors

From PR card or work permit, proof of address and income to opening an account, applying and using the card—step by step to your first Canadian card and credit score.

the smoothest path to your first card is open a newcomer account at a big bank → apply for that bank's newcomer card at the same time → pay in full and on time. Below: what to prepare and what to do (from banks' newcomer pages, checked November 2026).

Step 1: Prepare documents

DocumentNotes
Original IDRBC says originals only, no photocopies—e.g. passport, driver's licence, PR card or work permit
Immigration statusPR card / confirmation of PR (e.g. IMM5292, IMM5688) or work permit (IMM 1442); CIBC requires these for its higher-limit offer, BMO requires proof of status
Canadian proof of addressRBC accepts a utility bill, bank statement or lease
SINTD and CIBC list it on applications; RBC says optional for account opening. Get it early
Proof of incomeScotiabank asks for an annual income letter from a Canadian employer; limits depend mainly on verifiable income

Step 2: Choose a bank, open an account, apply

  1. Check the time window: most newcomer programs require arrival within 5 years (an RBC page says PRs within 12 months, work-permit holders within 48 months). After that you're assessed as a regular customer.
  2. Open the account and apply together: RBC's newcomer card is for RBC personal banking clients; Scotiabank requires joining StartRight. Visiting a branch or booking a newcomer advisor is easiest (RBC newcomer line: 1-866-756-1107).
  3. Mention foreign credit history: Scotiabank works with Nova Credit to consider your home-country credit score for a higher limit.
  4. Declined? Don't panic: BMO offers a secured card with deposit if you're not approved unsecured; secured cards like Neo start from about $50 with no credit check (check official terms).

Step 3: Build your credit score

  • Pay the full balance before the due date every month: RBC notes this avoids interest, and it's the foundation of credit building.
  • Small, regular spending: phone bill, groceries—so the card keeps reporting to the credit bureaus (Equifax and TransUnion in Canada).
  • Don't max it out: keep your balance well below the limit, especially if the limit is low.
  • Don't apply for many cards at once: each application leaves an inquiry; use the first card well for 6+ months first.
  • Check your reports: you can get free reports from Equifax and TransUnion and dispute errors.

FAQ

Can I apply before I land?

Scotiabank StartRight has pre-arrival guidance, but its card requires residency in Canada; check the official site.

My limit is very low—what now?

Use it well and pay on time, then request an increase after a few months, and update your income when it changes.
