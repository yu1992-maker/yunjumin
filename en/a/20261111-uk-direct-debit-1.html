UK Switching Bonuses: How Many Direct Debits Count, and Which Ones

Nov 11, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

The Direct Debit tests behind UK switching bonuses: how many are needed, what qualifies, what does not, common traps, and a comparison of 2026 bank terms.

Most UK switching bonuses require two or three active Direct Debits moved from your old account through CASS. Setting up a few new ones on the new account does not usually count. Some banks add a minimum deposit and others have none, so check the terms of the current offer.

The figures below come from third-party roundups such as Depositscout, Be Clever With Your Cash, Which? and Moneyfacts. Their dates vary and some contradict each other, and we flag those conflicts as such. Check the bank's own terms before you act.

First, what Direct Debits and CASS are

  • Direct Debit: your authorisation for a company, such as an energy, broadband, mobile or council tax provider, to take regular payments from your UK account. It can only be set up on a UK bank account.
  • CASS (Current Account Switch Service): the official UK service that moves Direct Debits and standing orders from your old account to the new one and closes the old account. Most switching bonuses require a full CASS switch.
Note: none of the bank pages we found state a residency requirement. UK accounts generally need a UK address, so readers in the US or Canada should confirm with the bank whether they can open an account or use CASS.

Which Direct Debits "count"

Take Club Lloyds' terms as an example, as summarised by Depositscout:

  • Counts: a DD that has actually collected a payment in the past 13 months and moves across with the switch.
  • Does not count: DDs set up after the switch starts, standing orders, recurring card payments, and newly created DDs that have not yet collected.

Santander's wording is different. It accepts only household bills, namely council tax, gas, electricity, water, broadband, mobile, landline and pay TV. Two are required, and both must stay in place until the bonus is paid.

So there is no single answer to "how many count". Lloyds looks at how many recently active DDs the account already has, while Santander looks at the type of bill.

Steps

  1. Audit the old account: list the DDs that have collected in the past 13 months and check whether you have enough. Do not rely on adding them after the switch.
  2. Set them up before switching: if you are short, create the DD on the old account before requesting the switch and let it collect once, for example a mobile or broadband bill. DDs created after the switch starts do not count.
  3. Check the category: if the bank accepts only household bills, as Santander does, match yours against the terms.
  4. Open through the required channel: some banks require app-only applications (HSBC, for example), and a web application may not qualify.
  5. Start CASS: choose a full switch, not a partial switch.
  6. Meet the deposit requirement: pay in the required amount within the deadline and keep records.
  7. Keep everything until the bonus arrives: do not close the new account or cancel the DDs until the bonus is paid.

Common traps

  • Treating standing orders as DDs: Lloyds does not count them.
  • Previous bonuses from the same group: Lloyds' terms exclude anyone who has received a switching bonus from Lloyds, Halifax or Bank of Scotland since 1 January 2023, and you cannot switch from those brands either. HSBC applies similar limits to recent HSBC or First Direct customers.
  • Joint accounts: Lloyds pays only one bonus per joint account.
  • Offers can be withdrawn: every bank's terms reserve the right to change or end the offer without notice.
  • Payment timing: the Lloyds summary page does not state when the bonus is paid; check the official site.

FAQ

How large does each Direct Debit need to be?

The terms we reviewed set no minimum amount per DD. They specify the number and type. Deposit thresholds are a separate requirement, such as Santander's £1,500 or Nationwide's £1,000.

The offer needs 3 DDs and I have only 1. What can I do?

Under Lloyds' wording, DDs created after the switch starts do not count, and neither do new DDs that have not yet collected. Either choose a bank that requires only 2 DDs or none, or set up the extra DDs and let them collect before you start the switch. Whether they are accepted depends on the bank's terms and on confirmation from customer service.

Can I do this if I do not live in the UK?

The pages we found do not state a residency requirement. Most UK accounts and CASS need a UK address, so ask the bank directly rather than guess.
