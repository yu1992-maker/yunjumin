Can you open Starling online without a UK address? Eligibility and alternatives

Nov 8, 20265 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Starling is for UK residents and its address documents are British, so applicants in the U.S. or Canada rarely qualify.

Realistically, no. Starling's personal current account is for UK residents, applications are made in the mobile app, and almost every document on its proof-of-address list is British. No single sentence on the site says a UK address is mandatory, but without one an applicant is unlikely to pass. Official and third-party statements are separated below.

What Starling says

Key points on the official pages we found:

  • To open an account you need a smartphone that can receive signal, valid photo ID (passport or driving license), a selfie video to match it, and possibly proof of address through the app.
  • The page says nothing about nonresidents, FATCA or tax residence.
  • Savings products (Easy Saver, Flexible Cash ISA) are stated to be for UK residents aged 18 or over who already hold a Starling personal or joint current account.
  • The ID page says an eVisa share code may be requested to confirm immigration status, and that one is needed when using a non-UK passport.

The site avoids saying directly whether people overseas can apply, but share codes and the address rules are plainly designed for people in the UK.

Note in particular that statements from e-money institutions such as Curve, Revolut and Wise are not accepted. Using a Wise or Revolut GBP statement as proof of address does not work, and U.S. utility bills and bank statements are not on the list.

Where sources disagree

  • "Must be a permanent UK resident." Wise's guide lists this as a condition, and other third-party guides say both Starling and Monzo require UK residence in their terms. We could not match the same sentence on Starling's own site.
  • "No proof of address needed." Finder lists Starling among banks that usually do not ask; Starling says it may. The two are consistent: it depends on electronic checks. Neither means a UK address is unnecessary.
  • "Starling helps nonresidents." One Finder guide says Monzo, Monese, Starling and Revolut can open accounts for nonresidents without proof of residence. It is an outlier among the sources and dated, and should not be relied on.

If you already have an account and move to the U.S. or Canada

  • Third-party guides say the terms limit accounts to UK residents, and that Starling may restrict or close one when you stop being resident.
  • A Financial Ombudsman decision (DRN-5318342) notes that the terms allow Starling to offer accounts only to UK residents and to close them on 60 days' notice. Another decision found three months' notice unfair where the customer had used the account for about two years without any issue being raised. Outcomes depend on the facts.
  • Some forum users report being told that keeping the account overseas is fine. That is individual experience, conflicts with the terms above, and is no guarantee.

Whether the account lasts depends on Starling's review at the time. Do not treat it as a permanent overseas account.

Business accounts are stricter

Company accounts require checks on credit data and the nature of the business, with evidence. Third-party sources say limited companies and LLPs need every person with significant control to be a UK resident, so a U.S. LLC has essentially no route. Check the official eligibility page yourself.

Alternatives without a UK address

  1. Wise: provides UK account details (sort code and number) as a multi-currency account, not a traditional bank account. Open to people in most countries.
  2. Revolut: third parties say it can give UK sort codes without a UK address, but only to residents of the EEA, the UK, Switzerland and certain supported countries. We could not confirm the U.S. or Canada; check the local app.
  3. Monese: third parties say no UK address or credit history is required, only email, phone and photo ID. This may be out of date.
  4. International accounts at traditional banks: one 2026 third-party source gives thresholds of about £50,000 for HSBC Expat and £100,000 for Barclays International. Figures vary, so confirm with each bank.

Checklist before applying

  1. Do you have a UK address and a document from the table? If not, do not spend the time.
  2. Do the names on ID and address proof match exactly, with no abbreviations?
  3. With a foreign passport, have an eVisa share code ready.
  4. Americans should plan for tax reporting: account data may go to the IRS, FATCA self-certification is common at opening, and annual FBAR and FATCA filings may apply. Check IRS.gov for current thresholds.
  5. If you need to receive and move pounds rather than a true bank account, start with something like Wise.

Frequently asked questions

Can I apply with a U.S. address and passport?

The site does not expressly prohibit it, but the account is for UK residents and no U.S. document is on the list. Third-party sources generally say nonresidents cannot open one.

Will a Wise or Revolut statement work as proof of address?

No. The help center says statements from e-money institutions such as Curve, Revolut and Wise are not accepted.

Will my account be closed if I move to the U.S.?

Possibly. The terms allow restriction or closure when you cease to be UK resident. Ombudsman decisions show that notice is usually required, with varying outcomes. Prepare a backup for receiving payments.
