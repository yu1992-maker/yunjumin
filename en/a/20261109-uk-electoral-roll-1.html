The UK Electoral Roll and Your Credit Score: What Happens If You Are Not Registered

Nov 9, 20265 min readYunjumin Forum editors

The Electoral Roll is a key source credit agencies use to verify your name and address. Not registering does not cost points directly, but it makes credit and accounts harder to get. Here is how to fix it.

The UK electoral register (Electoral Roll) is one of the main sources credit reference agencies such as Experian and Equifax, and lenders, use to verify your name and address. If you are not on it, your credit report lacks that entry and lenders find it harder to confirm your identity; applications for credit cards, loans and phone contracts may require extra proof or be declined. If you are eligible, register as soon as possible; if you are not, you can add an explanatory note to your credit report.

Note: this article relies mainly on Experian and Equifax's official English-language pages; the Equifax page was last updated in March 2022. Scoring models are not public, and the effect varies from person to person.

Why registering affects your credit

  • Once registered, your electoral details appear on your credit report. Experian says lenders use this to confirm your name and address, and your score may rise as a result.
  • Equifax calls the electoral register one of the most reliable ways to verify identity, but says its effect varies by lender, is only one of many factors and does not guarantee better credit.
  • Whether you actually vote is irrelevant; only registration counts.
  • On "how many points": an Experian press release from 2016 estimated around 50 points. That figure is old, and we found no current official number, so do not rely on it.

Experian adds that registering is a legal requirement in the UK, even if you do not plan to vote. Registration also helps with insurance, legal and accounting services, some public services and some financial-sector jobs.

Who can register

According to Experian, you must:

  • be 18 or over (in England and Wales you can register from 16 but vote from 18, per Equifax);
  • be a British, Irish or qualifying Commonwealth citizen;
  • live in the UK (or be a British citizen registered within the past 15 years);
  • not be legally barred from voting.

Commonwealth citizens also need leave to remain, or must not require it. EU citizens' eligibility changed after the Elections Act 2022; council pages differ and some are out of date, so check current rules on GOV.UK. Most residents from mainland China who are not British, Irish or Commonwealth citizens are not eligible.

How to register (England, Scotland and Wales)

  1. Go to the Register to vote page on GOV.UK and apply online, or post a paper form.
  2. Have your name, address, date of birth and National Insurance number ready.
  3. Use a long-term address where possible. Experian warns that temporary addresses carry identity-theft risk and may make you look less stable to lenders.
  4. Submit and wait; it usually appears on your credit report within 30 days.
  5. After a month, check your report through a free service to confirm the "Electoral Roll" entry.

Northern Ireland works differently and requires you to return a registration form.

Timing: the annual canvass

  • Councils send a Household Enquiry Form each year, typically between July and November according to Experian.
  • Experian says councils do not update the register during the canvass, so registrations made in that period appear on credit reports only from December 1.
  • As of November 9, 2026, anyone registering now may not see the entry on their report until after December 1.

Registered but not showing on your report?

  1. Contact your local Electoral Registration Office to confirm you are on the register; the Electoral Commission website lists contacts by postcode.
  2. If you are, ask them to notify the credit agencies, or ask Experian, Equifax and others directly for a correction.
  3. A Financial Ombudsman Service decision shows a case in which Experian corrected the record only after the person provided confirmation from the local council.
  4. Residents of Guernsey in the Channel Islands are not covered automatically; Experian says you can send proof of registration to update manually.

If you are not eligible

Experian says people who cannot register can ask for a note on their credit report explaining why, ideally with proof of address and length of residence. The note helps lenders understand your situation but does not guarantee approval; lenders still look at the rest of your file.

  • Keep plenty of address evidence, such as utility bills, tenancy agreements and bank statements.
  • Stay at one address and keep your details consistent across accounts.
  • Start with accounts that are easy to obtain and build a record gradually.

Should you opt out of the open register?

VersionUseCan you opt out?
Full registerElections, fraud prevention and credit checks; credit agencies can buy itNo
Open registerSold to companies for marketing; basic details onlyYes, via your local council

Several councils say opting out of the open register affects neither your right to vote nor your credit rating, because credit agencies still see you on the full register. One council's wording says agencies cannot pass your details on after you opt out, which refers to onward sharing rather than the agencies' own checks; council pages differ. If you want less marketing, you can opt out.

FAQ

Will my score definitely rise after registering?

Not necessarily. Experian says it may rise; Equifax says it depends on the lender and is one factor among many.

I moved. Do I need to register again?

Yes. Register at your new address after moving; Experian recommends using a long-term address. You may be registered in more than one place but can vote only once in each election.

If I am not eligible, can I be fined for not registering?

No obligation applies to people who are not eligible. The fine Equifax mentions applies to eligible people who receive a notice and fail to respond without good reason. If you are not eligible, request a note on your credit report.
