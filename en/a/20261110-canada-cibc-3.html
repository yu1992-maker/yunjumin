Cutting CIBC Foreign Transfer Costs: USD Account vs Converting and Sending

Nov 10, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Most of the cost of a CIBC transfer is in the exchange-rate markup. We compare holding USD directly with converting and sending, with wire fees, conflicting sources and steps to save.

The bulk of the cost of a CIBC foreign transfer is usually the exchange-rate markup, not the fee. To save, avoid an unnecessary CAD-to-USD conversion: if you earn or spend US dollars, receive and pay from a USD account instead of converting to CAD and back. When you must convert, compare quotes before choosing a bank or a third party.

Note: we could not retrieve CIBC's current fee page (the official pages we tried returned 404). The figures below come from third-party comparison sites, several of them articles by competitors such as Wise, which may be biased. Confirm with CIBC's website or a branch quote before sending.

2. How large is the markup? Sources differ

  • Finder: bank markups typically 2%–5%, depending on currency.
  • XE: about 2% on average at Canadian banks.
  • Exiap: about 3%.
  • Wise's CIBC article: about 2.5%–3.5%.
  • Best Exchange Rates: 4%–8% depending on amount (one CAD/AED snapshot was about 6.8% below mid-market, a single currency at a single point in time).

The range is wide, and we cannot give a precise figure. In practice, check the mid-market rate (for example on XE) on the day and compare it with CIBC's quote to see the real gap.

A rough calculation (illustrative)

Knightsbridge FX's example: converting C$1,000 to USD at CIBC's 0.7380 versus 0.7550 is a difference of about US$17. Using the 2%–3.5% range above, the hidden cost on C$10,000 would be roughly C$200–350, far more than a C$30 wire fee. This is an estimate, not a CIBC-published price.

3. Where a USD account saves money

In most cases it saves the conversion markup. Typical uses:

  • Deposit USD salary, refunds or royalties directly into the USD account without converting.
  • Pay US landlords, schools or relatives from the USD account rather than converting from CAD.
  • Hold US dollars you plan to spend (travel, US assets) in the USD account.

USD account fees (sources differ)

  • Personal USD account: a Wise article says US$0 a month with a US$1,000 minimum balance, otherwise US$8; interest 0.05%–0.30%.
  • Business USD chequing: US$8 a month plus US$1 per self-serve transaction.
  • Incoming USD wires: one Wise article says US$6.11, another says US$15 for personal and business accounts; the two conflict. Incoming CAD wires are consistently C$15 each.
  • Outgoing USD wires: the only figures found (US$30 domestic, US$40 international) are for CIBC Bank USA, the US subsidiary, and do not necessarily apply to Canadian USD accounts.

4. Steps to save

  1. Decide whether you need to convert at all. If you can pay in USD directly, do not convert.
  2. Get the day's actual rate and fees from online banking or a branch, and screenshot them.
  3. Look up the mid-market rate (for example on XE) and multiply the gap by the amount: that is your true cost.
  4. For large sums, reduce the number of transfers, since wires carry a fixed fee per transfer, but watch daily limits. One source says Global Money Transfer may require another channel above C$30,000 per day.
  5. Clarify who pays fees, so the recipient bank or an intermediary does not deduct a further charge.
  6. For larger amounts, compare a third-party quote such as Wise for the same day and amount, remembering that comparison sites have their own interests.
  7. Keep records of transfers for tax filing or proof of funds.

5. When a USD account may not pay off

  • Your USD balance is small and often below the minimum, triggering monthly fees.
  • You send one small USD payment a month, so the wire fee is proportionally high; compare third parties.
  • Usage limits: some reviews note Global Money Transfer counts against your plan's transaction allowance.

FAQ

Is Global Money Transfer really fee-free?

Several sources say there is no upfront fee, but the cost sits in the exchange rate. CIBC does not disclose its FX margin, so it is not accurate to call it free.

Is a USD account always cheaper than a regular transfer?

No. If you already need to pay in USD and have USD available, you avoid the markup and usually save. If you still have to convert CAD first, the markup remains.

Wire or online transfer?

Per third-party data, wires carry tiered C$30/50/80 fees plus the FX markup, while online transfers claim no upfront fee. Which is cheaper depends on the amount and the day's rate, so get both quotes.

These fees come from third-party sources, could not be verified on CIBC's website and may be outdated. Verify with CIBC; for tax reporting, large sums or cross-border assets, consult a CPA or lawyer.
