New Mexico LLC Costs: State Fees, Registered Agents and Annual Spend

Nov 6, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

The New Mexico LLC filing fee is about $50 with no annual report. Ongoing costs are mainly a registered agent (about $0 to $300 a year) and tax preparation.

Filing Articles of Organization for a New Mexico LLC costs a one-time $50, and most sources say there is no annual report and no annual fee. Acting as your own registered agent costs $0; a commercial agent typically charges about $50 to $300 a year. The floor is about $50, and a reasonable first-year budget is $50 plus roughly $125 for an agent.

A caveat: most figures below come from commercial formation sites. The New Mexico Secretary of State's site (sos.nm.gov) could not be opened for verification on this check, so confirm against the official fee schedule before filing.

2. Registered agent costs

New Mexico requires every LLC to appoint a registered agent with a physical address in the state.

  • You or a friend: $0, if that person has a New Mexico street address and is available during business hours. Residents of Florida and other states generally cannot do this.
  • Commercial agent: quotes vary widely, about $125 a year (LLC University and another source), $130 to $300, or $50 to $100. Sources differ; rely on the provider's current price.
  • Changing agents: file a change form with the Secretary of State; reported fee $22.

Owners living abroad or in another state will almost always need a commercial agent. Choose one that renews reliably and forwards legal documents promptly, not just the cheapest first-year promotion.

3. Recurring annual costs

  1. State annual report or fee: $0. LegalZoom, Northwest and ezel all say New Mexico LLCs have no annual or biennial report, the main reason it is called a low-maintenance state.
  2. Registered agent: about $0 to $300, most commonly around $125.
  3. Tax and accounting: LLC University cites about $300 to $900 a year for an accountant, up to $1,200, depending on complexity.
  4. Taxes and licences: New Mexico has its own tax regime (for example, state taxes that may apply to sales of goods or services). Amounts depend on industry and location; the sources gave no figures, so confirm with the state tax department or an accountant.

4. Federal level: is BOI still required?

FinCEN states that entities formed in the United States no longer need to file beneficial ownership information (BOI) reports; the final rule was published on 11 August 2026 and took effect on 14 August, and US persons need not be reported as beneficial owners. Only foreign entities registered to do business in a US state remain covered. A newly formed New Mexico LLC therefore has no BOI cost; if anyone charges a “BOI filing fee”, check FinCEN's site first.

5. Formation checklist

  1. Choose a name and check the Secretary of State's database for conflicts.
  2. Appoint a registered agent and obtain their New Mexico street address.
  3. File Articles of Organization online and pay $50.
  4. After approval, apply for a free EIN on the IRS site.
  5. Draft an operating agreement and open a business bank account.
  6. Check state and local tax registration and licensing for your business.

FAQ

Is there really no annual fee?

Most sources say there is no annual report or fee, so fixed state costs are $0. Registered agent, tax preparation and applicable taxes still apply. The Secretary of State's site governs.

I don't live in New Mexico. Can I be my own agent?

No. The agent needs a New Mexico street address. Non-residents need a commercial agent, commonly about $125 a year.

Do I need to file BOI with FinCEN after forming?

Not for an LLC formed in the US. FinCEN says the final rule effective 14 August 2026 exempts US entities; only foreign entities registered in the US may need to report.

Note: Fees and rules are governed by the New Mexico Secretary of State, the IRS and FinCEN. For complex tax, immigration-status or multi-state questions, consult a CPA or attorney.
