UK Self Assessment: Who Must File, the 31 January 2027 Deadline and Penalties

Nov 20, 20263 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Online returns and payment for 2025/26 are due 31 January 2027; first-time filers had to register by 5 October 2026. Who files, key dates, late penalties and Making Tax Digital from April 2026.

most UK employees have tax taken through PAYE and don't need to file. But if you have self-employment, rental or foreign income, you need to do Self Assessment. For the 2025/26 tax year (6 April 2025 – 5 April 2026), the online filing and payment deadline is 31 January 2027.

Compiled from GOV.UK and HMRC (checked November 2026); the official pages prevail.

Who needs to file

Common cases include:

  • Self-employed (sole trader) income over £1,000;
  • Partner in a partnership;
  • Untaxed income such as rental income;
  • Foreign income that's taxable in the UK;
  • You or your partner receive Child Benefit and one of you earns over £60,000.

Not sure? Use GOV.UK's “Check if you need to send a Self Assessment tax return” tool. If you no longer need to file, tell HMRC—otherwise a return is still expected and a non-filing penalty can follow.

Key dates for 2025/26

WhatDeadline
Register for Self Assessment (first time)5 October 2026 (if later, HMRC sets a deadline 3 months from its letter)

Pay what you owe through your tax codeFile online by 30 December 2026
Online return + payment11:59pm, 31 January 2027
Second payment on account (if applicable)31 July 2027

If you registered before but didn't file last year, you'll need to reactivate your account.

Late filing and late payment penalties

  • Late return: an initial £100, even if you owe no tax;
  • After 3 months: £10 a day, up to £900;
  • After 6 months: a further 5% of the tax due or £300, whichever is greater;
  • Late payment: 5% of unpaid tax at 30 days, 6 months and 12 months, plus interest.

A reasonable excuse can get a penalty cancelled.

New in 2026: Making Tax Digital (MTD)

From 6 April 2026, sole traders and landlords whose combined self-employment and property turnover (before expenses) exceeds £50,000 must keep digital records in compatible software and send quarterly updates to HMRC (first update due 7 August 2026). The threshold drops to £30,000 from April 2027 and £20,000 from April 2028. HMRC says it won't apply penalty points for late quarterly updates in the first year (2026/27), but late annual returns and payments are still penalised.

FAQ

I only have a salary taxed by my employer—do I file?

Usually not, unless you have one of the incomes above or HMRC writes asking you to file.

Are rates the same in Scotland?

No. Scotland has its own bands for earnings; the return calculates tax based on where you live.
