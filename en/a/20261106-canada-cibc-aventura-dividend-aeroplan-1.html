Choosing a CIBC Card: Aventura, Dividend and Aeroplan Compared

Nov 6, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Annual fees, earn rates, welcome bonuses and best-fit users for CIBC's Aventura, Dividend and Aeroplan Visa Infinite cards, with a four-step selection guide.

All three cards charge $120 to $139 a year, each with a first-year rebate. The choice comes down to spending and travel habits. For simple cash back, take the Dividend Visa Infinite. For frequent Air Canada flyers collecting miles, take the Aeroplan Visa Infinite. For flexible travel points and airport lounge access, take the Aventura Visa Infinite. Figures below come mainly from Prince of Travel's February 2026 update; welcome bonuses change often, so check CIBC's site before applying.

Aventura: flexible travel points

  • Welcome bonus: 15,000 points on first purchase, plus 30,000 after $3,000 spend in the first four statement periods, for 45,000.
  • Point value: Prince of Travel estimates about 1 cent per point for general use. Using the Aventura flight reward chart on an $800 fare can reach about 2.28 cents.
  • Perks: four Visa Airport Companion lounge visits a year, Skip+ membership ($10 monthly credit), NEXUS fee rebate every four years, and up to 10 cents a litre off at participating gas stations.
  • Insurance: out-of-country emergency medical up to $5,000,000, flight and baggage delay, trip cancellation and interruption, rental car damage; mobile device cover up to $1,000 for 24 months.
  • Note: the page says the offer is online-only and eligibility depends on the application channel. Opening, switching or closing another Aventura card within 12 months disqualifies you from the bonus.
  • Sources differ: Finder lists up to 35,000 points versus Prince of Travel's 45,000; Finder's offer page showed an expiry of 25 March 2026, now passed.

Dividend: low-effort cash back

  • Earn rates: 4% on gas, EV charging and groceries; 2% on transportation, dining and recurring payments; 1% elsewhere. The Dividend Platinum earns 3%/2%/1% for a $99 fee and needs only $15,000 income.
  • Welcome bonus: 10% back on net purchases in the first four statement periods, on up to $2,500 of spending, for a maximum of $250.
  • Other costs: Ratehub and others list purchase APRs of 20.99% to 21.99% and a 2.5% foreign transaction fee.
  • Sources differ: some say category rates are capped and drop to 1% above the cap; Prince of Travel lists no cap. Some pages mention $300 and $50 first-purchase bonuses not found in the main text. CIBC's site governs.

Aeroplan: Air Canada miles

  • Welcome bonus: up to 50,000 points in three tiers: 10,000 on first purchase; 15,000 after $6,000 spend within six months; 25,000 on the anniversary after $12,000 spend in the first year.
  • Perks: first checked bag free for you and up to eight companions, preferred Air Canada reward pricing, status qualifying credits (SQC) from spending, and an Avis Preferred Plus upgrade. Points do not expire while you hold the card.
  • Insurance: emergency medical up to $5M (first 15 days), flight delay $500, baggage delay $500 per person or $1,000 total.
  • No airport lounge access.
  • Sources differ: Finlywealth says 50,000 points, Finder 40,000. Prince of Travel's own page is inconsistent on SQC: 1,000 SQC per $20,000 in the summary box and per $10,000 in the benefits section.

Four steps to choose

  1. Check income: Infinite cards need $60,000 personal or $100,000 household; otherwise consider the Dividend Platinum ($15,000 minimum).
  2. Map a year of spending: if groceries and gas dominate, the Dividend's 4% is the most direct. Mind the bonus thresholds, from about $3,000 to $6,000.
  3. Consider travel: two or three Air Canada trips a year or more favours Aeroplan; occasional travel with lounge use and no airline loyalty favours Aventura.
  4. Count the first-year cost: all three rebate the first-year fee. To avoid later fees, Prince of Travel says Aventura and Aeroplan fees can be waived through a CIBC Smart Plus account.

Considerations for newcomers

  • Using the card in the US: the Dividend and Aeroplan charge 2.5% on foreign currency; the Aventura page does not state a figure. Check the terms before spending abroad.
  • CIBC cards generally require Canadian residence, a credit history and income; newcomers and temporary residents are subject to the bank's review.

FAQ

Can I hold all three?

You can apply for each, but bonus rules apply; for example, the Aventura bonus requires no Aventura card opened, switched or closed in the previous 12 months.

What happens after the first year?

The fee applies from year two. Prince of Travel says the Aventura and Aeroplan fees can be waived through a CIBC Smart Plus account, subject to the bank's conditions. Otherwise, decide whether to downgrade or cancel before the second fee posts.

Which earns the most?

It depends on your spending. If groceries and gas are a large share, the Dividend's 4% usually wins. Points are worth what you redeem them for; 1.5 points per dollar can be worth more or less than 1.5% on flights.
