Citi Double Cash Explained: 1% When You Buy, 1% When You Pay

Nov 10, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

How Citi Double Cash splits its 2% into 1% on purchases and 1% on payments, plus minimum-payment effects, redemption options, foreign fees and pairing strategies.

Citi Double Cash pays 2% in two halves: 1% when you buy and 1% when you pay. Pay on time and you effectively earn 2%, with no cap and no categories to activate. The catch is that the second 1% is based only on the amount you actually pay against purchases, so pay in full to secure the full 2%.

Compiled from Citi's official terms and FAQs and reviews by NerdWallet, AwardWallet and One Mile at a Time. Where sources disagree, we say so.

The rules in one table

StepCash backWhen it posts
Purchase1%After the purchase posts
Payment1%When you pay, based on the matching purchase amount
Total2%No cap, no category limits

Citi's wording: earn 1 ThankYou point per $1 spent and another point per $1 paid, provided there is a matching balance in the Purchase Tracker. The payment points follow your purchases.

How to earn the payment 1%

  1. Once purchases post, they appear as a balance on your statement.
  2. When you pay, Citi awards the second 1% on the amount paid against posted purchases.
  3. The terms require at least the minimum payment on time to earn cash back.
  4. Pay only the minimum and you earn 1% only on what you paid; the rest waits until you pay it off, while the remaining balance accrues interest.
Practical takeaway: pay the statement in full each month to earn the full 2% and avoid interest, which usually far exceeds the 2% reward.

What does not earn cash back

  • Balance transfers: no rewards, with a transfer fee and a promotional rate.
  • Cash advances: fees and no rewards.
  • Foreign-currency purchases: subject to a foreign transaction fee (see below).

Citi also warns that if you carry a balance-transfer balance and do not pay in full (including the transfer), new purchases may incur interest.

Reward form and redemption

Rewards are issued as ThankYou points, worth 1 cent each when redeemed for cash (Citi's example: 10,000 points = $100).

Points do not expire while the account is in good standing (per reviews citing Citi's terms).

Fees and pitfalls

  • Annual fee: $0 (consistent across sources).
  • Foreign transaction fee: 3% (One Mile at a Time and others); the Citi page we retrieved did not state the rate. At 3% you lose money abroad versus 2% back, so use a card without foreign fees.
  • Interest: carrying a balance incurs interest at the rate in your approval terms.
  • Citi Travel boost: Citi's page lists an extra 3% (5% total) on hotels, car rentals and attractions booked via Citi Travel, but some reviews describe it as a limited-time offer (one says through 31 December 2025). Sources differ; check the Citi Travel page before booking.
  • Welcome bonus: varies by period and channel; the terms say those who received a Double Cash bonus in the past 48 months are not eligible. Check the application page for the current amount.

How to pair it

  1. Use Double Cash as the 2% default for everyday domestic spending (groceries, utilities, online shopping).
  2. Where a category card pays more (such as some rotating 5% categories), use that card; otherwise default to Double Cash.
  3. If you also hold a premium ThankYou card such as Strata Premier or Strata Elite, points can be combined and transferred to partners (reviews say mostly 1:1). Held alone, Double Cash redeems only at 1 cent. Sources differ on some reduced transfer ratios for Double Cash; check Citi's transfer page.
  4. For cash redemption, reviews note Strata Premier and Elite redeem at only 0.75 cents per point, so keep points in the Double Cash account if you want cash.

Monthly checklist

  • After the statement date, confirm purchases have posted.
  • Pay in full before the due date; set Autopay to the full balance.
  • Check points after they post and redeem as statement credit or deposit when convenient.
  • Switch to a no-foreign-fee card before travelling abroad.

FAQ

1. If I pay only the minimum, do I still get 2%?

You earn the second 1% only on the amount paid, provided the minimum is paid on time; the rest is awarded when you pay it. Meanwhile the balance accrues interest, which is a poor trade.

2. What happens with refunds and returns?

We found no official Citi rule on reversing cash back for returns. Typically the related rewards are deducted; check your account records and Citi's terms.

3. Do points expire?

Reviews citing Citi's terms say no while the account is in good standing; they may be forfeited if the account is closed or seriously delinquent. Redeem regularly to be safe.
