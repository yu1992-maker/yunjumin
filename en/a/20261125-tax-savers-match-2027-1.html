Saver's Match 2027: a $1,000 federal match and a two-year lookback

Nov 25, 20266 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Single MAGI must be under $35,500; pre-tax deferrals are added back, withdrawals since 2025 cut the base.

From tax year 2027 the federal government will match up to 50% of the first $2,000 you put into a retirement account, up to $1,000 per person per year, even if you owe no tax. You claim it in 2028 on the new Form 8880-A. Three traps matter most: MAGI adds pre-tax contributions back, so a 401(k) deferral will not bring you under the limit; retirement withdrawals since 2025 shrink the base; and nonresidents and dual-status filers are excluded by statute, while the position of tax residents without green cards is unsettled.

Sources: the IRS Saver's Match page (updated November 10, 2026), Notice 2026-48 (IR-2026-89, August 7, 2026), section 6433 of the Internal Revenue Code, Notice 2025-67, IR-2026-136, the Justice Department Office of Legal Counsel (OLC) opinion of November 19, 2025, and 8 USC 1611 and 1641. Checked November 25, 2026. Notice 2026-48 announces an intent to propose regulations; neither the regulations nor Form 8880-A has been published.

The rate formula is in Q&A C-2 of Notice 2026-48: 50 × (MAGI − starting point) ÷ phaseout range, rounded down and subtracted from 50%. The ranges are $15,000 single, $22,500 head of household and $30,000 joint. For a single filer, every $300 of MAGI costs one point, or $20 on a full $2,000 contribution. Rounding favors the taxpayer: at MAGI of $30,000 the result is 31.67, only 31 points come off, and the rate is 19%.

2. Who qualifies: age, student status, residence

Section 6433(c) requires you to be 18 by year-end, not claimed as a dependent, and not a student (full-time in any five calendar months of the year). Nonresident aliens are excluded, and the wording is "any portion of the taxable year," so dual-status filers in an arrival year are out too unless they elect full-year resident treatment under section 6013(g) or (h).

Green card holders and other tax residents face different prospects. The OLC opinion of November 19, 2025 concluded that the Saver's Match is a "federal public benefit," and 8 USC 1611 bars aliens who are not "qualified aliens" from such benefits. The list in 8 USC 1641 includes permanent residents, asylees and refugees; it does not list nonimmigrant categories such as H-1B or F-1. Footnote 13 of Notice 2026-48 says only that the proposed regulations will address the point. Whether an H-1B worker or ITIN holder can claim for 2027 is therefore undecided. Neither section 6433 nor the notice itself states an SSN requirement.

3. Worked examples: same income, two years

The lowest earners gain most. Those between $35,500 and $40,250 single, or $71,000 and $80,500 joint, lose the old 10% credit. IR-2026-136 does raise the 2027 Saver's Credit limits to $83,000 joint and $41,500 single, but only for ABLE account contributions; it is often misread as an extension of the old credit.

4. Advanced techniques

  • Choose your year before April 15. Footnote 12 of Notice 2026-48 says an IRA contribution made from January 1, 2027 to the 2026 filing deadline (without extensions) may be designated for 2026, for the credit, or 2027, for the match. A single filer at $36,000 should choose 2026; someone with no 2026 tax should choose 2027.
  • Lower MAGI with deductions that are not added back. Q&A B-3 adds back pre-tax elective deferrals, deductible traditional IRA contributions and income excluded under sections 911, 931 and 933. HSA contributions are not on the list. A single worker on $30,000 who puts $3,000 into an HSA through payroll has MAGI of $27,000, a 29% rate instead of 19%, and $200 more match. This is our reading of the notice; the final regulations control.
  • A non-earning spouse counts separately. On a joint return eligibility is tested individually and income jointly. The IRS IRA limits page confirms spousal IRA contributions where one spouse has enough compensation, so a couple can reach $2,000 of match.
  • Set up the account first. Treasury pays directly only into traditional accounts. A Roth IRA destination runs through a conduit traditional IRA and a taxable conversion. Providers may decline; those that accept must register with the IRS and have an IRA tracking number. A match under $100 can be taken as a refund; exactly $100 (single MAGI of $34,000) cannot.
  • No offset for debts. Section 6433(f)(5) shields the payment from refund offsets for past-due support, federal debts and state income tax, and from other assessed federal taxes.

5. Common mistakes

  • "A small IRA withdrawal at the end of 2026 is harmless." The testing period covers the contribution year, the two prior years, and the time up to the filing deadline with extensions. The 2027 match looks back to January 1, 2025. The notice's example: $2,000 contributed in 2027, $500 withdrawn that year and $900 in 2026, leaves a base of $600. A joint-filing spouse's withdrawals count; plan loans, rollovers and Roth conversions do not.
  • "I can withdraw the match straight away." Before 59½ it is taxed and may carry the 10% additional tax. If the year-end balance falls below cumulative match amounts, a recovery tax under section 6433(f)(6) can apply, curable by redepositing before the deadline. Match money paid directly into a 401(k) is not available for hardship withdrawals.
  • "The match uses up my IRA limit." It does not. For 2027 the IRA limit stays $7,500 and the 401(k) limit is $25,500; the match is on top.
  • "An erroneous match has no consequence." It is treated as an underpayment of tax, though without accuracy penalties. Withdrawn by the filing deadline for the year of determination, it is free of income tax and the 10% additional tax.

Frequently asked questions

Is there anything to do in 2026?

The IRS page says no. What is worth doing is avoiding IRA or 401(k) withdrawals before year-end. TrumpIRA.gov, due on January 1, 2027 under the executive order, will list participating institutions.

Is $300 worth contributing?

Yes; there is no minimum. At 50% it earns $150, paid into the account. If the match is under $100 you may elect a refund.

What about U.S. territories?

Bona fide residents must claim through their territory's tax agency; the IRS page says it cannot process those claims.

Reminder: rely on IRS.gov, the final regulations and the Form 8880-A instructions. For immigration status, dual-status years or large withdrawals, consult a CPA or lawyer.
