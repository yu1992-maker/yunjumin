BRP to eVisa: do you need to tell your bank?

Nov 6, 20265 min readYunjumin Forum editors

BRPs have been replaced by eVisas. UK guidance does not require you to notify your bank, but banks may ask for a share code. How to update, what to watch and the key dates.

None of the UK government material we found says you must proactively notify your bank when your BRP is replaced by an eVisa. Your immigration status has not changed, only how you prove it. Banks may, however, ask for an eVisa share code during identity reviews, so set up your UKVI account now and follow the steps below when your bank contacts you.

We found no single official rule on how individual banks handle this; anything below about bank practice is labelled with its source.

What changed and what did not

  • Changed: the Home Office stopped issuing physical biometric residence documents from 31 October 2024. Most BRPs expired on 31 December 2024.
  • Unchanged: the government states that the expiry date applies to the card only, not to your immigration permission. Your status and conditions are unaffected.
  • Proof today: your eVisa is an online record; sign in to your UKVI account to view it and generate share codes for third parties such as banks, landlords and employers.

What the government told banks

The government publishes guidance on residence documents for financial providers (updated 17 October 2025). Key points:

  • BRPs and BRCs have been replaced by eVisas, and information on old cards may be out of date.
  • Acceptable evidence includes an eVisa, a signed passport (with valid UK permission where needed), a Home Office travel document, a Home Office identity document or a frontier worker permit.
  • Banks can verify your status in real time through the official "check someone's immigration status" service using your share code and date of birth, with no physical document.
  • The guidance does not say customers must notify their bank.

The safer reading: there is no uniform official requirement to notify, but banks may request updated documents under their own compliance procedures, and practice varies by bank.

Steps: set up UKVI and get a share code

  1. Create the account via gov.uk/evisa. Holders of expired BRPs with ongoing permission need an account to access their eVisa.
  2. Check your details: personal data, contact details and linked passport. If you have a new passport, update your travel document so the eVisa is linked to it.
  3. Generate a share code in the "view and prove your immigration status" service. For a bank, choose "Prove my immigration status for anything else", not the renting or work options.
  4. Give it to the bank with your date of birth. Codes are time-limited; some sources say 90 days, descriptions vary, so rely on what the page shows when you generate it.
  5. Do not rely on screenshots: organisations have reported that screenshots of your own eVisa are not accepted. Let the recipient check online with the code, or sign in and show it live.

How long can an expired BRP still be used? Sources differ

  • Current GOV.UK BRP page: an expired BRP can be used for 24 months after the expiry date on the card or until 31 December 2026, whichever is earlier.
  • An earlier GOV.UK page: 18 months after expiry.
  • Third-party guides: some say until July 2026.

We follow the current GOV.UK page. The permitted use is narrow: an expired BRP can be used only to access your eVisa and share codes, create a UKVI account or apply to extend your stay, not as everyday ID. As of 6 November 2026, 31 December is under two months away; if you have not set up a UKVI account, do so now.

Reported problems

  • In the Monzo community, a user was refused an account with an expired BRP; the March 2025 thread had no official staff reply, only peer discussion.
  • Users report family members with ILR and expired BRPs being told to use a share code, only for the online check to return "no record found".
  • Reports from the3million and others document eVisa record errors and third parties reluctant to accept share codes.

These are individual experiences and not representative of all banks, but they show why checking your eVisa record early matters.

Checklist

  • Sign in to UKVI and confirm your name, date of birth, photo and permission end date.
  • Keep your expired BRP card; it may still be useful for extension applications.
  • Note your bank's dedicated helpline and ask whether it accepts eVisa share codes.
  • If your eVisa details are wrong, contact UKVI via the gov.uk route to correct them before going to the bank.
  • Never send your share code and date of birth to anyone you cannot identify.

FAQ

1. Will my bank close my account because my BRP expired?

We found no official basis for "BRP expired, account must close". The government stresses that the document expires, not the status. Banks may still require updated records under their policies, and the consequences of not responding depend on their terms.

2. What if the bank does not accept a share code?

The government's guidance for financial providers explicitly mentions online checks with share codes. If staff are unfamiliar, refer them to the guidance or ask for escalation to compliance; failing that, use the bank's complaints process.

3. Which share code type should I use?

For banks and financial services, choose "Prove my immigration status for anything else". A renting or work code may return no result or irrelevant information.
