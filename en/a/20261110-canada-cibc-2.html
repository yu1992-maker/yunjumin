How to Open a CIBC US Dollar Account: Documents, Online Eligibility and Fees

Nov 10, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

The CIBC US$ Personal Account can be opened online but requires Canadian residency; U.S. residents can look at the CIBC Bank USA Smart Account. Documents and fees itemised.

CIBC's US$ Personal Account page has an online "Apply now" option, but you must be a Canadian resident who has reached the age of majority in your province or territory. People living only in the U.S. generally cannot open it; they can look at the CIBC Bank USA Smart Account, which requires an existing eligible CIBC Canadian deposit account. CIBC's site does not list required documents, so we flag below which points are official and which come from third parties.

Can you open it online?

  • Personal US$ account: yes. The page offers an online application and does not describe a branch process.
  • Existing CIBC clients: third-party articles (Finder and others) say you can open a foreign-currency account after signing in to online banking, with details pre-filled. This is a third-party claim; rely on CIBC.
  • New clients: third parties say you can also apply online with complete documents and tax-residency information. CIBC's site does not say so explicitly.
  • Smart Account: the page does not describe application steps or whether it can be done online; it says to contact CIBC or ask at a branch.

Documents

Neither the personal US$ account page nor the Smart Account page lists documents. The following is common practice, not an official CIBC list:

  1. Identification: passport or driver's licence (per third-party reviews).
  2. Proof of address: a utility bill or bank statement (per third-party reviews).
  3. Tax-residency information: usually declared at application, as third-party articles note.
  4. Smart Account additionally: an existing CIBC Canadian account. A 2024 third-party review says a passport or U.S. driver's licence is needed; not officially verified.
  5. Business account: CIBC may ask for incorporation documents, business licence, CRA documents and ID (Wise, citing CIBC).
The surest way to confirm the list is to start the online application, or to call or book a branch appointment.

Smart Account essentials

  • Non-interest-bearing U.S. dollar chequing; all transactions in US dollars.
  • CIBC states no monthly fee and unlimited transactions.
  • Up to 4 non-CIBC / non-CIBC Bank USA ATM withdrawals a month, with up to US$20 in fees reimbursed.
  • Deposits FDIC-insured within applicable limits.
  • Service in English only.
  • Real-time cross-border transfers to and from the personal US$ account.

Steps (personal US$ account)

  1. Confirm you are a Canadian resident with a Canadian address.
  2. Open the CIBC US$ Personal Account page and click "Apply now".
  3. Have ID, proof of address and tax-residency information ready.
  4. Existing clients should sign in to online banking first.
  5. Complete verification as prompted; contact a branch if needed.

Business US$ account

The CIBC U.S. Dollar Current Account costs US$8 a month even with no activity. Self-serve transactions are US$1.00, teller transactions US$1.25. Incoming wires US$15, outgoing US$30–80. Paper statements US$5. From January 16, 2027, some fees, including stop payments, incoming wires and dormant accounts, increase.

FAQ

1. I live in the U.S. Can I open a CIBC Canada US$ account?

CIBC requires Canadian residency and a Canadian address; non-residents generally do not qualify. We found no official exception; ask CIBC directly.

2. Does the personal US$ account have a monthly fee?

CIBC says $0. Wise says $8 unless you keep $1,000. Sources differ; rely on CIBC and the account agreement.

3. Can the US$ account receive U.S. ACH payments?

Wise says the personal US$ account does not support ACH and has no dedicated U.S. routing number. For U.S. domestic payments, consider the Smart Account, which requires an eligible CIBC Canadian account; confirm features with CIBC.
