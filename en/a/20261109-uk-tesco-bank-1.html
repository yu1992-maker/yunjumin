Can Newcomers Get a Tesco Bank Credit Card? No UK Credit History and Income Requirements

Nov 9, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Tesco Bank's Foundation card targets people with no credit history: starting limits of £200–£1,500 and a 29.9% APR. Minimum income figures vary by source (£3,000 or £5,000).

Yes, you can apply, but choose the right card. Tesco Bank says its Foundation credit card is for people who are "new to credit or have a poor credit rating and want to build it," and applicants must be UK residents aged 18 or over. The official site does not state a minimum income, and third-party sources differ: Money to the Masses cites £3,000 and Finder £5,000. Treat the result of the eligibility check as decisive.

Bottom line

  • The card for newcomers: the Foundation Credit Card, positioned by Tesco for beginners and credit building.
  • Less suitable for newcomers: cards such as Clubcard Plus, whose eligibility questions include "annual income of at least £5,000" and a good credit history.
  • Income threshold: the Foundation page gives no figure; the numbers below come from third parties and may be out of date.

Tesco's Clubcard Plus page lists "annual income of at least £5,000" among its eligibility questions. We found no official Tesco figure for the Foundation card, so do not treat either number as a firm threshold. Income is only one factor; the eligibility check also looks at your credit score, outstanding debts and whether you appear on fraud-prevention databases.

What having no UK credit history means

Experian explains that foreign credit scores do not transfer to the UK, so newcomers start from zero. With nothing on your file, lenders have little to go on and rejection is more likely than for someone with a record. Tesco aims the Foundation card at beginners, but that is not a guarantee: approval is "subject to status."

Steps before applying

  1. Have a UK address and a UK bank account. Third-party guides list a permanent UK address and a UK bank or building society account as common requirements; this is not Tesco's own wording.
  2. Register on the electoral roll if eligible. Experian calls this one of the best starting points because it helps lenders verify your name and address. Many newcomers are not eligible, so check on GOV.UK first.
  3. Open a UK bank account and use it normally. If refused, try a basic bank account or one of the newer challenger banks.
  4. Move your phone from pay-as-you-go to a monthly contract. If refused, try again after a few months.
  5. Wait roughly 3–6 months. Experian says an account can need up to about six months of history before there is enough for lenders to assess.
  6. Run the eligibility check before a full application. Tesco's check does not affect your score, whereas a declined full application may lower it.

Using the card once approved

  • Experian suggests keeping spending to about 25% of your limit and paying in full each month to avoid 29.9% interest.
  • Tesco cardholders can view their score through CreditView, provided by TransUnion.
  • Paying monthly gas and electricity bills on time may also be recorded. If you pay rent, after about six months you may be able to add it to your file through the Rental Exchange service.

If refused, or if you do not want to wait

  • HSBC UK: lets credit card applicants bring in foreign credit history from 12 countries (according to Which? and industry reports), worth a look for those with US or Canadian records.
  • Vanquis Credit Builder: aimed at people without UK history, representative APR 42.9% (per Vanquis; subject to change).
  • Other credit-builder cards: usually low limits and high rates, with some sources citing up to about 55%. Proceed with care.

FAQ

Will Tesco always refuse me without UK history?

Not necessarily. The Foundation card is designed for beginners, but income, address and identity checks still apply. There is no guaranteed approval.

What exactly is the minimum income?

Sources differ: £3,000 (Money to the Masses) and £5,000 (Finder, matching the £5,000 on Tesco's Clubcard Plus page). The Foundation page does not say. Use the official eligibility check.

Do I need to have lived in the UK for three years?

The three-year requirement we found applies to Tesco personal loans (per NerdWallet and Money to the Masses reviews); we found no equivalent rule for credit cards. The credit card pages say only "UK resident, 18 or over."
