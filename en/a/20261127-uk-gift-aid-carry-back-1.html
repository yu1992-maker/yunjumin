UK Gift Aid carry-back: give before 31 January and claim against last year

Nov 27, 20265 min readYunjumin Forum editors

The 25% Gift Aid uplift goes to the charity; higher-rate donors can reclaim 20–25% more. File online by 31 January 2027 and this year's gifts can count for 2025/26.

The 25% Gift Aid top-up belongs to the charity. Donors gain personally only if they pay higher or additional rate tax. The under-used lever is carry-back: if you file your 2025/26 Self Assessment return online by 31 January 2027, gifts made between 6 April 2026 and the day you file can be treated as 2025/26 donations, provided the two years' gifts together do not exceed four times the tax you paid in 2025/26.

Sources: GOV.UK “Tax relief when you donate to a charity”, HMRC “Adjusted net income”, GOV.UK pages on the Personal Allowance and the High Income Child Benefit Charge, and GOV.UK “Claiming Gift Aid as a charity or CASC”. Checked on 27 November 2026. Rates below are for England, Wales and Northern Ireland; GOV.UK says Scottish taxpayers can also claim the difference.

1. The mechanics: three slices

According to GOV.UK, a charity or community amateur sports club (CASC) can claim an extra 25p from HMRC for every £1 given under Gift Aid, at no cost to the donor. The 25p represents basic-rate tax: a £100 gift is treated as £125 gross, with £25 already paid by you at 20%.

Donor's bandCharity receives on a £100 giftDonor can reclaimDonor's net cost
Basic rate 20%£125£0£100
Higher rate 40%£125£25 (£125 × 20%)£75
Additional rate 45%£125£31.25 (£125 × 25%)£68.75

The higher-rate figures are GOV.UK's own example; the additional-rate row applies the same method. You claim either on your Self Assessment return or by asking HMRC to change your tax code. Non-filers can claim £5,000 or less by phone; above £5,000 the claim must be in writing, and for £10,000 or more HMRC also wants the dates and recipients.

2. Carry-back rules and timeline

GOV.UK notes that a tax return normally covers only the previous tax year, but for Gift Aid you can also include gifts made in the current year up to the date you file. It is aimed at people who want relief sooner and at people who paid higher-rate tax last year but will not this year.

DateWhat it means
6 April 2026Start of 2026/27; gifts from here can be carried back to 2025/26
31 October 2026Paper deadline for 2025/26 has passed; paper filers can no longer carry back this year
The day you file your 2025/26 returnCut-off for gifts that can be carried back
31 January 2027Online deadline for 2025/26; filing late forfeits carry-back
5 April 2027End of 2026/27

Two limits apply: the return must be filed on time, and total gifts across both years must not exceed four times the Income Tax and Capital Gains Tax you paid in the earlier year. Carried-back gifts drop out of 2026/27.

3. Worked example: one gift, two layers of relief

Suppose 2025/26 employment income of £110,000, no pension or other deductions, and a net gift of £8,000 in January 2027, before filing, carried back to 2025/26.

  1. Gift Aid. The charity receives £10,000 (£8,000 plus £2,000 from HMRC).
  2. Higher-rate relief. Your basic-rate band is extended by the £10,000 gross gift, moving that slice from 40% to 20%: £2,000 back.
  3. Personal Allowance restored. HMRC deducts the grossed-up gift when calculating adjusted net income: £110,000 less £10,000 is £100,000. GOV.UK says the allowance falls by £1 for every £2 above £100,000, so the £5,000 that had been withdrawn returns in full, saving £2,000 at 40%.

Total: £4,000 less tax, a net cost of £4,000, and £10,000 to the charity. On a full computation, Income Tax falls from £33,432 to £29,432. The same logic applies to the High Income Child Benefit Charge, which GOV.UK says claws back 1% of Child Benefit for every £200 above £60,000 and all of it from £80,000. At adjusted net income of £68,000, a net gift of £6,400 (£8,000 gross) brings it back to £60,000 and the charge from 40% to nil.

4. Pro tips

  • Push gifts back into a high-income year. In the year you leave the UK, take a sabbatical, go part-time or retire, you may pay only basic rate. Carrying gifts back to the previous higher-rate year turns zero personal relief into 20% or 25%.
  • Pair with pension contributions. HMRC deducts both grossed-up Gift Aid and grossed-up relief-at-source pension contributions from adjusted net income, the two standard tools for getting under £100,000 or £60,000.
  • Backdate declarations. GOV.UK says one declaration can cover current and future gifts and those made in the last four years. If you gave without ticking Gift Aid, sign one now so the charity can reclaim the 25%.
  • Give from the higher-rate spouse. Personal relief follows the donor's band, so the partner paying 40% should make the gift in their own name.
  • Small cash gifts need no paperwork. Charities can claim a top-up on cash or contactless gifts of £30 or less under the Gift Aid Small Donations Scheme without a declaration, up to £2,000 of top-up per tax year.

5. Common misconceptions

MisconceptionThe rule
Ticking Gift Aid saves me 25%The 25% goes to the charity; only higher and additional-rate taxpayers get personal relief
Anyone can tick the boxGifts must not exceed four times the tax you paid that year; if the charity reclaims more than you paid, HMRC may ask you to pay the difference
Higher-rate relief arrives automaticallyYou must claim it on your return or via a tax-code change
Payroll Giving can be Gift Aided tooPayroll Giving is deducted before Income Tax and cannot also carry Gift Aid; National Insurance is still due
Charity dinner tickets and auction lots countPayments for goods or services, gifts with benefits above the limits and CAF vouchers do not qualify

Newcomers should note the second point: in a first UK tax year with little UK tax paid, a large Gift Aided gift can easily breach the four-times cap.

6. FAQ

I have already filed for 2025/26. Can I carry back a December gift?

No. Only gifts made up to the date you file qualify, so give first and file afterwards.

Can a non-resident tick Gift Aid?

What matters is whether you paid enough UK Income Tax or Capital Gains Tax that year, not where you live. GOV.UK's declaration rules include a specific format for a “non-UK donor”.

Do I report a carried-back gift again for 2026/27?

No. Once carried back to 2025/26 it is excluded from 2026/27.
