Canada's FHSA: Open by 31 December and Your 2027 Room Doubles to $16,000

Nov 22, 20265 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Per the CRA, FHSA room starts in the year you open the account and carries forward up to $8,000. An empty account opened this year unlocks $16,000 in 2027.

FHSA room in Canada starts accruing in the year you open the account, not the year you become eligible. According to the CRA, participation room is $8,000 in the year you open your first FHSA, and up to $8,000 of unused room carries forward. Open an FHSA by 31 December 2026, even with a zero balance, and you can contribute $16,000 in 2027; open it in January 2027 and your 2027 room is $8,000.

Sources: the CRA's FHSA pages (Opening your FHSAs; Participating in your FHSAs; Tax deductions for FHSA contributions; Withdrawals and transfers out of your FHSAs; Closing your FHSAs; Definitions for FHSAs), accessed 22 November 2026. All amounts are in Canadian dollars.

1. Eligibility: three conditions newcomers overlook

Per the CRA's "Opening your FHSAs" page, at the time of opening you must:

  • Age: be at least 18 (19 where that is the age of majority) and no older than 71 on 31 December of the year you open.
  • Residency: be a resident of Canada for tax purposes. Immigration status is not the test.
  • First-time home buyer: not have lived, as your principal residence, in a qualifying home owned by you (or your current spouse or common-law partner) in the current calendar year or the previous four. The CRA explicitly includes homes outside Canada that would qualify if located in Canada.

The last point matters most for newcomers: owner-occupying a home abroad before landing can delay eligibility. You also need a SIN, and you must file Schedule 15 for the year you open your first FHSA even if you contribute nothing.

2. The timeline: why 31 December is a hard deadline

The CRA states that participation room is $8,000 in the opening year; the participation room carryforward is $0 in that year and thereafter the lesser of unused room and $8,000; the lifetime limit is $40,000. No account, no room and no carryforward.

The gap between A and B is a full year of room. Because carryforward is capped at $8,000, skipping two years still leaves a maximum of $16,000 in the third year; the extra year only delays reaching the lifetime limit.

3. Worked example: you choose when to deduct

The CRA says FHSA contributions can be deducted in the year made or any later year, and undeducted amounts carry forward even after the account is closed. That suits newcomers whose income is rising.

Scenario: a newcomer who landed in May 2026 earns about $30,000 in Canada in 2026 and expects $95,000 from 2027.

  1. Open in December 2026 and contribute $8,000, but do not claim the deduction on the 2026 return; report the account and contribution on Schedule 15.
  2. Contribute $8,000 in 2027 and deduct $16,000 on the 2027 return.
  3. Assuming a combined 30% marginal rate in 2027 (rates vary by province; this is an assumption), the deduction is worth about $4,800. Deducting $8,000 in 2026 at an assumed 20% rate would be worth about $1,600.

Investment growth does not use up room, and qualifying withdrawals are tax-free. The CRA also notes there is no minimum holding period before a qualifying withdrawal.

4. Pro tips

  • Open an empty account. Opening starts the clock. A $0 FHSA opened before 31 December, plus Schedule 15 on next spring's return, preserves a year of room.
  • No 60-day grace period. The CRA is explicit: contributions made in the first 60 days of a year cannot be deducted for the prior year. Unlike an RRSP, money must land by 31 December.
  • Move existing RRSP assets. A direct RRSP-to-FHSA transfer on Form RC720 has no immediate tax, but it uses FHSA room, is not deductible and does not restore RRSP room. Useful when cash is short but an RRSP balance exists.
  • Stack with the HBP. The CRA confirms you can make an FHSA qualifying withdrawal and a Home Buyers' Plan withdrawal for the same home, provided each set of conditions is met. Eligible spouses can each withdraw from their own FHSA.
  • No purchase, no loss. The participation period ends on 31 December of the year of the earliest of: the 15th anniversary of opening, turning 71, or the year after the first qualifying withdrawal. Before then, a direct transfer to an RRSP or RRIF on Form RC721 generally affects neither unused RRSP deduction room nor FHSA room, which in effect adds up to $40,000 of extra RRSP space.

5. Common mistakes

  • "Eligibility creates room automatically." Years before the account is opened generate nothing.
  • "Carryforward accumulates indefinitely." It is capped at $8,000, so a single year tops out at $16,000.
  • "A small over-contribution is harmless." The CRA charges 1% per month on the highest excess amount until corrected, and a qualifying withdrawal does not eliminate an excess. Fix it first with Form RC727.
  • "The opening and withdrawal definitions are the same." They are not. For a qualifying withdrawal you need a written agreement to buy or build with completion before 1 October of the following year, Canadian residency from the first qualifying withdrawal until acquisition, intent to occupy within a year, and Form RC725.
  • "Withdrawing and re-depositing into an RRSP is a transfer." An indirect move is a taxable withdrawal and a new RRSP contribution.

FAQ

Can work- or study-permit holders open an FHSA?

The CRA test is Canadian tax residency plus a SIN; residency is determined case by case under CRA rules. The CRA has a separate page on non-residents and FHSAs if you later leave Canada.

Can I hold more than one FHSA?

Yes, but all FHSAs share one participation room.

Where do I see my room?

After a return with Schedule 15 is assessed, in CRA My Account under Savings and pension plans, and on your Notice of Assessment.
