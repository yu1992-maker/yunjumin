Adding a member to a New Mexico LLC: does the IRS need to know, and how filings change

Nov 6, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

There is no IRS form to report a new LLC member, but the tax classification may switch to partnership. EIN, Form 1065, Form 8822-B and state steps explained.

Adding a member to a New Mexico LLC does not involve any form that “notifies the IRS of a new member”; the IRS learns from how you file next. A single-member LLC that adds a member becomes a partnership by default, must file Form 1065 and issue K-1s, and usually does not need Form 8832. An LLC that already had several members keeps filing Form 1065 with updated percentages; if the responsible party changes, file Form 8822-B within 60 days.

Which case are you?

BeforeFederal tax status afterWhat to do
Single-member LLC (disregarded by default)Partnership by defaultSwitch to Form 1065 + K-1; EIN question below
Two or more members (partnership)Still a partnership1065 and K-1s reflect the new split; no new EIN
Elected corporate tax (8832/2553)Still a corporationOutside this article; see a CPA

Basis: the IRS LLC page says a domestic LLC with at least two members is classified as a partnership unless it files Form 8832 to be taxed as a corporation, and a single-member LLC is by default disregarded as separate from its owner for income tax.

Do you have to tell the IRS?

  • Classification change: happens automatically under the default rules; no Form 8832. That form is for electing corporate status or changing classification. Some commercial sites say a single-member LLC adding a member must file 8832; that conflicts with the IRS page. Sources differ; follow the IRS.
  • Responsible party change: the IRS Form 8822-B page says a change of responsible party must be reported within 60 days, for example if the original responsible party leaves or you appoint the new member. The form also updates the business address.
  • Otherwise: there is no dedicated new-member notice; the IRS sees the change on the next Form 1065.

Do you need a new EIN?

This is where sources disagree most.

  • The IRS “Do you need a new EIN?” page says a partnership with an ownership change that does not terminate it needs no new EIN, while a sole proprietor who forms a partnership does.
  • The same page does not directly address a single-member LLC adding a second member.
  • Commercial sites split. Rev. Rul. 99-5 treats this as the original owner contributing assets to a new partnership, so in practice many apply for a new partnership EIN, but we found no explicit IRS rule. Sources differ.

Suggested approach: if the former single-member LLC has employees or files employment taxes, ask a CPA before deciding on the EIN. Multi-member LLCs keep their EIN.

How the returns change

  1. Form 1065: one per year for the partnership, usually due 15 March for calendar-year filers (extensions available).
  2. Schedule K-1: one for each member showing their share.
  3. Members' own returns: K-1 figures flow to each person's Form 1040. The original owner, who probably reported on Schedule C or E, changes how this income is reported.
  4. Splitting the transition year: Rev. Rul. 99-5 distinguishes a new member buying an interest from the owner versus contributing money to the LLC, with different results for basis and holding period. Leave this to a CPA.

Late Form 1065 penalty

The penalty is charged per partner per month, up to 12 months, with part months counted as full. Most secondary sources give $255 for 2026, but others say $235, $245 or $260; sources differ. We could not retrieve the IRS page text, so rely on that year's Form 1065 instructions. More members means a bigger penalty, so do not miss the year a member joins.

New Mexico steps

  • Amend the articles: if your Articles of Organization list members, you generally file Articles of Amendment. Sites quote $50 or $52; sources differ; check the Secretary of State fee schedule.
  • Update the operating agreement: record the new member's contribution, profit share and voting rights. Without a written agreement, state default rules apply, which invites disputes.
  • Annual reports: commercial sites say New Mexico does not require LLC annual or biennial reports; the Secretary of State's biennial report applies to corporations. We could not verify the LLC rule on an official page; confirm with the Secretary of State.
  • Filing method: the Secretary of State accepts business filings online only, via enterprise.sos.nm.gov.

FinCEN BOI report

FinCEN's BOI page says US domestic companies need not report beneficial ownership; a final rule published on 11 August 2026 made the exemption permanent, effective 14 August. Adding a member therefore requires no BOI update.

Checklist

  1. Confirm whether the LLC was single- or multi-member, and whether it elected corporate tax.
  2. Update the operating agreement for contributions and allocations.
  3. Amend the articles if they list members.
  4. Confirm with a CPA whether to change EIN and how to split the transition year.
  5. If the responsible party changed, file Form 8822-B within 60 days.
  6. File Form 1065 and issue K-1s the following year.

FAQ

Must I file Form 8832 after adding a member?

Not necessarily. Per the IRS, an LLC with at least two members is a partnership by default; without a corporate election, no 8832 is needed.

We are already multi-member. Does a new member mean a new EIN?

Per the IRS page, an ownership change that does not terminate the partnership needs no new EIN.

Do we tell FinCEN?

Not at present. FinCEN exempts US domestic companies from BOI reporting.

For reference only; IRS, FinCEN and New Mexico Secretary of State guidance govern. For single-member-to-partnership conversions, asset contributions, basis or foreign members, consult a CPA or lawyer.
