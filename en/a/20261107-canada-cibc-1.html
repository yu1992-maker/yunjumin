CIBC credit card grace period: when interest starts

Nov 7, 20263 min readYunjumin Forum editors

The minimum 21-day interest-free grace period runs from the end of the statement period; if you do not pay in full, interest is charged back to each transaction date. Cash advances have no grace period.

According to CIBC, the interest-free grace period on its credit cards runs from the end of the statement period, lasts at least 21 days and applies only to purchases not placed on an instalment plan. Pay the full statement balance by the due date and purchases incur no interest; otherwise interest is charged from each purchase's transaction date. Cash advances, balance transfers and convenience cheques have no grace period and accrue interest from the day you receive the funds.

Three dates to know

  • Billing cycle: usually about a month; purchases in the cycle appear on the same statement.
  • Statement date: the end of the cycle, when the statement is issued. The grace period starts here.
  • Payment due date: CIBC says it is at least 21 days after the statement date. Use the date printed on your statement.

Federally regulated banks in Canada must provide at least 21 days. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC) states the grace period begins on the last day of the statement period and federally regulated institutions must offer at least 21 days.

Retroactive interest is the most common trap. If you spend $1,000 this month and pay only the minimum after the statement date, interest is calculated not just on the remaining balance but from each original transaction date. A third-party article (money.ca) walks through the same arithmetic, though that is an illustration, not a regulation.

A simplified timeline

The dates below are hypothetical; your statement governs:

  1. Billing cycle October 1 to October 31; you make a purchase on October 5.
  2. The statement is issued October 31 and the grace period begins.
  3. The due date falls on or after November 21 (at least 21 days).
  4. Pay the full balance by the November due date and the purchase incurs no interest.
  5. Pay only the minimum and interest on that purchase runs from October 5.

Practical checklist

  • Find the "Payment due date" and "Statement balance" and pay the statement balance, not the minimum.
  • Set up automatic payment for the full balance. CIBC recommends paying the entire statement balance by the due date each month.
  • Avoid cash advances; interest starts even if you repay the same day.
  • If last month's balance was not paid in full, new purchases may also lose the grace period; see the next section.
  • FCAC warns that missing minimum payments can lead the issuer to raise your rate.

If last month was not paid in full, are new purchases still interest-free?

Sources differ. FCAC says only that if you do not pay the balance by the due date you pay interest until it is repaid. Some third-party articles (such as money.ca) say the grace period is lost once you carry a balance. CIBC's page does not address the point. Check your cardholder agreement or ask CIBC.

Where sources differ

  • Length of grace period: official sources agree on at least 21 days. Some third parties cite 25 or 26 days at certain banks; we found no such figure on CIBC's pages, so rely on your statement.
  • CIBC rate sheet: we could not extract text from CIBC's rates-and-fees PDF, so we do not quote specific APRs. Check your card's rate on CIBC's site.

FAQ

Does paying on the due date count as on time?

CIBC says to pay by the due date. To be safe, send payment two to three business days early to allow for processing. Check CIBC's posting cut-off times.

If I buy something the day before the statement date, do I still get 21 days?

Officially the grace period runs from the end of the statement period, so that purchase gets at least 21 days after the statement date like any other. Posting dates can lag purchase dates, so check the transaction list on your statement.

If I repay a cash advance immediately, do I pay interest?

Yes. CIBC says cash advances accrue interest from the moment you receive the funds, with no interest-free period, so even a quick repayment incurs interest, and fees may apply under your card's terms.

Note: rely on CIBC's website, FCAC and your own cardholder agreement. Terms vary by card; for complex situations contact CIBC or a licensed financial or legal professional in Canada.
