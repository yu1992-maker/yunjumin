Stacking FDIC Coverage from $250,000 to $2,500,000: The Ownership Categories

Nov 25, 20265 min readYunjumin Forum editors

At a single bank, splitting cash across single, joint, IRA and trust categories multiplies coverage; fintech deposits face three pass-through tests.

FDIC's $250,000 is not one limit per person per bank. It applies per depositor, per separately chartered insured bank, per ownership category. A married couple using individual, joint, IRA and payable-on-death accounts at one bank can be insured for up to $2,500,000 under the FDIC's own formulas. Conversely, accounts in the same category are added together, and money held through a fintech app is covered only if every pass-through condition is met.

Sources: FDIC, Your Insured Deposits (updated May 14, 2024); Deposit Insurance at a Glance (updated April 1, 2024); Financial Products That Are Insured; and the FDIC's Electronic Deposit Insurance Estimator (EDIE). Checked November 25, 2026.

1. How the limit works: the ownership categories

The FDIC states that coverage is per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category. Branches of the same bank are not insured separately; separately chartered banks are. The main categories:

CategoryTypical accountsCoverageEasy to miss
SingleChecking, savings, money market and CDs in one name, including a sole proprietor's DBA account$250,000 per owner in totalSole proprietorship accounts are added to personal accounts
Certain retirementTraditional and Roth IRAs, self-directed 401(k), 457 plans$250,000 per owner in totalCovers bank deposits only, not stocks or funds inside the IRA
JointTwo or more owners, no beneficiaries$250,000 per co-ownerAll owners must be living people with equal withdrawal rights who signed the signature card
TrustPOD/ITF, living trusts, irrevocable trustsOwners × eligible beneficiaries × $250,000, capped at $1,250,000 per ownerThe same beneficiary counts once per bank
Corporation, partnership, unincorporated associationLLC and company accounts$250,000 per entityMust be engaged in an independent activity
Employee benefit planNon-self-directed retirement plans$250,000 per participant's non-contingent interestPasses through by participant share
GovernmentPublic funds$250,000 per official custodianMore available under specific conditions

The FDIC is explicit that a depositor need not be a U.S. citizen or resident. Newcomers on work or student visas, or without an SSN, are covered on the same terms.

2. Worked example: a couple at one bank

A married couple with two children wants to keep about $2,500,000 at one insured bank. Under the FDIC's published rules:

AccountBalanceInsured
Husband, single account$250,000$250,000
Wife, single account$250,000$250,000
Joint account, no beneficiaries$500,000$500,000 ($250,000 each)
Husband, IRA deposit$250,000$250,000
Wife, IRA deposit$250,000$250,000
Husband, POD naming both children$500,000$500,000 (1 × 2 × $250,000)
Wife, POD naming both children$500,000$500,000
Total$2,500,000$2,500,000

Name five eligible beneficiaries (for example, adding parents or an IRS-recognized charity) and each owner's trust coverage rises to $1,250,000, $2,500,000 for the couple, for total coverage of $4,000,000. The FDIC's Example 4 shows an owner naming six beneficiaries across three trust accounts and still capped at $1,250,000, because five is the maximum that counts.

The FDIC's Example 3 shows the joint-account trap. Mary and John share two accounts and also hold a $270,000 CD with Robert. Each co-owner's share is split equally and added across accounts; Mary's comes to $330,000, leaving $80,000 uninsured.

3. Pro tips

  • Name five beneficiaries. Since April 1, 2024, revocable and irrevocable trusts share one trust category, insured at $250,000 per eligible beneficiary up to $1,250,000 per owner regardless of how the money is allocated among them. For a POD account, beneficiaries only need to appear in the bank's records; the title no longer needs to say POD.
  • Back into a plan's ceiling. The FDIC's Example 6 divides $250,000 by the largest participant's share. A clinic plan whose largest share is 35% can hold $714,285 at one bank fully insured. Small-business owners choosing a bank for a team plan can use the same arithmetic.
  • An HSA can sit in the trust category. According to the FDIC, an individual HSA with beneficiaries named in the agreement or bank records is insured as a trust account; without them it joins the single category. If you hold large savings and an HSA at the same bank, naming beneficiaries keeps them apart.
  • Check with EDIE first. The FDIC's estimator works from actual account titles and is more reliable than mental math; for complex structures, call 1-877-275-3342.

4. Common mistakes

  • "Different branches mean separate coverage." The FDIC says branches of one insured bank are not insured separately. The test is the charter, which you can check in the FDIC's BankFind.
  • "If the app says FDIC insured, I am safe." Funds placed through a third party pass through to you only if three conditions hold: the bank's records disclose the agency or custodial relationship; your identity and interest can be determined from the bank's or third party's records; and you actually own the funds. The FDIC decides this when a bank fails, and it notes that deposit insurance protects against the failure of an insured bank, not against theft or fraud.
  • "A brokered CD is insured separately." It passes through and is added to your other deposits in the same category at that bank. The FDIC's example: a personal checking account plus a broker-purchased CD at the same bank share one $250,000 single-account limit.
  • "There is a grace period if a beneficiary dies." There is a six-month grace period when an owner dies, but none when a beneficiary dies. The FDIC's example: a mother's $500,000 POD naming two children drops to $250,000 of coverage immediately if one child dies.
  • "Coverage merges the moment two banks merge." Deposits of the acquired bank stay separately insured for at least six months; CDs until their first maturity after that period, unless renewed on different terms or amounts, in which case only until the six months end.
  • "Everything a bank sells is insured." Stocks, bonds, mutual funds, crypto assets, annuities, life insurance, municipal securities and safe deposit box contents are not. Treasury securities are not FDIC-insured either, though they are backed by the full faith and credit of the U.S. government.

FAQ

Is an account opened with an ITIN or passport insured?

Yes. The FDIC says any person or entity can be insured at an insured bank, with no citizenship or residency requirement.

Which category applies if a joint account names POD beneficiaries?

The trust category: each owner is insured at $250,000 times the number of eligible beneficiaries. Once beneficiaries are named, the account is no longer in the joint category.

Are LLC and personal accounts combined?

A properly formed LLC or corporation engaged in an independent activity has its own $250,000. A sole proprietor's DBA account is added to the owner's single accounts.

Reminder: the FDIC website is authoritative; for estate planning and trust terms, consult an attorney or CPA.
