Will a UK Bank Close Your Account When a Visa or eVisa Expires?

Nov 7, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Under the Immigration Act 2014 banks check status and must act once notified. Who is affected, and how to fix errors.

An expired visa or eVisa does not by itself make a bank close your account at once. But if you remain in the UK without leave, you may be treated as a "disqualified person". Once notified by the Home Office, the bank must close or restrict the account. That is a legal duty, not a commercial choice. People who have already left the UK face a different position, covered below.

The legal basis

According to official guidance, the Immigration Act 2014 bars banks and building societies from opening current accounts for people in the UK without leave, and requires status checks and notification to the Home Office. A disqualified person meets all three of these tests:

  • is in the UK;
  • requires leave to be in the UK but does not have it;
  • is someone the Secretary of State considers should be disqualified.

A 2016 amendment extended checks to existing accounts. The guidance says all existing current accounts must be checked at least once a quarter; accounts operated for trade, business or profession are outside the periodic checks.

What the bank does

After notification, the guidance says the bank must:

  1. close the account "as soon as reasonably practicable", or restrict the person's use of it;
  2. tell the account operator why, where lawful;
  3. it may delay closure for a "reasonable period" to deal with debts and third-party interests.

Joint accounts differ. If the other holder is not disqualified, or you are only a signatory or beneficiary, the bank may restrict your access instead of closing the account.

The gov.uk pages we found give no number of days' notice before closure. Figures circulating online do not match the official documents and should not be relied on.

Freezing orders

The Home Office may also ask a court to freeze a disqualified person's current accounts. If granted, the bank must prevent withdrawals and payments by anyone until the order is lifted. The order may allow for reasonable living costs, legal fees, or withdrawals by a person who is not disqualified.

eVisa and share codes

Status is now proved electronically through an eVisa. You need a UKVI account and use "View and Prove" to generate a share code. Several third-party sources say banks take an S code and enter it with your date of birth on gov.uk. Sources differ on validity (30 or 90 days); follow the date shown when you generate it.

What to do, in order

  1. Check your status. Log in to your UKVI account and confirm the expiry date.
  2. Apply to extend or switch before expiry. The banking pages do not state the legal effect, such as whether your stay remains lawful; ask an immigration lawyer or regulated adviser.
  3. Do not wait for a bank letter. If leaving, move funds in good time to an account you can keep.
  4. Keep documents: decision letters, application receipts, bank letters.
  5. If departing, the Home Office offers help with voluntary return at gov.uk/return-home-voluntarily.

If the status is wrong

If the Home Office has given the bank incorrect information, use the official route to ask it to check your status:

  • Use the online form, or write to the Complaints Allocation (IC) team at the Customer Correspondence Hub in Croydon. Send copies, not originals.
  • The stated response time is seven working days, longer if documents are missing or more checks are needed.
  • Include the bank's closure or refusal letter, any information sheet, reference numbers and decision letters. Documents must be in English or Welsh.
  • A representative needs your signed authority dated within 12 months.
  • Errors in eVisa personal details use a separate reporting route.

FAQ

If my visa expired yesterday, is the account closed tomorrow?

The official documents do not say so. The duty arises on notification, and closure may be reasonably delayed to settle debts. No fixed notice period is given, so do not treat this as a grace period.

I am back in the US or Canada. What happens?

The definition requires being in the UK. We found no uniform policy on overseas customers with expired documents; contact the bank.

Are savings accounts and credit cards affected?

The official rules concern current accounts. For other products, ask each bank.

Note: gov.uk and FCA pages prevail and rules change. For extensions, status disputes or frozen accounts, consult a regulated immigration adviser or lawyer.
