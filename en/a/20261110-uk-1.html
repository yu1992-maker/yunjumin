UK Credit Card Minimum Payments: How They Are Calculated and What Paying Only the Minimum Costs

Nov 10, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Barclaycard's minimum-payment formula as an example, published cases of what paying only the minimum costs, and the FCA's persistent debt rules.

there is no single UK formula for the minimum payment; each issuer sets its own, usually the highest of a percentage of the balance plus interest, or a fixed floor. Pay only the minimum and the principal shrinks very slowly while interest can exceed the original debt. Which? cites £3,000 at 19% APR taking about 27 years and 7 months to clear, with £4,192 in interest.

How the minimum is set

Terms vary, but most use "the highest of" several figures. Barclaycard's formula, as reported by MoneySavingExpert, is a useful reference:

PeriodMost Barclaycards (Avios, Platinum, Rewards)Forward card
Before 22 July 2024Highest of 3.75% of balance; 2.5% + interest; £5Highest of 4.25% of balance; 3% + interest; £5
From 22 July 2024All Barclaycards: highest of 1% of balance; 1% + interest; £5
  • The minimum usually covers the month's interest and fees plus a sliver of principal.
  • Other issuers use different percentages and floors. Some calculator sites put the usual range at 1%–3% plus interest, or a fixed floor such as £25; these are unofficial and we could not verify a specific rule in the FCA Handbook.
  • Only your card's terms and statement tell you which formula applies.

A lower minimum eases the monthly burden but raises the total cost sharply. In the Barclaycard case, cutting the minimum from 3.75% to 1% stretched repayment from 9 years 8 months to 19 years 3 months and interest from £699 to £1,655.

Why it gets slower

  1. The minimum is a percentage of the balance, so it falls as the balance falls.
  2. Interest accrues on the remaining principal, while the principal portion of each payment grows slowly.
  3. Late in the term you are mostly paying interest.

FCA research found customers in this position paid about £2.50 in interest for every £1 of principal repaid (as reported by Which?).

The FCA's persistent debt rules

The FCA finalised its rules in PS18/4, effective 1 March 2018, with full compliance required by 1 September 2018:

  • Definition: over 18 months you paid more in interest and fees than in principal.
  • 18 months: the issuer must contact you, explain the benefit of paying more and point you to debt help.
  • 27 months: a further prompt if you are likely to still be in persistent debt at 36 months.
  • 36 months: the issuer must offer a way to repay faster and show forbearance, which may include reducing or waiving interest or fees. Sources word this step slightly differently.
  • Customers in persistent debt for over 12 months will not be offered credit limit increases (per Which?).

One source (a FICO blog) says the 18-month clock resets when the balance falls below £200; we could not verify this in official text.

Use a fixed payment instead

  1. On your latest statement, find the minimum payment, the APR and the repayment-time warning.
  2. Set a fixed monthly amount: the statement's "pay off in X" figure or the most you can afford.
  3. Set up a Direct Debit; if you can choose a fixed amount, do not choose "minimum".
  4. Consider a 0% balance transfer, after checking the transfer fee and the rate after the promotion.
  5. Pay new spending in full separately so you are not rolling interest on top of old debt.

For newcomers

  • First cards for people with thin UK credit files tend to have low limits, which makes minimum-only payment tempting. Set a fixed payment from the start.
  • Paying the minimum on time is not recorded as late, but a high balance uses up available credit and can affect future applications; credit reference agencies describe the scoring effect differently.
  • If repayments are becoming unaffordable, contact your issuer, or a free service such as MoneyHelper.

FAQ

Is the minimum payment the same across the UK?

No. Each issuer sets its own formula. For Barclaycard from 22 July 2024 it is the highest of 1% of the balance, 1% plus interest, or £5.

Is paying only the minimum counted as late?

No, provided it is on time, but interest continues and total interest can be several times the original balance.

Will the bank warn me?

Yes. Under FCA rules the issuer contacts you at 18 months and must offer a faster repayment option at 36 months. Do not wait that long.
