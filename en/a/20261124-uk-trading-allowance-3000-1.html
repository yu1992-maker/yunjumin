UK Trading Allowance Rises to £3,000, but Only From April 6, 2027

Nov 24, 20265 min readYunjumin Forum editors

This tax year still runs on £1,000, and one payment from an employer can void the allowance for the whole year.

The UK's 2026 Budget raises the trading allowance from £1,000 to £3,000 and lifts the Self Assessment reporting threshold for trading income to match. Both take effect on April 6, 2027. The current tax year, from April 6, 2026 to April 5, 2027, still runs on £1,000, and anyone above it must register by October 5, 2027. One exclusion is rarely mentioned: if any of your side income comes from your employer, or your spouse's employer, the whole allowance is lost for that year.

Sources are HMRC's policy paper of October 28, 2026, the Budget overview of tax legislation (OOTLAR) and its Annex A, Part 6A Chapter 1 of the Income Tax (Trading and Other Income) Act 2005 (ITTOIA), and GOV.UK guidance on visas, National Insurance and digital platforms, with reference to ACCA. Checked November 24, 2026. The new figure requires legislation in Finance Bill 2026-27; until enacted it is announced policy.

HMRC estimates that up to 1.4 million people could benefit: about 400,000 would see an average tax cut of £380, and about 100,000 would no longer need to report.

2. Gross income, one allowance per person

The test is gross income, before platform commission, materials or postage. Under section 783AC ITTOIA, relevant income is the sum of receipts from all your sole trades plus miscellaneous income. Tutoring, delivery work, reselling and advertising revenue are added together and share a single allowance. Partnership trading income does not qualify.

At or below the allowance, full relief applies and profit or loss is treated as nil. Above it, you may elect for partial relief: deduct the allowance from gross income and give up all actual expenses. Under sections 783AI and 783AK, partial relief applies to every relevant trade you carry on, with the allowance split between them as you choose. You cannot use the allowance in one business and deduct expenses in another.

The rule from 2027-28 fits in a sentence: use the allowance if actual costs are below £3,000, deduct the costs if they are above. Class 4 National Insurance is ignored because it begins only above £12,570 of trading profit.

4. Three rules that void the whole allowance

  • Payments from an employer (section 783AO). If relevant income includes a payment by your employer, or the employer of your spouse or civil partner, no relief is given for that tax year. The statute does not merely strip out that payment. One paid translation for your own company can cost you relief on a year of tutoring.
  • Payments from your own company (section 783AQ). The same result follows where the payer is a close company in which you or an associate participate.
  • Payments from a partnership (section 783AP). Likewise where you, or someone connected to you, is a partner.

5. Pro tips

  1. Receipts that straddle the year. Cash basis is the standard method for sole traders, according to GOV.UK, and records income when received. An invoice issued in March 2027 and paid in the ordinary course on April 20 falls in 2027-28. The method must be consistent and the payment date genuine.
  2. Elect out of full relief when you have a loss. Full relief sets losses to nil. Under section 783AL you may elect out by the first anniversary of the normal filing date: for 2026-27, the date is January 31, 2028, so the election runs to January 31, 2029.
  3. Keep access to Class 2. GOV.UK allows voluntary Class 2 for the self-employed with gross income of £1,000 or less, or above £1,000 with profits under £7,105. In 2026-27 Class 2 costs £3.65 a week and Class 3 £18.40, roughly £190 and £957 over 52 weeks. To pay Class 2, or to claim Maternity Allowance or Tax-Free Childcare on self-employed income, you must still register for Self Assessment.
  4. Each spouse has an allowance. Two people genuinely trading on their own account could cover £6,000 in 2027-28. Booking one person's income under the other's name does not work.
  5. Other calculations follow. GOV.UK says the lower income counts for the High Income Child Benefit Charge and student loan repayments. Universal Credit is unaffected.

6. Common misconceptions

  • "The platform reported me, so I owe tax." GOV.UK says otherwise. From April 2027 a seller between about £1,700 and £3,000 may be reported and still have nothing to file. Platforms report by calendar year while tax runs April to April, so keep your own records.
  • "Selling old belongings uses up the allowance." Selling personal possessions is generally not trading; a single item sold above £6,000 may raise capital gains tax.
  • "Below £3,000 I need no records." You must still keep income records.
  • "Tax-free means permitted." Immigration rules are separate. A Student visa bars self-employment; a Graduate visa allows it; a Skilled Worker may do up to 20 hours a week in another job or their own business, subject to occupation conditions, with invoicing and other administration counted.

FAQ

I will gross £2,000 in 2026-27. Can I wait for the new rule?

No. It is not retrospective. Above £1,000 you must register by October 5, 2027 and file online by January 31, 2028.

If I gross £3,200 in 2027-28, is it all taxed?

With partial relief only £200 is taxed, £40 at 20%, but a return is needed. The election has the same one-year limit (section 783AM).

Can I use the £3,000 against room rent?

No. Rent is property income, covered by the £1,000 property allowance or, where eligible, the £7,500 Rent a Room Scheme.

GOV.UK and the enacted law prevail. For complex cases, consult an accountant or solicitor.
