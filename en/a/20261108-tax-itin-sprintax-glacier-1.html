Tax software for nonresidents and ITIN filers: Sprintax, Glacier, TurboTax

Nov 8, 20263 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Comparing price, eligibility and limits for Form 1040-NR and ITIN filers.

In short: F-1 and J-1 students should first use Glacier Tax Prep (GTP) or Sprintax through their school if offered. For nonresidents without a school route, Sprintax is the simplest choice, with federal Form 1040-NR listed from about $54.95. Several third-party sources say general software such as TurboTax and H&R Block does not support Form 1040-NR and so does not suit nonresidents. Prices and features were checked in November 2026 and may change.

Know which form you need

  • Form 1040-NR: for nonresident aliens with U.S.-source income or a U.S. trade or business. The IRS page shows the 2025 form as current, with a correction to the instructions posted February 20, 2026.
  • Form 8843: the statement that F and J visitors file even without U.S. income. Several universities say there is no standalone e-filing for it, so it is usually printed and mailed.
  • ITIN: used to file without an SSN. Sprintax's blog says an ITIN can be entered on Form 1040-NR. We found no separate ITIN charge.
  • Dual-status: Thomson Reuters' help page says the IRS does not allow e-filing of dual-status returns, which must be mailed.
The IRS page itself does not set out deadlines, e-filing or ITIN details. See the official Form 1040-NR instructions.

Sprintax versus Glacier

  • Who pays: GTP runs on school licenses and is free if yours has one. Sprintax works with schools and also charges individuals directly.
  • Coverage: GTP is normally for people inside the institution. Others, such as short-term visitors or graduates, usually have Sprintax or a CPA.
  • Features: some guides consider the federal functions similar, with the choice following the school or employer's recommendation. Columbia says Sprintax produces Form 8843 with no income and Form 1040-NR with income.
  • State returns: Sprintax charges separately; GTP depends on the license.

How to choose

  1. Confirm status. Are you a nonresident under the substantial presence test? If unsure, ask the international office or a CPA.
  2. Ask the school. Check the international student office site for a free GTP or Sprintax code.
  3. No school route. Use Sprintax, and look at the final total: federal, each state and Form 8843.
  4. Complex cases. For dual status, a spouse or dependents with ITINs, or amended returns, go to a CPA.

Pitfalls

  • Do not use TurboTax for Form 1040-NR. Several third parties say it is unsupported; the result may be rejection or an incorrect return.
  • State e-filing. eFile.com lists states that do not allow e-filing of state returns with a 1040-NR, including Alabama, DC, Hawaii, Nebraska and Oklahoma.
  • Conflicting prices. One review site gives $29 for amendments and $99 for CPA review, which does not match Sprintax's own $79.95. Use the official page.
  • Form 1040-NR-EZ. Thomson Reuters says the IRS does not allow e-filing of 1040NR-EZ. Check IRS.gov for whether the form is still in use.

Frequently asked questions

I have an ITIN and no SSN. Can I use Sprintax?

Sprintax's blog says an ITIN can be entered on Form 1040-NR. We found nothing on extra fees, so read the page before paying.

I had no U.S. income. Must I file?

People in F or J status usually still file Form 8843. Sprintax and GTP can both produce it, or you can complete and mail it by hand.

Can I make do with TurboTax?

Third parties say it supports Forms 1040 and 1040-SR and state returns, but not 1040-NR or 8843. We found no Intuit page confirming this, so check with Intuit first.
