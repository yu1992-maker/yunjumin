Renting with an eVisa: What to Give a UK Landlord for a Right to Rent Check

Nov 10, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

With an eVisa you give the landlord an R-prefixed share code plus your date of birth. How to generate it, how the check works and what to do if it fails.

with an eVisa you do not show the landlord a physical document. Log in to your UKVI account, generate a share code for renting, and send it with your date of birth; the landlord checks it on GOV.UK. The code is valid for 90 days and can be reused in that period. If you hold only an eVisa, the online check is the only route.

What the landlord needs

  • A right to rent share code (starts with R; codes starting with W or S are for work and other purposes and cannot be used)
  • Your date of birth, which must accompany the code

The landlord does not need your eVisa screen or your original passport. Official guidance says the landlord must enter the details on the government site; a screenshot or printout is not enough.

Step 1: generate the share code

  1. Have your passport, national ID card, biometric residence card (valid or expired) or expired BRP, or your UKVI customer number ready.
  2. Make sure you can receive verification at the phone number or email linked to your UKVI account.
  3. Log in to view your eVisa (view-immigration-status.service.gov.uk/status), choose to prove your right to rent, and generate the code.
  4. The service shows what the landlord will see; confirm, then send the code and your date of birth.

If your name or nationality has changed, or your photo no longer looks like you, update your UKVI account first; otherwise the photo check may stall.

Step 2: how the landlord checks

StepLandlord action
1Enter the code and date of birth in the Home Office online service on GOV.UK
2Match the photo to you (in person, by video call, or through a certified identity service provider)
3Save a PDF or HTML copy of the result showing the photo and check date
4Keep it for at least one year after the tenancy ends

If your permission is time-limited, the landlord will note a follow-up date and re-check before it, so you will need to send a fresh code. For indefinite leave to remain, guidance says a follow-up check is generally not needed.

Share code details

  • Validity: 90 days, unlimited use within that period; generate a new one after it expires.
  • Purpose: renting, work and travel use different codes.
  • Physical documents: eVisa-only holders must use the online check. An expired BRP is not accepted for a manual check.
  • Visa vignettes: from 1 July 2026 the UK no longer issues vignettes as evidence of immigration permission. A landlord may manually check a still-valid vignette only for someone who has not created a UKVI account, and a vignette cannot generate a share code.

If the online check does not work

If you have a pending application, review or appeal, or cannot access your eVisa or it is wrong, the landlord can ask the Home Office Landlord Checking Service:

  • The stated response time is two working days; the landlord should wait before letting you move in.
  • A positive notice gives the landlord a 12-month statutory excuse.
  • A negative notice means no statutory excuse if they proceed.
  • If no answer comes within two working days, an automatic response lets the landlord proceed, also with 12 months' protection.

Errors in the eVisa itself are fixed through UKVI's reporting channel, not the Landlord Checking Service. GOV.UK also says that if you cannot access your eVisa, you may use original immigration documents. Landlord helpline: 0300 790 6268 (from a third-party summary of GOV.UK; verify before calling).

Why landlords are cautious

Penalties apply per disqualified occupant:

TypeFirst breachRepeat within three years
Tenant£10,000£20,000
Lodger£5,000£10,000

A first breach paid within 21 days is reduced by 30%, to £7,000 (tenant) or £3,500 (lodger). A landlord who insists on checking the code personally is protecting themselves, not making things difficult.

Checklist

  • Tell the landlord at viewing stage that you use an eVisa and will send an R code.
  • Log in early to make sure your account works and your photo is current.
  • Send your date of birth with the code and mention the 90-day validity.
  • If your status is time-limited, send a new code before it expires.
  • We found no official statement on how many days before move-in the check must be done; ask the landlord or agent.

FAQ

Can the landlord demand my original passport?

Guidance says landlords should not refuse you because you offer acceptable original documents instead of a share code, so either route is open, except that eVisa-only holders must use the online check.

Does the code expire after one use?

No. GOV.UK says it can be reused for 90 days.

The code I generated starts with W.

Only a right-to-rent code (starting with R) works. Generate a new one and select renting.
