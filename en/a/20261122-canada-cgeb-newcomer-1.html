Canada's CGEB for Newcomers: File RC151 to Claim Up to $679 a Year

Nov 22, 20265 min readYunjumin Forum editors

The GST/HST credit became the CGEB in July 2026 and rose 25%. Newcomers must file RC151 in their first year, plus RC66 if they have children.

Canada's GST/HST credit became the Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit (CGEB) in July 2026. Eligibility and the formula are unchanged, but amounts rose 25%: for July 2026 to June 2027 the maximum is $679 for a single person, $890 for a couple and $234 per child. Long-time residents receive it automatically by filing a return. Newcomers must file Form RC151 for their first year, and those with children must also mail RC66 and RC66SCH. If they skip this step, the first year's payments may be missed.

1. What the CGEB pays in 2026–27

The CRA says the CGEB replaced the GST/HST credit in July 2026 and that eligibility, payment calculation and structure stay the same. The 25% increase applies for five years, 2026 to 2031. Payments are tax-free and are not reported on your return.

Two one-off payments are easily confused. The top-up paid from 5 June 2026 equalled 50% of the 2025–26 annual credit and went automatically to anyone who received the GST credit in January 2026. Budget 2026 (page dated 5 November 2026) proposes a further supplement in early 2027 of up to $223 per adult, $117 per child and $117 for the single supplement. As of this writing it is still a budget proposal.

RC4210's definition of family net income deserves a careful read: for any part of the year when you or your spouse were not resident, family net income includes income from all sources inside and outside Canada, computed under Canadian rules. Pre-arrival salary abroad cannot be left off. Under-reporting leads to overpayments, which the CRA recovers from future benefits and refunds.

3. Worked examples

Case one: single, no children, resident all of 2025, net income $30,000. That is below $46,432, so there is no reduction, and the single supplement is fully phased in at $11,564 + $234 ÷ 2% = $23,264. Entitlement for 2026–27: $679, about $169.75 a quarter.

Case two: single, no children, 2025 net income $56,432. That is $10,000 over the threshold, so the benefit falls by $500 to $179. At $44.75 a quarter it is below $50, so the CRA pays the full amount in July 2026.

Case three: CCB. The CRA calculation page (still showing July 2025 to June 2026 figures at the time of checking) gives this example: one child under six, adjusted family net income $45,000. The $7,513 above $37,487 is reduced at 7%, or $525.91, leaving $7,471.09 a year, about $622.59 a month. The same family also receives $234 per child through the CGEB. Amounts are indexed each year; rely on the notice in your CRA account.

4. Pro tips

  • Families should apply through RC66. The CRA says information on RC66 is also used to assess CGEB and related provincial programs, with no separate provincial application.
  • Provincial credits ride along. RC4210 lists, for example, the Ontario sales tax credit of up to $378 per adult and per child, and the New Brunswick HST credit of $300 per adult and $100 per child, all paid with the quarterly CGEB to those who file on time.
  • Same-sex parents need no extra letter. The CCB presumes the female parent in the home is primarily responsible; another parent claiming needs her signed letter, but the CRA states no letter is required when both parents are the same sex.
  • Late filers can catch up, with paperwork. Missed CGEB amounts are paid retroactively after assessment. For CCB claims reaching back more than 11 months, the CRA asks for status documents, at least three kinds of proof of residence and at least three kinds of proof of care.
  • Open a CRA account to see the next payment, the full schedule and a statement of account, and to sign up for reminder emails about a week before each payment.

5. Common misconceptions

  • "The GST credit was abolished." It was renamed; eligibility and structure are unchanged, and amounts are 25% higher for 2026–2031.
  • "No income, no need to file." The CRA asks everyone to file every year, even with no income. Filing after 30 April can interrupt quarterly payments.
  • "A work-permit holder qualifies for CCB on arrival." Temporary residents must have lived in Canada for the previous 18 months and hold a valid permit in the 19th month that does not say "does not confer status". Holders of a Refugee Protection Claimant Document are not eligible.
  • "Foreign income before landing does not count." RC4210 includes worldwide income for any non-resident part of the year.
  • "Under-19s cannot receive it." They can if they have a spouse or partner or are a parent living with their child; others start on the first payment date after turning 19, provided last year's return is filed.

FAQ

I landed in September 2026. What do I get this year?

Eligibility is tested at the end of the month before, and the start of the month of, each payment, so you qualify only for payments after you became resident. File RC151 and the CRA will determine the amount.

Do both spouses file RC151?

No. One application per household is enough, but both spouses must file their own returns afterwards.

Is the CGEB taxable?

No. CGEB and CCB are tax-free and not reported, but the CRA can offset them against tax or other government debts.
