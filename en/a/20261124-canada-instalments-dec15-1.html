Canada's December 15 tax instalment: the $3,000 threshold and 7% interest

Nov 24, 20266 min readYunjumin Forum editors

The reminder is only one of three calculations. First-year newcomers are exempt, and year-end withholding can bring you under the threshold.

The last 2026 tax instalment for individuals in Canada is due on December 15. You must pay by instalments only if your 2026 net tax owing exceeds $3,000 ($1,800 in Quebec) and it also exceeded that threshold in 2025 or 2024. A newcomer in the first year, with no Canadian net tax owing in the two prior years, has no instalment obligation whatever the bill. The figure on a CRA reminder is only one of three permitted calculations: if your income has fallen, the current-year option may bring the last payment to $0. Missed amounts currently accrue interest at 7%, compounded daily.

This article relies on the CRA pages on required tax instalments for individuals (updated January 20, 2026), sections 156, 156.1, 161(4.01) and 163.1 of the Income Tax Act, the CRA's quarterly prescribed rates for 2026 and the Budget 2026 tax measures. Checked on November 24, 2026. Amounts are in Canadian dollars.

1. Who must pay: two tests, both required

Employers withhold tax on wages, so most employees are unaffected. The rule matters for self-employment, rent, investments, capital gains, several jobs and some pensions. Under section 156.1, net tax owing is broadly federal plus provincial tax less tax withheld during the year.

Your province is where you live on December 31. Quebec's threshold is lower because the federal government does not collect Quebec's provincial income tax, as Budget 2026 also explains.

The reminder itself matters. The February INNS1 covers March and June; the August INNS1 covers September and December. If you receive one but your 2026 net tax owing stays at or below the threshold, no payment is due. Instalment interest arises when you are required to pay, received a reminder, and paid late or too little.

2. Three calculations: $12,000 or $6,000 for the same person

Section 156(1) allows three methods. Take a self-employed person with net tax owing of $4,000 for 2024, $12,000 for 2025 and an estimated $6,000 for 2026. CPP contributions and voluntary EI premiums are left out for simplicity but must be added in practice.

Due dateNo-calculation (reminder)Prior-year (2025)Current-year (2026 estimate)
March 15$1,000$3,000$1,500
June 15$1,000$3,000$1,500
September 15$5,000$3,000$1,500
December 15$5,000$3,000$1,500
Total$12,000$12,000$6,000

Under the no-calculation option the first two payments are each a quarter of 2024, and the last two are each half of the 2025 total less half of 2024, which is why the second half can jump. Someone who followed the reminder for three payments has paid $7,000, more than the $6,000 estimate, and may skip December 15.

The risk is estimating too low. If the year ends at $9,000, the $2,000 shortfall on December 15 costs about $53 in interest to April 30, 2027 at 7% compounded daily, before any credit interest on earlier overpayments. The CRA notes that the prior-year and current-year options still attract interest if the estimate is too low. Section 161(4.01), however, measures interest against whichever method gives the least amount, including the reminder, so choosing the wrong method is not itself penalised.

3. The cost of missing: the $25 and $1,000 lines

The prescribed rate on overdue tax was 7% in all four quarters of 2026, against 5% on overpayments by individuals. Instalment interest compounds daily from each due date to the balance-due date, April 30, 2027, and is charged only if it exceeds $25. The penalty applies only when instalment interest exceeds $1,000: take actual interest, subtract the greater of $1,000 and 25% of the interest that would have applied with no payments, and halve the difference.

4. Advanced techniques

  • Use year-end withholding to drop below the threshold. Net tax owing in section 156.1 counts tax withheld over the whole year, not by month. An employee can file a new TD1 and have more tax taken from December pay or a bonus, provided payroll can process it before the last pay date of the year. An estimate of $4,200 with a further $1,300 withheld becomes $2,900, which is not above $3,000, so no instalments were required for 2026 and earlier missed payments bear no instalment interest. The CRA's Hugh example uses the same logic, starting in January.
  • Earn credit interest. Overpaying or prepaying the next instalment generates credit interest that offsets interest on other late payments for the same year. It is not refundable and does not carry over.
  • August reminder only: 75% and 25%. If the reminder shows nothing for March and June, the prior-year and current-year options call for 75% of the total on September 15 and 25% on December 15; the no-calculation option follows box 2.
  • Allow processing time. The CRA suggests waiting 3 business days for online payments and 10 for cheques before checking. Dates falling on a weekend or recognised holiday move to the next business day; December 15, 2026 is a Tuesday.
  • Check the allocation. A payment posted to arrears or another year can be moved with "Transfer payment(s)" in your CRA account. Claim the year's total on line 47600.
  • Leaving Quebec. If you paid Revenu Québec and then moved, request a refund and a letter showing the payment dates; the CRA honours those dates when you send both on.

5. Budget 2026: a $7,500 threshold, but not until 2028

Budget 2026 proposes raising the threshold for individuals from $3,000 to $7,500 ($1,800 to $4,500 in Quebec), indexed to inflation, and letting individuals whose main income is from a business make one payment a year, two-thirds of net tax owing by December 31, as farmers and fishers do. Both would apply from the 2028 tax year. A separate proposal would move the filing and payment date for all individuals to May 15 for 2027 and later years.

For 2026 the existing rules stand: a $3,000 threshold and a balance due on April 30, 2027. Proposals may change before enactment.

6. Common misconceptions

  • "No reminder, but I must still pay quarterly." The CRA lists three conditions for instalment interest: you are required to pay, you received a reminder, and you paid late or too little.
  • "A reminder is a bill." It is built on past data and does not know this year's income.
  • "Self-employed people pay on June 15." They may file by June 15; the balance is still due on April 30.
  • "The first $3,000 is exempt." It is a threshold. With net tax owing of $3,100, the whole $3,100 is subject to instalments.

Questions

What should a newcomer watch in year two?

First-year net tax owing becomes the test for the second. If the first year exceeded $3,000 and the second is expected to, instalments start in year two, and the first reminder may not come until August.

I had a one-off capital gain. Must I pay instalments next year?

Not if next year's net tax owing falls back to $3,000 or less; the reminder may be disregarded. If it stays above the threshold but is much lower, use the current-year option.

Can an overpayment be refunded?

The CRA accepts a letter to your tax centre or a call to 1-800-959-8281 and reviews each case. Otherwise it is settled through line 47600 on your return.

Reminder: the CRA website and your CRA account prevail. For Quebec, changes of residence or large gains, consult a CPA.
