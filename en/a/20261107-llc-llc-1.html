New Mexico, Wyoming or Delaware LLC: which state for a foreign owner's small business

Nov 7, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

State fees, annual costs, privacy and federal filings compared for a foreign-owned single-member LLC. For a small business, annual and compliance costs matter most.

For a foreign owner running a small business with no plans to raise capital, New Mexico is usually cheapest ($50 to form, no annual report or fee), followed by Wyoming ($100 to form, annual report from $60). Delaware wins on financing and legal maturity but rarely pays off for a small business, with an annual tax from $400. Whichever state you choose, a foreign-owned single-member LLC must file Form 5472 with the IRS, and that matters more than any state fee.

These exclude a registered agent, which all three states require. Acting yourself or using an acquaintance can cost nothing; commercial services typically charge a little over $100 a year, and quotes vary.

Delaware's annual tax: sources differ

Delaware's official LLC tax page says $400 a year, due by June 1. An article from Wolters Kluwer/CT Corporation says $300, possibly an older figure. We follow the official site; recheck before paying.

Privacy and public records

  • New Mexico: some sources say public filings need not list members' names and addresses, only the registered agent. This comes from commercial guides such as Wise, not state text, and banks will still ask for your details.
  • Wyoming: commercial guides commonly describe it as privacy-friendly; check WyoBiz for what is actually public.
  • Delaware: often described as private, but we found no official statement to that effect.

Federal rules: the same in all three states

1. Form 5472 with a pro forma 1120

A US LLC wholly owned by a foreign person (a disregarded entity for tax) must file Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120. Only the name, address and items B and E on page 1 of the 1120 are completed, with "Foreign-owned U.S. DE" written across the top.

  • How to file: the IRS says it cannot be e-filed; fax to 855-887-7737 or mail to the PIN Unit in Ogden, Utah.
  • Extension: Form 7004.
  • Penalty: $25,000 for failing to file correctly and on time, plus another $25,000 if not corrected within 90 days of IRS notice, and more for each further 30 days.

Commercial sites give due dates of April 15 or March 15; sources differ. The IRS page says only "the due date of the 1120". Confirm your tax year and deadline with a CPA.

2. FinCEN beneficial ownership (BOI) reports

FinCEN currently states that companies formed in the US and their beneficial owners do not need to file BOI reports; only companies formed abroad and registered in a US state do. Some law firms caution that the requirement could return if the rule is overturned in court.

How to choose

  1. Tightest budget, simple business: New Mexico. $50 to form, no annual fee or report.
  2. Willing to pay $60 a year for a widely used state: Wyoming, with ample online resources and providers.
  3. Planning to raise money or build an equity structure: Delaware. Unnecessary for most small businesses.
  4. Actually operating in another state (office, staff, inventory): you will usually need a foreign qualification and local fees there too, so forming in one of these three may mean paying twice.

Banking and tax pitfalls

  • Opening a bank account can be harder than choosing a state, depending on the bank's rules for foreigners and whether you have a US address and an SSN or ITIN.
  • New Mexico levies a statewide gross receipts tax that matters if you operate there. State income tax passes through to the owner; some sources put rates at 1.5% to 5.9%.
  • Wyoming has no corporate income tax or franchise tax; the annual report and license tax are an LLC's only routine state obligations.

FAQ

Can I form in New Mexico without living there?

Yes, but you need a registered agent with a physical address in the state: yourself if present there, an acquaintance, or a service.

Do I file Form 5472 with no income?

Yes. The IRS requires it for foreign-owned US disregarded entities regardless of income. Some commercial sites explicitly say "file even with no activity"; ask a CPA about any exceptions.

What if I miss Wyoming's annual report?

There is no fixed late fee, but after 60 days overdue the Secretary of State may dissolve the LLC. File promptly and check WyoBiz.

State, IRS and FinCEN official pages prevail; fees and rules change. Verify on the official site; for foreign-owner tax, Form 5472 or out-of-state operations, consult a CPA or attorney.
