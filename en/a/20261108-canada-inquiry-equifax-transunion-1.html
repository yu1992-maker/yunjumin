Can a credit inquiry be removed in Canada? Equifax and TransUnion

Nov 8, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Only unauthorized, duplicate or fraudulent inquiries may be removed. Filing steps, and where sources disagree.

A legitimate inquiry, made when you applied for a card, loan or rental, generally cannot be removed; it drops off when it expires. Only inquiries you did not authorize, duplicates, or those resulting from identity theft may be removed on request to Equifax Canada or TransUnion Canada. Requests are free, and Equifax accepts them online and by mail. Sources disagree on how long inquiries are kept, as noted below.

Which inquiries can be removed

SituationRemovable?
A card or loan you applied for, approved or declinedGenerally no; wait for expiry
A check by an organization you never applied toYou may request it as unauthorized
Repeat checks by one organization for one applicationYou may request it as a duplicate
Identity theft or fraudulent applicationYou may request it, and should deal with the identity theft as well

Being declined, or regretting an application, is not a ground for removal. Several sources (Koho, Borrowell and others) agree that a legitimate inquiry is not removed because of a denial.

How long inquiries stay (sources disagree)

  • Equifax Canada: secondary sources citing an Equifax page say inquiries clear automatically 3 years after the date of the check. The Equifax dispute page we opened does not state a retention period. Another source says about 2 years.
  • TransUnion Canada: one source says up to 6 years, against 3 at Equifax. We could not open TransUnion's official page to verify (it returned 403), so check its website yourself.
  • The effect on the score usually ends before the inquiry disappears. An inquiry for a new card typically costs a few points; late payments and collections do far more damage.

Step 1: identify the inquiry

  1. Get both the Equifax and TransUnion reports. They may differ.
  2. Find the date and organization. Recall whether you applied for a phone plan, rental, car loan or card that day; some companies appear under a parent or finance subsidiary.
  3. Check whether it is a soft check (viewing your own report, pre-approvals). Soft checks do not affect the score and need no removal.
  4. Confirm the organization and date before deciding to dispute.

Step 2: Equifax Canada

The following is from Equifax Canada's official dispute page:

  • Online: request the dispute form on the website (sent by email), then submit online with up to 3 files; combine multiple pages into one PDF. Estimated time is about 15 to 20 days.
  • Mail: print the form and send it with documents to Equifax Canada Co., National Consumer Relations, Box 190, Montreal, Quebec H1S 2Z2. Estimated time is about 20 to 25 days.
  • Phone: the page lists numbers only in notices about Canada Post disruptions, and two notices give different numbers (1-866-828-5961 and 1-800-871-3250) without saying which is current. Confirm before calling.
  • Documents: personal information changes require two government-issued photo IDs (both sides) and proof of address within 90 days. Account disputes require bank statements, letters from the organization and so on. Include the Equifax reference number if your report has one.

Online filers receive the result by email and mail filers by letter. Equifax may ask for more information in the meantime.

Step 3: TransUnion Canada

We could not open TransUnion's official page. The following comes from an August 2021 Prince of Travel article and may have changed; check TransUnion Canada's website before filing:

  1. Log in to your TransUnion online account and choose a general investigation.
  2. Select the inquiry you do not recognize and state that you do not recognize it.
  3. Attach evidence, such as emails showing you did not apply or proof of identity theft.
  4. If online identity verification fails, use phone or mail. Online cases take up to 25 business days.

The author found TransUnion easier to deal with than Equifax, and mail more effective. That is personal experience, not an official position.

Should you contact the lender first?

Sources disagree. Koho and Borrowell suggest contacting the organization that made the check, explaining that it was unauthorized and asking it to withdraw. The Prince of Travel author finds organizations slow and skips this step. The safer course is to do both: write to the organization to create a record and file with the bureau at the same time. If the organization replies that the check was legitimate, the bureau will usually not remove it.

If you are refused

  • Ask for a file number and follow up by phone.
  • Add evidence and appeal by mail.
  • If you suspect a full report was pulled without consent, complain to the organization's privacy officer. A Borrowell article notes that a lender needs your authorization to pull a full report (PIPEDA).
  • In case of identity theft, report promptly to police and both bureaus and consider a fraud alert.

Frequently asked questions

I was declined for a card. Can that inquiry be removed?

No. If you applied yourself, the inquiry is legitimate whether approved, declined or withdrawn, and you must wait for it to expire.

Must I file separately with Equifax and TransUnion?

Yes. They are independent and keep separate records. Removal at one does not mean removal at the other.

Will removing one inquiry raise my score noticeably?

Usually not by much. Sources generally say inquiries have a small effect, and late payments and collections matter more. If you need a loan soon, apply for less new credit rather than fight legitimate inquiries.

Rely on official information from Equifax Canada, TransUnion Canada and the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC). For identity theft or appeals, consult a licensed professional or lawyer.
