Trump Accounts: the $1,000 needs an election, and parents may use an ITIN

Nov 23, 20266 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Accounts opened automatically from October 1, 2026, but the $1,000 still needs Form 4547. The child needs an SSN; a parent without one may enter an ITIN.

Since October 1, 2026 the Treasury has opened Trump Accounts automatically for eligible children. Automatic opening does not deliver the $1,000, nor does it let relatives contribute. The $1,000 still has to be elected on Form 4547 and goes only to U.S. citizens born from 2025 through 2028; family contributions require the account to be claimed and activated first. The line that matters most to immigrant families sits in the Form 4547 instructions: the child needs an SSN valid for employment, but a parent who is not eligible for an SSN may enter an ITIN.

This article draws on the IRS Trump Accounts page and FAQ (updated October 21, 2026), the Instructions for Form 4547 (December 2025), Notice 2025-68, and temporary regulations TD 10056, published in the Federal Register on September 30, 2026. Sources were checked on November 23, 2026. The temporary regulations apply to taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2026 and expire on September 30, 2029. The rules we found on the $1,000 pilot and employer contributions are still proposed, so details may change.

Many parents mistake the first row for the whole. Under the temporary regulations, an auto account may receive only two kinds of money during the growth period: qualified general contributions routed through Treasury by governments or charities, and the $1,000 pilot contribution. A grandparent's $500 cannot go into an unclaimed auto account.

Filing a claim is not enough either. The account must be activated under the trustee's instructions, including signing the agreement, before the auto account's balance moves across. BNY is the designated financial agent; Robinhood Securities LLC is the brokerage provider and initial trustee.

The parent's side is far looser. Part I of the Form 4547 instructions states that a nonresident or resident alien who does not have, and is not eligible for, an SSN should enter an ITIN. Do not read this across to the child tax credit or other benefits, which have their own identification rules. We found nothing on these IRS pages about whether a parent without an SSN can pass the app's identity check; if it fails, call 866-USA-4547 or file Form 4547 on paper.

Two situations are easily missed. If the child's card reads "Not Valid for Employment" but the child is now a citizen or permanent resident, the instructions say to ask the SSA for a card without the legend. And the SSN must be issued before the election; filing first and adding the number later does not work.

3. December 31: five weeks left for children born in 2009

The growth period runs from the day the account is established to December 31 of the year the child turns 17. For a child born in 2009 it ends on December 31, 2026, after which ordinary traditional IRA rules apply and contributions generally require compensation. Children born in 2008 turn 18 in 2026 and no longer qualify.

Notice 2025-68 notes that section 219(f)(3), which lets ordinary IRA owners fund the prior year until the filing deadline, does not apply during the growth period. There is no April 15 grace: the 2026 limit can be used only in 2026.

4. Worked example: the source of the money decides the later tax

No contribution is deductible. What differs is basis. Family contributions create it; the government's $1,000, qualified general contributions and section 128 employer contributions do not. After 18, each distribution is split in proportion to basis.

Scenario (figures assumed): the account is worth $40,000, made up of $16,000 from parents, $1,000 from the government, $5,000 from an employer and $18,000 of growth. The child withdraws $10,000:

  • Basis ratio: $16,000 ÷ $40,000 = 40%;
  • Tax-free: $10,000 × 40% = $4,000;
  • Ordinary income: $6,000;
  • If no exception applies, the 10% additional tax is $6,000 × 10% = $600. Exceptions named in Notice 2025-68 include qualified higher education expenses and first home purchases.

An account with little basis may look like free money, but more of each withdrawal is taxable. Keep your own record of the source and date of every contribution.

5. Advanced moves

  • Elect the $1,000 first, then deal with the claim. Neither replaces the other. A child already auto-enrolled does not need Form 4547 to open the account, but the pilot election still requires it. If you have already elected, do not file again.
  • In separated families, activation order matters. Where several people claim the same auto account, the first authorized person to activate becomes the responsible party and chooses investments and transfers. The $1,000 election follows a different test: the person who anticipates the child will be a qualifying child that year.
  • Ask the employer before you contribute. Section 128 contributions are excluded from the employee's income up to $2,500 a year, but they consume the $5,000 limit and create no basis. If the family has already put in $5,000, the employer's tax-free money will not fit.
  • The child's own IRA is untouched. Growth-period contributions do not count against other IRA limits, and under Notice 2025-68 the IRA aggregation rule applies separately, so a parent's backdoor Roth calculation is unaffected.
  • You may change trustee. The balance can go directly to a rollover Trump account elsewhere, but only in full; a child may have only one funded account at a time.

6. Common misconceptions

  • "The $1,000 arrives once the account is opened." The IRS page says you must elect it even if Treasury opened the account.
  • "I can close it and withdraw." A funded account cannot be closed during the growth period except after a permitted distribution, and Notice 2025-68 rules out hardship distributions.
  • "Both parents must be citizens." The citizenship test for the $1,000 applies to the child.
  • "Errors need an amended return." The IRS says not to use Form 1040-X; follow the CP318 notice and resubmit Form 4547.
  • "The instructions say Form 4547 is required to open." The December 2025 instructions predate auto-enrollment and differ from the IRS page updated October 21, 2026. Follow the newer page and watch for revised instructions.

FAQ

Can we choose investments?

During the growth period only funds tracking a broad index of primarily U.S. companies, without leverage and with annual costs of no more than 0.1%, are allowed. This describes the account rules and is not investment advice.

What if we move to China, Canada or the UK?

Form 4547 accepts foreign addresses. We found no dedicated guidance from other tax authorities on how they treat the account, so take advice in both countries before moving.

How do I know whether my child was enrolled?

Check the official app or trumpaccounts.gov. Use only the store links on the IRS page; IRS telephone staff cannot correct an election or claim the account for you.
