Cash Back Is Tax-Free, Bank Bonuses Are Not: The New $2,000 1099 Threshold

Nov 22, 20265 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Spend-based rewards are a purchase discount; account and referral bonuses are income. From 2026 the 1099-MISC threshold rises to $2,000, but reporting duties do not change.

In the US, rewards you earn by spending, including spend-based sign-up bonuses, are generally treated as a purchase discount and are not taxable income. Rewards that require no purchase, such as bank account bonuses, referral bonuses and points for opening an account, are generally taxable. From 2026 the 1099-MISC threshold for "other income" rises from $600 to $2,000, so you will receive fewer forms, but what you owe does not change.

1. The dividing line: did you buy something?

The question is whether the reward reduces the price of a purchase or is money paid to you for nothing in return. The first lowers your cost; the second is income.

The 1099-MISC/NEC instructions ("What's New") state that for tax years beginning after 2025 the minimum threshold for certain information returns and backup withholding rises to $2,000, with inflation adjustment possible from calendar year 2027; prizes, awards and other income in box 3 use the $2,000 figure. The 1099-INT instructions require a form for interest of at least $10.

2. Two cases that mark the boundary

Shankar (2014): account-opening points redeemed for a flight are taxable

According to published reports, the taxpayer received 50,000 ThankYou points from Citibank for opening a bank account and in 2009 redeemed them for an economy ticket; Citibank issued a 1099-MISC for $668. The Tax Court held the ticket's value was gross income. There was no purchase; the points were consideration for opening the account. A footnote left open whether the taxable event was receipt or redemption of the points.

Anikeev (2021): cash back is not income, unless you bought cash equivalents

The taxpayers earned more than $277,000 in Amex Blue Cash rewards, largely by buying Visa gift cards, reloading Green Dot debit cards and buying money orders. The court reaffirmed that cash back is generally not includible in an individual's gross income, but held rewards attributable to cash-equivalent purchases taxable because no goods or services were acquired. Commentators differ on exactly where gift cards fall; the consensus is that money-order and debit-reload rewards were held taxable.

3. Worked example: $1,850 of rewards in 2026

A single filer earns $70,000 in W-2 wages in 2026, lives in Florida (no state income tax) and takes the standard deduction. IRS figures for 2026: standard deduction $16,100; the 22% rate applies above $50,400 of taxable income and 24% above $105,700.

  1. Taxable wages: $70,000 − $16,100 = $53,900, already in the 22% bracket.
  2. Rewards: a $750 card sign-up bonus (requires $4,000 of spending); a $400 checking bonus and a $200 savings bonus (both on 1099-INT); a $500 card referral bonus (under $2,000, so likely no 1099-MISC). Total $1,850.
  3. Taxable: $400 + $200 + $500 = $1,100. The $750 is treated as a purchase discount.
  4. Extra federal tax: $1,100 × 22% = $242. In states with an income tax, add state tax.

The $500 referral is the one most often missed: the issuer may no longer send a form, but taxability never depended on receiving one.

4. Pro tips

  • Keep a rewards ledger. Log each bank or referral bonus with posting date, amount, institution and whether spending was required. Reconcile against forms in February and ask for corrections to avoid a CP2000 mismatch notice.
  • Tax follows the posting year. A bonus earned in December but credited in January generally belongs to the next year; follow the year on the bank's form and do not double-report.
  • Points are valued as the institution reports them. In Shankar the court used Citibank's $668. Keep the issuer's valuation for any no-spend points.
  • Business card knock-on effect. Under the discount logic, cash back on business spending lowers the real cost of that expense, and most commentators say the deduction should be reduced accordingly. Self-employed readers should confirm with a CPA.
  • Avoid cash equivalents. Rewards from money orders or debit-card loads were held taxable in Anikeev, and issuers often treat such transactions as cash advances or close accounts.

5. Common mistakes

  • "No 1099, nothing to report." Pub 550: you must report all your interest income even if you do not receive a Form 1099-INT. The higher 2026 threshold will make this mistake more common.
  • "All sign-up bonuses are tax-free." Only those conditioned on spending are treated as discounts.
  • "Interest under $10 isn't income." $10 is the bank's reporting threshold, not a tax exemption.
  • "$2,000 is a new exemption." It governs whether the payer files a form, not whether you have income.

FAQ

Do the same rules apply to ITIN filers?

Resident aliens follow the same income rules whether they file with an SSN or an ITIN. Nonresident aliens face separate sourcing and withholding rules and should seek professional advice.

Where does a bank bonus go on the return?

Report a 1099-INT amount as interest; a 1099-MISC box 3 amount is generally other income on Schedule 1. Without a form, report according to the nature of the bonus.

What if a bonus is clawed back?

Spend-based rewards were never income, so a reversal does not matter for tax. If a no-spend bonus is reclaimed, keep the bank records and confirm the adjustment with a CPA.
