Citi checking bonus: direct deposit amount, waiting time and fine print

Nov 7, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Citi Regular Checking bonus: the $3,000 direct deposit requirement for $325, the 90-day window, payout timing, fees and where sources disagree.

As of November 7, 2026, sources report a $325 Citi direct deposit bonus: open a Regular Checking account and, within 90 days, receive at least two Enhanced Direct Deposits (EDD) totaling $3,000 or more. The bonus is not paid immediately, and the account must stay open. Citi updates offers often; the figures below are from late October 2026 reports, so check Citi's current offer page before opening.

For most people the $325 direct deposit tier has the lowest bar and needs no large balance.

What counts as an Enhanced Direct Deposit

Per Citi terms quoted by Doctor of Credit, an EDD is an ACH deposit such as payroll, pension, Social Security or government benefits.

  • Counts: employer payroll via ACH; Zelle received via ACH; Venmo or PayPal transfers arriving via ACH.
  • Does not count: branch or cash deposits, checks, mobile check deposits, wires, transfers between Citi accounts, ATM deposits, debit card loads, P2P sent to a Citi debit card, and instant transfers initiated from other banks.

Whether Zelle or P2P counts depends on whether it travels over ACH; test with a small amount and track progress in the Citi app.

Steps

  1. Check eligibility: 18 or older, meet tax requirements, and no Citi checking account in the past 365 days.
  2. Apply through Citi's offer page, not the homepage, or the offer may not be attached.
  3. Note the opening date; the 90 days start then.
  4. Arrange two or more ACH deposits totaling $3,000, from payroll or qualifying ACH transfers.
  5. If the app's Rewards & Upgrades section shows a tracker, check it; not everyone sees one.
  6. Do not close the account before the bonus posts.

Payout timing: sources differ

  • Citi's terms summary, quoted by Doctor of Credit: the bonus posts between day 90 and day 120 after opening, if the account is open and in good standing.
  • Other sources (Finder and some aggregators): within about 30 days of meeting the requirement.

These conflict. Plan conservatively for three to four months after opening, and do not close or empty the account at day 90.

If you will not keep the account, budget for monthly fees and close only after the bonus posts.

Why $425 and $475 are confusing

Doctor of Credit said on October 28 that the $425 and $475 offers had ended, then on October 30 that $475 had reappeared, possibly by invitation, with terms requiring notice from Citi. The same page limits $475 to certain states and areas (such as New York, Connecticut, New Jersey, DC, Virginia, Maryland, California, Nevada and parts of Florida and Illinois) and says it is currently unavailable. Without an invitation, do not plan around it.

Verify the offer deadline

Several sources cite an October 26, 2026 deadline, and a July 15 Doctor of Credit update mentioned the $475 offer extended to October 22. As of November 7 these dates may have passed or been replaced; confirm the dates and amounts on the official page first.

Things to note

  • Tax: bank bonuses are income; Doctor of Credit notes they are treated as income or interest, usually with a 1099.
  • Funding: terms say no credit card funding, and debit card funding up to $1,000.
  • Referrals: Doctor of Credit says referrers earn $100 per successful referral; referred customers get the standard bonus.
  • Repeat bonuses: a Citi checking account in the past 365 days disqualifies you.
  • Account status: keep it open and in good standing until the bonus posts.

FAQ

Is a single paycheck enough for $3,000?

It depends on the total: at least two EDDs totaling $3,000 within 90 days. Biweekly pay can qualify if the total is met and each deposit is ACH, not a check.

Can I use Zelle or Venmo to create my own direct deposits?

Citi's definition says ACH-based Zelle and P2P can count, but debit-card and instant transfers do not. Results vary and we cannot confirm them; test small first.

How long until payout, and must I keep the account?

Sources differ: 90 to 120 days per Citi's terms summary, or within 30 days of qualifying per others. Plan for three to four months and keep the account open until paid.

Verify on the official site; for tax, cross-state or status questions, consult a CPA or attorney.
