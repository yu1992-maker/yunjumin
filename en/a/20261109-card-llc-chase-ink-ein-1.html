Can a New Mexico LLC get a Chase Ink card? EIN and credit requirements

Nov 9, 20263 min readYunjumin Forum editors

A New Mexico LLC can apply. How the EIN, personal credit, 5/24 and a new company affect the outcome.

Yes, a New Mexico LLC can apply. Approval for a Chase Ink business card rests mainly on the applicant's personal credit and income; the state of registration is not a bar. The form usually asks for an EIN ("Federal tax ID"), but having one does not guarantee approval.

Key points

The EIN: how and when

  • Cost: the IRS issues EINs free; any fee is a third party's.
  • With an SSN or ITIN: several third-party guides say the online application needs a responsible party who is an individual with an SSN or ITIN.
  • Without either: third parties say phone, fax or mail with Form SS-4 is the only route; reported times range from days to weeks.
  • Sole proprietor or LLC: one source says an LLC with an EIN applies with it and the bank may also check business credit, while a sole proprietor may use an SSN alone. A single-member LLC is taxed as a sole proprietorship by default, but which box to choose depends on Chase's application.

See the IRS EIN page and the Form SS-4 instructions for the rules; this site does not replace them.

Personal credit: what score is safe

Ink cards carry a personal guarantee, so your own credit is checked. Third-party views differ:

  • Upgraded Points and similar guides suggest a personal FICO of 670 or higher.
  • Others say most approvals are around 700 or above; one 2026 guide suggests 680+ for Ink Business Unlimited.
  • Chase publishes no cutoff.

Our suggestion: aim for 700 or above, at least two years of history, and few recent accounts.

5/24: the gate people overlook

  1. 5/24 counts the personal cards you opened with any issuer in the past 24 months. Most sources say you generally need fewer than five for Chase approval.
  2. An approved Ink card usually does not add to your count, because most do not appear on personal reports.
  3. Sources say Capital One and Discover business cards do count; reporting for some cards is disputed.
  4. The rule is unpublished, and some reports say Chase occasionally relaxes it during new offers.

New LLC, no revenue

Chase states that a business card is possible without revenue and that revenue may be $0. The form usually asks for your total annual personal income, because the issuer requires a personal guarantee.

Forum reports vary:

  • On the FICO forum, some were told a new company may enter projected revenue; approval depends mainly on personal credit, and the limit on stated revenue.
  • Some new LLCs were declined for Ink Unlimited or Ink Preferred because the business was "too new".
  • A FlyerTalk thread says business approvals tightened after April 2020; that is old and current policy may differ.

Checklist before applying

  1. Register the LLC through the New Mexico Secretary of State's business site and keep the documents. Most sources give a $50 fee (some add about $2) and no annual report; the Secretary of State prevails.
  2. Keep the IRS confirmation (CP 575 or similar).
  3. Match the company name, address and EIN to the registration. A FlyerTalk post says an unverifiable name leads to denial; one blogger's new LLC was asked for proof of the LLC and EIN.
  4. Check your personal report and the cards opened in 24 months.
  5. State real personal income and real revenue.
  6. If sent to manual review, supply the documents; avoid reapplying.

Questions

1. Is an LLC with no revenue declined outright?

Not automatically. Chase says $0 is acceptable with personal income, but forum reports include "too new" denials, so results vary.

2. Does an Ink card affect my 5/24 count?

Most sources say an approved Ink card does not count, though your existing personal cards are checked. Other issuers' business cards depend on the card.

3. Can I apply with only an ITIN, or neither number?

We found no official Chase statement. For the EIN, third parties say the online form needs a responsible party with an SSN or ITIN; others use Form SS-4 by paper or phone. Ask Chase business card service about its own requirement.

Official sources prevail: IRS.gov for EINs, the New Mexico Secretary of State for LLCs, Chase for card terms. For tax, entity or nonresident questions, consult a CPA or attorney.
