The UK £100,000 trap: a 60% marginal rate and the childcare cliff

Nov 22, 20265 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Each pound from £100,000 to £125,140 is taxed at 60%, and £1 over the line ends childcare support. Pension contributions are the compliant fix.

In the 2026/27 UK tax year, every pound of adjusted net income between £100,000 and £125,140 is effectively taxed at 60%, or 62% once 2% National Insurance is added. The same £100,000 line is a cliff edge for Tax-Free Childcare and England's 30 hours of free childcare: one pound over and the entire entitlement goes. Pension contributions and Gift Aid reduce the figure directly, making them among the few fully compliant planning tools with this much leverage.

Sources: GOV.UK Income Tax rates and Personal Allowances; HMRC guidance Personal Allowances: adjusted net income (updated 30 May 2025); the High Income Child Benefit Charge, Tax-Free Childcare, Free Childcare for Working Parents, pension tax relief and annual allowance pages; and HMRC's salary sacrifice policy paper of 4 December 2025. Checked on 22 November 2026. Figures apply to England, Wales and Northern Ireland; Scottish bands differ.

1. Three thresholds, one measure: adjusted net income

HMRC defines it as total taxable income (employment, self-employment profits, rent, savings interest, dividends and foreign income) less trading losses, gross pension contributions and grossed-up Gift Aid (the amount paid × 1.25). On 16 May 2025 HMRC added foreign income covered by a foreign income and gains (FIG) claim to step one, so newcomers using the four-year FIG regime still count that income towards these lines.

Child Benefit for 2026/27 is £27.05 a week for the eldest child and £17.90 for each other child, about £2,337 a year for two. A two-child household earning between £60,000 and £80,000 therefore pays 40% income tax, 2% NI and gives back £2,337 on the next £20,000: a marginal burden of roughly 53.7%.

2. Worked example: £110,000 salary, two children

Assumptions: England, employee, £110,000 salary and no other income; children aged 2 and 5, each with £10,000 a year of childcare costs.

  • Personal Allowance: £10,000 over the line removes £5,000 of allowance, cutting it from £12,570 to £7,570.
  • Tax on the £10,000 above £100,000: £4,000 at 40%, plus £2,000 on the lost allowance, a total of £6,000; NI adds £200 at 2%. Effective marginal rate: 62%.
  • Childcare: above £100,000 the family loses Tax-Free Childcare (£2 top-up for every £8 paid, capped at £2,000 per child a year), up to £4,000 for two children, and the 2-year-old loses the 30 free hours.

Now pay £8,000 net into a relief-at-source personal pension. The provider adds £2,000 basic-rate relief, making £10,000 gross. Adjusted net income falls to £100,000, the full £12,570 allowance returns and the basic-rate band extends by £10,000. A further £4,000 comes back through Self Assessment (£2,000 from the restored allowance, £2,000 from the 40% versus 20% difference).

3. Pro tips

  1. Prefer salary sacrifice. It reduces adjusted net income in the same way and also saves the 2% employee NI, another £200 in the example. HMRC's policy paper of 4 December 2025 says that from 6 April 2029 the NI exemption on salary-sacrificed contributions will be limited to the first £2,000 a year; income tax relief is unchanged.
  2. Claim higher-rate relief yourself. Under relief at source the provider adds only basic-rate relief; GOV.UK says anyone paying above 20% must claim the rest through Self Assessment. Without a claim the £4,000 in the example is lost.
  3. Use carry-forward. The annual allowance is £60,000 and unused allowance from the previous three tax years can be carried forward. In a bonus year this can bring income back below £100,000, though relief remains capped at 100% of that year's earnings.
  4. Gift Aid counts too. An £800 donation reduces adjusted net income by its £1,000 gross value, a useful year-end fine-tuning tool for regular donors.
  5. Keep savings in an ISA. Savings interest and dividends count towards adjusted net income; ISA returns are not taxable income and do not.
  6. Mind the timing. Tax-Free Childcare and the 30 hours use expected adjusted net income for the current tax year and must be reconfirmed every three months. Offset pay rises and bonuses with contributions before the tax year ends on 5 April.

4. Common misconceptions

  • "Everything over £100,000 is taxed at 60%." Only the slice between £100,000 and £125,140; above £125,140 the additional rate of 45% applies.
  • "It is household income that counts." The Child Benefit charge and the allowance taper are individual. Childcare eligibility fails if either partner exceeds £100,000: a couple on £90,000 each can claim Tax-Free Childcare, a household on £105,000 and £0 cannot.
  • "Foreign income under FIG is ignored." HMRC's May 2025 update lists it within adjusted net income, and the Tax-Free Childcare page says foreign income is included.
  • "Do not register for Child Benefit to avoid the charge." GOV.UK suggests registering and opting out of payments, which preserves National Insurance credits towards the State Pension and gives the child an NI number automatically.
  • "The more you contribute, the better." Once income is below £100,000, further relief drops back to 40%, and exceeding the annual allowance triggers a charge reportable on Self Assessment.

FAQ

A mid-year pay rise takes me over £100,000. Will Tax-Free Childcare be clawed back?

Eligibility is based on expected income for the tax year and reconfirmed every three months, so report changes accurately. Bringing full-year adjusted net income back within £100,000 by pension contributions before 5 April can preserve eligibility. Any recovery is decided by HMRC case by case.

Does this apply to Scottish taxpayers?

The allowance taper is identical, £1 lost per £2 above £100,000, but Scottish bands differ, so the marginal rate is not 60%. Additional pension relief in Scotland is claimed at 21%, 42%, 45% or 48%.

Do I need to claim relief on an employer net pay scheme?

No. Net pay contributions are deducted before tax, so relief at your top rate is automatic. Only relief-at-source schemes and schemes without automatic relief require a claim.

Verify on GOV.UK and HMRC before acting. For foreign income, multiple jobs or self-employment, consult a chartered tax adviser or accountant.
