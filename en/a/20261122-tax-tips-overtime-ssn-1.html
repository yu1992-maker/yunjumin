No Tax on Tips and Overtime: The $25,000 Cap, the SSN Test and the New W-2 Codes

Nov 22, 20265 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Tips and overtime are deductible for 2025–2028 on Schedule 1-A, but only with an SSN valid for employment. ITINs do not qualify; 2026 W-2s add codes TP and TT.

for tax years 2025 through 2028, the tips deduction is capped at $25,000 per return and the overtime deduction at $12,500 ($25,000 on a joint return). Both are claimed on Schedule 1-A, whether you itemize or take the standard deduction. Three conditions apply: the worker who earned the tips or overtime must have a Social Security number valid for employment, married taxpayers must file jointly, and the deduction phases out above $150,000 of modified AGI ($300,000 joint). ITIN filers cannot claim it. From tax year 2026, Form W-2 carries two new box 12 codes, TP and TT, that feed directly into the calculation.

The phase-out is steep but simple: per the Internal Revenue Manual (IRM 21.6.4), the cap falls by $100 for every $1,000 of MAGI above the threshold. The deduction lands on Form 1040 line 13b, below adjusted gross income. It reduces federal taxable income, not AGI, so anything keyed to AGI is unaffected.

2. "SSN valid for employment": the gate most people miss

  • Definition. The SSN must be valid for employment and issued by the Social Security Administration before the return's due date, including extensions. An SSN obtained as a US citizen is always valid for employment.
  • ITINs do not qualify. An ITIN is not an SSN.
  • Card says "Not Valid for Employment". If you are now a citizen or permanent resident, ask SSA for a replacement card without the legend.
  • Card says "Valid for Work Only with DHS Authorization". The SSN counts only while the DHS authorization is valid.
  • Mixed-status couples. The instructions require a valid SSN for the spouse who received the tips. A household where one spouse works in a restaurant with an SSN and the other holds an ITIN can still claim on a joint return. Married filing separately rules out both deductions.

FS-2026-04 notes that Schedule 1-A can be attached to Form 1040, 1040-SR or 1040-NR. Whether a particular nonresident qualifies still depends on the SSN and occupation tests, so complex cases belong with a CPA who handles nonresident returns.

3. Worked examples

Tips. A, a single server, earns $40,000 in W-2 wages plus $20,000 in reported card and cash tips in 2026, with no other income, and takes the standard deduction. The IRS 2026 figures: standard deduction $16,100; 10% bracket to $12,400; 12% to $50,400.

  • Without the deduction: taxable income $43,900; tax = $1,240 + $31,500 × 12% = $5,020.
  • With a $20,000 tips deduction: taxable income $23,900; tax = $1,240 + $11,500 × 12% = $2,620.
  • Saving: $2,400. Social Security and Medicare still apply to the tips (7.65% employee share, about $1,530).

Phase-out. B, a single bartender with MAGI of $165,000 and $25,000 of qualified tips, is $15,000 over the threshold. The cap drops by 15 × $100 = $1,500, leaving $23,500.

Overtime. C, a $30-an-hour warehouse worker, is paid $22,500 at time-and-a-half for 500 overtime hours in 2026. Only the premium counts: $22,500 ÷ 3 = $7,500. If the employer pays double time, the extra above time-and-a-half does not count, as Example 2 in the instructions shows.

4. Pro tips

  • Check three boxes on your 2026 W-2. Box 12 code TP (cash tips reported to the employer), code TT (qualified overtime) and box 14b (Treasury tipped occupation code). If they are blank or look low, ask for a corrected W-2c before you file.
  • For 2025, use an IRS-approved method. Employers were not required to break these out for 2025. If your pay records show total overtime pay, divide by three; if your employer provides nothing, a reasonable estimate based on your regular rate and hours over 40 a week is allowed, provided you keep records. Returns already filed can be amended on Form 1040-X.
  • A tip chosen on the card terminal counts; an automatic service charge does not. The instructions contrast an automatic 18% large-party charge (not qualified) with 18% selected by the customer on a terminal that also offers "no tip" (qualified).
  • Unreported cash tips can still count once reported on Form 4137, along with the Social Security and Medicare tax due on them.
  • Self-employed workers are limited to net profit from the business that earned the tips, and those in a specified service trade or business under section 199A are excluded. Until final regulations are issued, people in listed occupations are treated as outside an SSTB.

5. Common misconceptions

  • "Tips and overtime are now tax-free." Only part of the federal income tax goes away. Payroll taxes remain, and state conformity varies.
  • "All overtime pay is deductible." Only the FLSA-required half-time premium counts. Exempt salaried staff paid overtime by choice of the employer do not qualify.
  • "Each spouse gets $25,000 for tips." The tips cap is $25,000 per return. The $25,000 joint figure applies to overtime.
  • "File with an ITIN now and add the SSN later." The SSN must be issued by the due date of that year's return, including extensions. If it arrives later, that year's deduction is lost.

FAQ

I am on F-1 or H-1B and earn overtime. Can I deduct it?

Two questions come first: whether your SSN is valid under a current work authorization, and whether you file as a resident or nonresident. Schedule 1-A can be attached to Form 1040-NR, but the FLSA and occupation tests still apply. Speak to a CPA who handles nonresident returns.

Do tips paid through Venmo or Zelle count?

The instructions include electronic settlements and mobile-payment apps denominated in cash. The tip still has to be voluntary, set by the customer, and reported on a W-2, a 1099 or Form 4137.

When do the new W-2 codes matter?

On 2026 returns, filed in 2027.
