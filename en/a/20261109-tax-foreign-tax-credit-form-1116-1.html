Crediting Chinese Income Tax Against US Tax With Form 1116

Nov 9, 20263 min readYunjumin Forum editors

How US tax residents credit Chinese income tax: who can use Form 1116, how the limit works, carryovers and common traps.

Income tax paid to China can usually be credited on Form 1116 against US federal tax on the same income. The credit is limited, computed by income category, to the US tax on that income. Unused amounts carry back one year and forward ten.

Credit or deduction

You may credit foreign income tax or deduct it on Schedule A, but must choose one treatment for all of it each year. The IRS notes that the credit usually helps more, because it reduces tax directly.

Which taxes qualify

IRS Topic 856 gives four conditions:

  1. the tax was imposed on you;
  2. you paid or accrued it;
  3. it is a legal and actual foreign tax liability;
  4. it is an income tax or a tax in lieu of one.

Interest, penalties and surcharges do not count. Tax on income excluded under the foreign earned income exclusion (FEIE) cannot be credited. If a treaty rate applies, only tax at that rate qualifies.

Do you need Form 1116?

  • Generally yes, attached to Form 1040, 1040-SR or 1040-NR.
  • Exception: all foreign income is passive, reported on a qualified payee statement, and other conditions are met; the IRS limit is $300 ($600 joint). Estates and trusts cannot use it. See the current 1040 instructions.
  • Chinese tax withheld on wages requires Form 1116.
  • In years you use the exception, unused credit cannot be carried either way.

Income categories

Under section 904, excess credit in one category cannot cover another:

CategoryTypical contents
PassiveInterest, most dividends, rents, royalties, investment income
GeneralWages and active business income

Each category needs its own Form 1116.

The calculation in four steps

  1. List China-source income by category in US dollars, using the exchange-rate rules in the current Form 1116 instructions, which we could not read in full.
  2. Total qualifying foreign tax in dollars.
  3. Compute the limit:
    limit = US tax before credits × (foreign taxable income in the category ÷ worldwide taxable income)
  4. Take the smaller of tax paid and limit.

Simplified example (hypothetical)

ItemAmount
Worldwide taxable income$100,000
China-source (general)$20,000
US tax before credit$15,000
Limit = 15,000 × 20%$3,000
Chinese tax paid$2,000
Credit$2,000 (the smaller)

If Chinese tax were $4,000, only $3,000 would be allowed this year and $1,000 would carry over.

Unused credit

  • One year back, ten years forward (Topic 856).
  • A carryback usually needs Form 1040-X; attach a statement showing the unused tax and computation when carrying.
  • Exception: taxes on section 951A (GILTI) income cannot be carried.

The US-China treaty

Signed in 1984 and effective in 1987, the treaty has not been updated since, per Taxes for Expats; Article 22 covers double taxation. Treaty resourcing needs a separate Form 1116 and may need Form 8833. We found no authoritative text on how the saving clause affects US citizens; where sources differ, check the treaty and IRS instructions.

FAQ

My Chinese wages were taxed. Can I skip Form 1116?

No. The $300/$600 exception covers passive income only.

Can I use the FEIE and the credit on the same income?

Tax on excluded income cannot be credited. Have a CPA model both.

What if the credit exceeds US tax?

It is not refunded but carries back one year and forward ten, except GILTI-related tax.
