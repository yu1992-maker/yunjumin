UK ISAs: The £12,000 Cash Cap Is Scrapped and the LISA Charge Falls to 20%

Nov 21, 20268 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Budget 2026 drops the £12,000 cash ISA cap. Savings tax rises to 24%/44%/49% in 2027, and the LISA charge for homes over £450,000 is now 20%.

Budget 2026 has scrapped the plan to cut the cash ISA allowance to £12,000 for under-65s from 6 April 2027. Savers can still put the full £20,000 into cash ISAs, and the anti-avoidance package, including a 22% charge on interest earned on cash inside stocks and shares ISAs, will not take effect. Meanwhile, tax rates on savings income rise to 24%/44%/49% from 6 April 2027, which makes the ISA wrapper more valuable. And since 29 October 2026, the Lifetime ISA withdrawal charge for homes costing more than £450,000 has fallen from 25% to 20%.

1. ISAs in 2027: the plan versus the outcome

HMRC's policy paper of 17 September 2026, "Cash Individual Savings Account (ISA) limit reduction", set out amendments to the ISA Regulations 1998. From 6 April 2027 the annual cash ISA subscription limit for people under 65 would fall to £12,000 within an unchanged £20,000 overall allowance. The paper also restricted transfers from non-cash ISAs into cash ISAs and imposed a flat 22% charge on interest paid on cash held in non-cash ISAs. About six weeks later, Budget 2026 reversed the plan. HMRC's newsletter 24 states that the £20,000 cash ISA limit will be retained from 6 April 2027, that the reduction and anti-circumvention rules announced at Autumn Budget 2025 "will not take effect", and that the Individual Savings Account (Amendment) (No.2) Regulations 2026 will be revoked.

2. Why the wrapper matters more after 2027: higher savings tax rates

According to HMRC's newsletter 24, from 6 April 2027 the basic, higher and additional rates on savings income rise by a further two percentage points, to 24%, 44% and 49% for 2027/28. The starting rate for savings and the Personal Savings Allowance are unchanged. GOV.UK sets the Personal Savings Allowance at £1,000 for basic-rate taxpayers, £500 for higher-rate taxpayers and nil for additional-rate taxpayers. Where other taxable income is below £17,570, up to £5,000 of interest may also fall within the starting rate for savings. Interest, income and gains inside an ISA are tax-free and do not need to be declared on a Self Assessment return.

Worked example (the interest rate is an assumption for illustration): a higher-rate taxpayer in 2027/28 holds £60,000 in cash earning 4%.

ScenarioTaxable interestAfter £500 allowanceTax at 44%
All in an ordinary savings account£2,400£1,900£836
£20,000 sheltered in a cash ISA£1,600£1,100£484

One year's allowance saves about £352 a year, and the gap widens as allowances accumulate. Under the abandoned £12,000 cap, £1,920 of interest would have sat outside the wrapper, producing roughly £625 of tax, about £141 more than with the full £20,000. That is the practical value of the reversal for cautious savers.

3. Newcomers and leavers: residence decides whether you can subscribe

GOV.UK requires ISA holders to be 18 or over and resident in the UK, with exceptions for armed forces members and Crown servants working overseas and their spouses or civil partners. A Lifetime ISA must also be opened before age 40. Residence is determined by the Statutory Residence Test; see our article on UK tax residence and the four-year FIG regime for newcomers.

ISA relief is a UK tax relief only. Holders who are also tax resident or citizens elsewhere, such as US citizens or green card holders, need to check separately whether that country recognises it. GOV.UK says nothing on this point.

4. The Lifetime ISA after 29 October 2026, and the First Time Buyer ISA

Under the GOV.UK Lifetime ISA guide, you can pay in up to £4,000 a year until age 50, with a 25% government bonus worth up to £1,000 a year. LISA contributions count towards the £20,000 overall allowance. A penalty-free home purchase requires all of the following: the purchase takes place at least 12 months after your first payment, a conveyancer or solicitor acts for you, you buy with a mortgage, and the price does not exceed £450,000. Withdrawals for other reasons before age 60, other than for terminal illness, incur a 25% charge.

The arithmetic of the 20% charge is worth spelling out. GOV.UK's example is £800 of savings plus a £200 bonus, making £1,000. A 25% charge on full withdrawal leaves £750, a £50 loss of your own money. At 20%, £1,000 of savings plus a £250 bonus makes £1,250, the charge is £250, and you get back exactly the £1,000 you put in. So from 29 October 2026, using a LISA towards a home above £450,000 broadly means forfeiting the bonus rather than losing capital, ignoring investment returns.

5. Pro tips

  • Prefer a flexible ISA. GOV.UK's example: after paying in £10,000 and withdrawing £3,000, a flexible ISA lets you pay in £13,000 more in the same tax year; a non-flexible ISA allows only £10,000. Flexibility is set by the provider, so ask before opening.
  • Always switch providers with an ISA transfer form. Money you withdraw yourself loses its tax-free allowance. GOV.UK sets limits of 15 working days for cash-to-cash transfers and 30 calendar days for other transfers. If a transfer overruns, complain to the provider, then to the Financial Ombudsman Service.
  • Split one year's allowance across several ISAs. GOV.UK's Example 3 shows £10,000 in one cash ISA, £3,000 in another and £7,000 in a stocks and shares ISA. You can lock in one bank's fixed rate while keeping an easy-access account elsewhere.
  • No need to abandon a LISA if you are buying above £450,000. At 20%, your capital is broadly intact. The reduced charge applies only to eligible withdrawals on or after 29 October 2026 and is not retrospective.
  • Crypto ETNs have moved out of stocks and shares ISAs. According to GOV.UK, from 6 April 2026 cryptoasset ETNs not already held there must sit in an Innovative Finance ISA.

6. Common misconceptions

  • "From 2027 I can only put £12,000 into a cash ISA." Budget 2026 withdrew the plan, and the regulations are being revoked. Articles from mid-2026 still describe the old rules, so check publication dates.
  • "I can take money out and put it back without using allowance." Only in a flexible ISA.
  • "I must close my ISA when I leave the UK." You may keep it. You just cannot pay in, and you must tell the provider.
  • "ISA income goes on my Self Assessment return." GOV.UK says you do not need to declare ISA interest, income or gains.
  • "I can open a First Time Buyer ISA now." It is planned for April 2029 and still needs legislation.

FAQ

I arrived in the UK a few months ago. Can I open an ISA?

What counts is whether you are UK tax resident, not how long you have been in the country. If you meet the SRT and are 18 or over, you can open one. GOV.UK does not pro-rate the annual allowance by month of arrival.

What is the "new ISA incentive" in Budget 2026?

The Budget 2026 OOTLAR says only that the government will work with industry on an incentive for people investing in a stocks and shares ISA for the first time. No design has been published. Hargreaves Lansdown expects it to be added to the existing stocks and shares ISA rather than launched as a new product, but that is the firm's view, not government policy.

What happens to my Help to Buy ISA?

HMRC's newsletter says Help to Buy ISA balances can be transferred in full into a First Time Buyer ISA between 6 April 2029 and 1 December 2030, outside the annual subscription limit.
