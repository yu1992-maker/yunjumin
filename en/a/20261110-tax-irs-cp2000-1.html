Received an IRS CP2000? How to respond when your income does not match

Nov 10, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

A CP2000 is a proposed adjustment, not a bill. The 30-day deadline, how to agree or disagree, what to attach and what happens if you ignore it.

A CP2000 is not a bill. It is an IRS proposed adjustment issued because income reported by banks, employers or brokers does not match your return. Check the response date on the notice, generally 30 days from the notice date (60 days if you live outside the US), and reply in one of three ways: agree, partly agree or disagree. Do not ignore it.

What a CP2000 is

  • The IRS describes it as a proposal, not a bill; it may increase your tax, produce a refund or leave it unchanged (Topic 652).
  • The series includes CP2000, CP2000A, CP2000B, CP2000C, CP2000D and CP2000E (IRS explainer).
  • Common causes: amounts on 1099-INT, DIV, B, NEC, K, DA or W-2 omitted or under-reported; or a 1099-B showing proceeds without cost basis, which the IRS treats as zero basis.

First steps

  1. Check the date: the response date printed on the notice governs. Topic 652 says 30 days (60 abroad); some law-firm blogs say 60 days. Sources differ, so use the date on your notice.
  2. Match line by line: the notice lists each payer and amount the IRS received. Compare them with your 1099s, W-2s and broker annual statements.
  3. See what the IRS holds: if forms are missing, request a Wage and Income Transcript through your IRS account.
  4. Decide which case applies (see the table), then respond.

Three ways to respond

SituationWhat to do
The IRS is rightComplete, sign and date the response form; both spouses sign on a joint return. Return it on time; pay online, wait for a bill, or request an installment agreement (fees may apply).
You disagree in part or in fullTick disagree on the response form, attach a signed explanation and supporting documents.
The IRS is right but you have other unreported items (income, credits, deductions)File Form 1040-X marked "CP2000" at the top and send it with the response form. Correct the same error in other years too, so penalties do not keep accruing.

If you agree and have nothing else to report, you do not need to amend your return (IRS).

Writing a disagreement letter

The following reflects common practice among law firms and tax services, not a mandatory IRS format:

  • Include the notice number, tax year, your name and the last four digits of your SSN, with no unnecessary extra information.
  • Answer item by item; address each disputed amount separately rather than saying "all wrong".
  • Accept the parts the IRS has right and contest only the rest.
  • Attach third-party documents such as broker cost-basis reports, trade confirmations and bank statements. Self-made spreadsheets carry little weight.

Typical scenarios

  • Stock or crypto sales without basis: attach the broker's cost-basis report or purchase records showing the real gain is far below gross proceeds.
  • Income belonging to someone else: for a nominee 1099, identify the actual recipient.
  • An incorrect 1099: ask the payer for a corrected form and attach it.
  • Identity theft: include Form 14039 with your response.

Sending your response

  • Online: the IRS Document Upload Tool (JPG, PNG, PDF) using the access code on the notice.
  • Fax: the number on your notice (for example 877-477-0583 for Austin; use your own notice).
  • Mail: the address at the top left of the notice. Keep copies and use tracked mail.

More time or professional help

  • You can call before the deadline or ask for more time with your response. Some firms say the IRS usually grants one 30-day extension; that is a blog claim, and IRS pages do not state a number.
  • A CPA, EA or attorney can act for you with Form 2848.
  • The Taxpayer Advocate Service and Low Income Taxpayer Clinics can also help.

Interest, penalties and ignoring the notice

  • Interest: runs from the original due date of the return (regardless of extensions) to 30 days after the notice date, and keeps accruing if unpaid. Paying within 30 days stops new interest and may avoid additional penalties (Topic 652).
  • Penalties: IRS pages say only that penalties may apply and may not appear on the notice. The often-cited 20% accuracy penalty rate does not appear on the two IRS pages we checked; rely on your notice.
  • No response: the IRS issues a Statutory Notice of Deficiency (CP3219A). TAS says you then have 90 days (150 if abroad) to petition the US Tax Court; the deadline cannot be extended and talks with the IRS do not pause it (TAS).

FAQ

Does a CP2000 mean I am being audited?

Not in the traditional sense. It is an automated document-matching review; the IRS usually wants an explanation or payment, but it still cannot be ignored.

If I agree, should I pay first?

You can pay online, or sign the response and wait for a bill. Paying within 30 days limits new interest; if you cannot pay, request an installment plan.

I cannot find my cost records. What now?

Ask the broker for historical confirmations and annual statements; request a Wage and Income Transcript for missing forms. If records remain incomplete, have a CPA estimate and communicate with the IRS. Do not invent figures.
