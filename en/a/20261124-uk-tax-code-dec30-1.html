UK tax bills under £3,000: file by December 30 to pay through your code

Nov 24, 20266 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Collected over 12 months in 2027-28, and the base for payments on account drops too. HMRC late interest is 8% from today.

File your 2025/26 Self Assessment return online by 11:59pm on December 30, 2026, owe less than £3,000, and HMRC will automatically collect the bill through your 2027-28 tax code over 12 months. The less noticed effect comes second: an amount that goes into the code is removed from the base for payments on account, so many people also escape the first advance payment on January 31. File on December 31 and both advantages are gone.

This article relies on the GOV.UK payment and deadline pages, HMRC's SA100 and SA150 (2026), SA110 Notes (2026), the Self Assessment Manual and the PAYE Manual. Checked on November 24, 2026. Rates are for England, Wales and Northern Ireland; Scotland differs.

1. Three conditions, all required

GOV.UK lists them: you owe less than £3,000; you already pay tax through PAYE as an employee or company pensioner; and the return arrives by October 31 on paper or December 30 online. Meet all three and the process is automatic. It is opting out that takes action, by marking box 2 in the "not paid enough tax" section of the SA100.

You can still be excluded. The PAYE Manual (PAYE12070, PAYE98005) and GOV.UK set three gates: not enough PAYE income to collect from; tax exceeding 50% of that income; and tax more than double the usual amount. SAM141001 is blunt: the system estimates the most the code can collect, and if you owe more than that, nothing is transferred. There is no partial coding.

The rate change follows the Bank of England, which raised base rate from 3.75% to 4.00% on November 5, 2026. HMRC charges base rate plus four points on late payments and pays base rate minus one, now 3.00%, on repayments. The gap between owing and overpaying is five points.

2. The valuable part: payments on account fall away

You make payments on account when last year's Self Assessment tax reached £1,000 and tax at source did not exceed 80% of the total. Each is half the bill, due January 31 and July 31. Page 26 of the SA110 Notes gives the formula: A356 = A340 minus (A330 + A333 + A334 + A353), and A353 is the "amount that can be coded out". If A356 is below £1,000, nothing is due. SAM141010 confirms that payments on account are automatically reduced to reflect the amount transferred to PAYE.

Take a first-time filer with a £40,000 salary and £9,000 of rental profit, and no other income. PAYE has collected £5,486; tax of £1,800 (£9,000 × 20%) remains. Total tax is £7,286, of which 75.3% was deducted at source, short of 80%. The table assumes box 3 of the SA100 is marked, so 2026/27 tax on the rent is not collected in-year.

One week changes the cash position by £3,000. It is timing, not forgiveness: tax on the 2026/27 rent is still settled by January 31, 2028, or coded again with the next return.

What the code looks like: under PAYE12080 the system converts the debt into reduced allowances at your own rate. At 20%, £1,800 equals £9,000 of allowances, so 1257L becomes roughly 357L. At 40% it is £4,500, or roughly 807L. A sharp fall in the code is no cause for alarm; check the "underpayment" line on the notice first.

3. Typical refusals

  • Salary too low. On £20,000 the PAYE tax is £1,486. A £1,800 bill would more than double it, so none of it is coded and the whole is due January 31. PAYE12070 calls the doubling rule a non-statutory safeguard that HMRC may override at your or your agent's request once you understand the effect on take-home pay. The 50% limit has no such override.
  • Paying part to get under £3,000. GOV.UK and SAM141010 both rule this out. A £3,200 bill with £300 paid is still judged as £3,200.
  • An unpaid earlier payment on account. SAM141010 defines the balance as the balancing charge plus outstanding payments on account, capped at £2,999.99. An unpaid July 31, 2026 instalment counts.
  • Class 2 National Insurance. It cannot be coded and must be paid by January 31, 2027, or benefit entitlement may suffer.
  • Employment ended. SAM121546 says a PAYE source that has ceased cannot be used. Anyone leaving a job at year end should set the cash aside.

4. Advanced techniques

  • Use genuine reliefs to get below the line. SAM141010 says an online amendment received by December 30 that cuts the balance from £3,000 or more to £2,999.99 or less is transferred automatically. If you omitted higher-rate pension contributions or Gift Aid, amending now may save tax and unlock instalments. The claim must be real; this is different from part payment.
  • Decide boxes 2 and 3 separately. Box 2 governs the 2025/26 debt. Box 3 governs current-year 2026/27 tax on savings, property, casual income and the High Income Child Benefit Charge, which HMRC tries to collect in-year; SA150 says it normally does not where such income exceeds £10,000. Leave both blank and current tax may be taken in the rest of 2026/27, followed by the old debt in 2027-28. Marking only box 3 keeps the instalments but means one payment on January 31, 2028.
  • Two kinds of coding. SAM141045 describes a separate scheme for overdue debts: a sliding limit from £3,000 (pay under £30,000) to £17,000 (£90,000 and above), which can be split and spread over two years, with interest running from the original due date. The December 30 route does not use the scale, is always under £3,000 and is collected within one tax year. One is enforcement; the other is ordinary payment.
  • If you owe £3,000 or more. HMRC's December 9, 2025 release says debts up to £30,000 can go into a self-service Time to Pay plan, but only after filing. Filing early costs nothing: payment is still due January 31.
  • Reduce payments on account if income falls. Where coding is unavailable and 2026/27 income will drop, claim a reduction in boxes 10 and 11 of the SA110 or online. Estimate too low and interest applies to the shortfall.

5. Common misconceptions

  • "Any time before New Year." The online cut-off is December 30. SAM141001 says later returns must not be manually coded out.
  • "You have to phone." It is automatic; only refusal needs a mark on the form.
  • "Deferring is being in arrears." Once transferred, the January 31 balance is nil. By contrast, £1,800 neither coded nor paid accrues about £71 of interest by July 31, 2027 (8.00%, 181 days), plus a 5% penalty of £90 at 30 days.
  • "It can be spread over years." PAYE12070 says Self Assessment debts are collected in one tax year only; anything longer means Time to Pay.

Questions

I arrived recently and registered after October 5. Is there time?

GOV.UK says HMRC will give a separate filing deadline, three months from its letter or email, but tax is still due January 31, 2027. The coding date does not move: it is December 30. Without a UTR you cannot file online, so keep the cash ready.

What if I change jobs?

The deduction follows the code, and the new employer applies whatever HMRC issues. SAM141010 says any amount not collected in the year is picked up in the next Self Assessment return.

How do I know it worked?

Check the statement in your Self Assessment account. SAM141010 says it shows "Included in PAYE Code for Year yy/yy". If the line is missing, plan to pay by January 31.

Reminder: GOV.UK and HMRC prevail. For foreign income, FIG claims or several jobs, consult a UK chartered tax adviser or accountant.
