Why ClearScore and Credit Karma Show Different UK Credit Scores

Nov 9, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

ClearScore uses Equifax data on a 1,000-point scale; Credit Karma uses TransUnion on a 710-point scale. Different data and formulas mean the scores cannot be compared directly.

ClearScore and Credit Karma show different scores because they draw on different credit reference agencies: ClearScore displays Equifax data and Credit Karma displays TransUnion data. Each agency holds its own record of you and scores it with its own formula and its own maximum, so the numbers should not be expected to match. Do not compare a 700 on ClearScore with a 700 on Credit Karma.

First, know whose score you are looking at

The UK has no single national credit score. There are three main credit reference agencies (CRAs): Experian, Equifax and TransUnion. ClearScore and Credit Karma are not CRAs; they present one agency's data to you.

The ClearScore 1,000, Credit Karma 710 and Experian 1,250 figures come from Experian's official guide. Credit Karma's own article also gives 710, and cites 999 for Experian and 1,000 for Equifax. Because sources differ on Experian's maximum, we suggest going by what Experian's website currently shows. Another source says TransUnion's old and new scales have different ceilings (710 old, 999 new); this is also disputed, so go by the scale shown in your app.

Why the same person can score so differently

1. Different data

Lenders do not report to all three agencies. Experian's guide states plainly that lenders do not necessarily report to every agency, and updates may arrive at different times. One source gives the example of energy bill payments reported to one agency while credit card payments go to the other two. The three reports therefore differ by design.

2. Different formulas

Credit Karma writes that each agency calculates scores with a slightly different formula; factors such as recent missed payments or credit utilisation may be weighted differently.

3. Different scales

With different maximums, the numbers naturally diverge. One third-party example shows the same person scoring 872, 743 and 598 at the three agencies, each rated "good" on its own scale. The example illustrates the effect but says nothing about your own case. Experian's guide also calls large apparent gaps "completely normal."

How to read your scores

  1. Identify the agency first. Check whether the app uses Equifax, TransUnion or Experian.
  2. Compare only with yourself. Within one app, watch whether your score rises or falls and which band (Good, Excellent) it sits in; do not compare numbers across apps.
  3. Check all three. For a full picture, look at ClearScore (Equifax), Credit Karma (TransUnion) and Experian.
  4. Look for errors. Wrong addresses, names, old accounts or incorrect missed payments should be disputed with the relevant agency.
  5. Investigate unusually large gaps. One source suggests that an abnormally large difference is worth checking against the report contents.

What lenders actually look at

Experian's guide says lenders may check any or all of the three agencies and usually combine that with application details, income and their own records; the decision is theirs. If you are refused, you can ask which agency's report was used, and the lender must tell you. In other words, the score in your app is a guide, not the number the lender sees.

New arrivals with no UK credit history

  • Newcomers may have thin files at every agency; a low or sparse score reflects little data, not bad credit.
  • Keep your name and address consistent across the electoral register (if eligible), bank accounts and phone contracts so agencies can match you to your records.
  • Note: this section describes general practice; there is no single official statement of each agency's weightings, and we found no precise figures to cite.

Update frequency: sources differ

Sources disagree on how often the apps refresh: some say both update weekly, others that Credit Karma's free tier updates monthly, and Credit Karma's own article does not say. Check the explanation inside each app for the exact frequency.

FAQ

Which score is more "accurate"?

Neither is more accurate or less genuine. They are different agencies' results from their own data and formulas. Lenders choose which agency to use, so it is worth checking each.

ClearScore says Excellent but Credit Karma says Good. Does it matter?

Usually not, because the scales and bands differ. What matters is whether the reports contain errors, missed payments or unexpected accounts. If the gap is large, compare the two reports line by line.

Does checking my own score lower it?

The sources we consulted this time did not address this clearly, so we draw no conclusion. Generally, checking your own file and a lender's application search are different in nature, but rely on each agency's official explanation.
