Standard Mileage or Actual Expenses for the Self-Employed

Nov 7, 20263 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Schedule C vehicle rules, the two 2026 mileage rates, first-year choice limits and record keeping.

Choose the standard mileage rate if you want simplicity, drive a modest car or cover many miles. Actual expenses are worth calculating when the car is costly to buy, fuel, insure or repair and is driven little. The key rule for a car you own: if you did not choose standard mileage in the first year of business use, you cannot use it later.

1. The two methods

Standard mileageActual expenses
CalculationBusiness miles × the IRS rateTotal vehicle cost × business-use share
CoversOperating costs, including depreciationFuel, oil, repairs, tires, insurance, registration, licences, depreciation or lease payments
Also deductibleParking, tolls, business share of loan interestParking, tolls
RecordsMainly a mileage logMileage log and all receipts

The business share is normally business miles divided by total miles.

2. 2026 rates: two halves

The 2026 rate changes mid-year, according to the IRS rates page:

PeriodBusinessMedicalCharity
January 1 to June 3072.5 cents20.5 cents14 cents
July 1 to December 3176 cents23.5 cents14 cents

Many articles from January give only 72.5 cents and are out of date. Media reports attribute the second-half change to fuel prices. We found no official figure for the depreciation component in the second half and so do not quote one.

Example: 6,000 business miles in the first half and 5,000 in the second. 6,000 × 0.725 + 5,000 × 0.76 = 4,350 + 3,800 = $8,150.

3. How to decide

  1. Is this the first year of business use? To keep the mileage option you must choose it that year.
  2. Compare two numbers: the mileage amount, and actual cost times business share.
  3. Look at price and depreciation. On a new or expensive car depreciation may be large, but it is capped.
  4. Consider the paperwork. Actual expenses require every receipt for the year.
  5. Think ahead. Moving from mileage to actual is allowed; the reverse is not if the first year used actual.

4. IRS restrictions

  • Owned cars: choose standard mileage in the first business year and you may choose either method each year after.
  • Leased cars: once standard mileage is chosen it applies for the whole lease, including renewals.
  • Not available if five or more cars are used at the same time, if the car has had MACRS, Section 179, special depreciation or a method other than straight line, or if a lease was previously reported on actual expenses.
  • Switching from mileage to actual: while the car is not fully depreciated, use straight line over the remaining useful life.
  • First year on actual: special depreciation must be claimed unless you elect out.
  • The mileage choice is made by the return due date, including extensions, and cannot be revoked (Publication 463).

5. Depreciation caps

Publication 463 (2025) gives these caps for passenger cars placed in service in 2025:

YearWith special depreciationWithout
1$20,200$12,200
2$19,600$19,600
3$11,800$11,800
4 onward$7,060$7,060

Reduce them by the business share where there is personal use. We have not verified the 2026 caps; see the current Publication 463. Section 179 requires business use above 50%, and heavy SUVs face a separate $31,300 limit (2025).

6. Records

  • For each trip: date, start and end points, business purpose, miles.
  • Odometer readings at the start and end of the year.
  • For actual expenses: all receipts for fuel, repairs, insurance and registration.
  • Reporting: Schedule C for sole proprietors, with vehicle details also on the relevant part of Form 4562 (follow that year's instructions).
  • Retention: generally three years from filing; six if more than 25% of income was omitted (IRS Topic 305). After a sale, keep records through the limitation period for that year.

FAQ

I used actual expenses last year. Can I switch to mileage?

For an owned car, not if the first year did not use standard mileage. If it did, you may switch between methods afterwards.

On standard mileage, can I deduct fuel and insurance too?

No, the rate includes operating costs. Parking, tolls and the business share of loan interest are separate.

Is the 2026 rate 72.5 or 76 cents?

Both: 72.5 cents for January 1 to June 30 and 76 cents for July 1 to December 31. Your log must be able to split the two halves.

Verify with the IRS. For leases, multiple vehicles, heavy SUVs or Section 179, consult a CPA or attorney.
