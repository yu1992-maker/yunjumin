Changed Your Name or Passport in Canada? Updating Your Credit Reports

Nov 11, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

A new passport number does not affect your credit report, but a legal name change must be sent separately to Equifax and TransUnion, then to your lenders. Steps and pitfalls.

if only your passport number changed and your name did not, your credit reports usually need no update. Canadian official and consumer-education sources list name, date of birth, current and previous addresses, and in some cases phone number, employer and SIN (sources differ); none we found mentions a passport number. If your legal name has changed and your report still shows the old one, submit proof of the change separately to Equifax Canada and TransUnion Canada, then notify banks, card issuers and other creditors.

Note: some Equifax and TransUnion pages could not be opened directly, so we could not verify every form and document requirement. Unconfirmed points are flagged below; check both bureaus' websites on the day you apply.

Which situation are you in?

SituationUpdate credit reports?

Why both bureaus

Equifax and TransUnion keep independent files, and the personal and account data may differ. A third-party guide notes that once Equifax is updated, TransUnion does not follow automatically, and a name mismatch can cause identity-verification problems with lenders. Notify both yourself.

Step 1: get both reports and see where you stand

  1. Request reports from Equifax Canada and TransUnion Canada (free by mail or online; check each site for access and fees).
  2. Check spelling, former names, addresses and date of birth.
  3. Note which accounts use the old name and which the new, and record each creditor's name for later.

Step 2: correct your file with Equifax Canada

  1. Equifax Canada has a Personal Info Dispute Form (PDF, produced February 2025) on its dispute page at equifax.ca.
  2. According to our search results, the form has a "name update" option and requires proof of address dated within 90 days showing your legal name and current address, such as a utility bill or financial statement. One Canadian user reports being asked by phone or mail for copies of two IDs (front and back) in the new name plus proof of the change. This is second-hand; Equifax's current requirements prevail.
  3. Common proof includes a marriage certificate or change-of-name certificate. Borrowell's help page says Equifax requires such official documentation.
  4. Timing: sources differ. Borrowell says it takes a few weeks; another source says 7–10 business days after all documents arrive; a Wayves user reports up to 30 days. Allow a month.

Step 3: dispute with TransUnion Canada

  1. TransUnion says personal-information errors such as name can be disputed free of charge online, by phone or by mail; a name change requires a change-of-name or marriage certificate.
  2. TransUnion has a Credit Investigation Request Form (PDF) on transunion.ca.
  3. TransUnion verifies with the reporting institution. Per Hardbacon, investigations are usually completed within 30 days of receipt, but a dispute does not guarantee a change.
  4. If the information stands after verification, you may add a free consumer statement of up to 100 words (200 in Saskatchewan). This requires a written request with at least two accepted IDs, stating your name, current address and date of birth, signed.

Step 4: notify creditors

Account information is reported by creditors: banks, card issuers, phone carriers. If you only fix the bureau file, the old name may reappear at the next monthly update. Hardbacon also advises asking creditors to check their records and send updated information to the bureaus, which may speed things up.

  • Banks, credit cards, loans and mortgages: first.
  • Phone, internet and utility accounts: some also report.
  • Insurers, employer, CRA, provincial health plan: may not affect credit, but mismatches cause other problems.

Equifax's US guidance also lists notifying banks and creditors of your new legal name as a required step. That page is American; the logic applies, but follow Canadian procedures.

Step 5: re-check after a month

  1. Pull both reports again.
  2. Confirm the primary name is updated and the old name appears only as a former name (old names are commonly retained as aliases, which is routine; we found no explicit Canadian official statement on this).
  3. Contact any creditor still reporting the old name.
  4. If unhappy with a result, ask TransUnion to escalate; federally regulated financial institutions must have a complaint-handling process.

FAQ

My passport number changed. Must my credit report change?

Not if your name is the same. The Canadian sources we found do not include passport numbers in the personal section. If a lender used your passport for ID, simply ask it to update its records.

Once Equifax updates, will TransUnion follow?

No. The files are independent; submit to each.

How long does it take?

Sources range from 7–10 business days to several weeks or 30 days. TransUnion usually finishes investigations within 30 days. If the old name returns, a creditor probably has not updated; go back to that creditor.

Reminder: compiled from public sources. Forms, ID requirements and timelines are set by Equifax Canada, TransUnion Canada and the Government of Canada (FCAC). For identity theft, credit refusals, immigration or legal name-change complications, consult a CPA or lawyer.
