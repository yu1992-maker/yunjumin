Canada: adding a consumer statement when a credit dispute fails

Nov 8, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

A free statement on Equifax and TransUnion reports: length limits, addresses, wording and how to escalate.

If you dispute an item with Equifax or TransUnion in Canada and remain dissatisfied after the investigation, you can add a free consumer statement to your credit report so that lenders see your explanation. The statement does not change or delete the record; it sits beside the disputed item. Length limits vary by bureau, and sources disagree on them.

What a consumer statement is, and is not

  • It is a short explanation in your own words attached to your credit file. Lenders and others who view the report may take it into account.
  • It is not a correction. Accurate records stay as they are, and Equifax's education page says it is unlikely to affect your score.
  • Cost: free, according to both the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC) and Equifax.

When to use it

  1. You have already filed a dispute with the bureau.
  2. The investigation has finished and the information was confirmed as reported, or the dispute was not resolved.
  3. You still believe it is wrong, or it is accurate but needs context, such as a late payment during unemployment, the pandemic or illness.

FCAC lists this among the options when you are unhappy with the outcome. If the bureau will not change the record, add the statement and pursue the escalation channels below at the same time.

Equifax's dispute page has at times carried a notice about a Canada Post strike, so check how requests are being accepted before mailing. Confirm the length limit as well, to avoid truncation.

Steps

  1. Keep the dispute file: forms, replies, dates and account numbers.
  2. Read the outcome: identify exactly which item was verified as accurate.
  3. Write the statement within the limit (see below).
  4. Prepare identification: two copies, with name and address matching the report.
  5. Send it: use trackable mail and keep a copy. For TransUnion, check whether the online service accepts it.
  6. Pull the report again later: confirm the statement appears against the right item. Each bureau needs its own submission.

How to write it

  • Facts only, one point: which account, which entry, your position.
  • Keep the tone even. No insults and no long narrative.
  • Leave out full account numbers and your SIN.
  • Mention any resolution, such as repayment or a written agreement with the creditor.
Example: "Regarding XX Bank credit card ending 1234: I dispute the late payment entry. I filed a dispute with the bureau in [month] 2026 with written confirmation from the bank. The delay resulted from a period of unemployment in 2026. I have since returned to work and repaid the balance. Supporting documents are available on request."

Equifax's own example takes the same approach: explain circumstances beyond your control, then what you did about the late payment.

If the statement achieves nothing

  • Escalate: FCAC suggests asking to speak with a supervisor at the bureau.
  • Complain to the bank: federally regulated institutions must have a complaint procedure, with an external complaints body beyond it.
  • Complain about the bureau: start with the bureau, then use the process on the ISED website, and if necessary contact provincial or territorial consumer affairs. FCAC notes that the federal government does not regulate credit bureaus.
  • Some commercial sites claim a bureau must investigate within 30 days. We could not confirm that on an official page, so do not rely on it.

Questions

Will a statement raise my score?

Essentially no. It changes no record. Equifax says it is unlikely to affect the score; its purpose is to give context to a human reviewer.

Do I need one for each bureau?

Yes. Equifax says TransUnion must be contacted separately. The files are independent and the limits differ.

Can I remove or correct it?

Equifax says you may remove it at any time. TransUnion accepts a written request for early removal; otherwise it stays six years. Confirm the channel by phone or on the website.

Reminder: official websites prevail, and provincial rules and length limits may change. For major loans, debt disputes or identity theft, consult a CPA or lawyer.
