UK Voluntary NI From Abroad: The 10-Year Test and the 5 April 2027 Deadline

Nov 23, 20266 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Class 2 at £3.65 is gone for time abroad from 2026 to 2027. Class 3 costs £18.40 and needs 10 years. Past years keep the three-year test.

From the 2026 to 2027 tax year, people abroad can no longer buy UK National Insurance years at the Class 2 rate of £3.65 a week. Class 3 at £18.40 a week is all that remains, £767 a year more, and new applicants need 10 years in the UK rather than three. The change is not retroactive: gaps for 2025 to 2026 and earlier are still governed by the three-year test, and eligible people can still use Class 2 for them. Two clocks stop on April 5, 2027: the last day to fill 2020 to 2021, and the deadline for transitional protection.

This article draws on the GOV.UK voluntary National Insurance guide, HMRC's policy paper on contributions abroad from April 6, 2026, HMRC manual pages NIM33202 to NIM33205, and DWP pages on the new State Pension. Sources were checked on November 23, 2026. It is aimed at people who worked in the UK and moved to the US, Canada, China or elsewhere, and at foreign residents planning to leave.

Either test is enough. GOV.UK lists qualifying contributions as Class 1, 2 or 3 paid or treated as paid while in the UK; Class 1 or 2 paid abroad under a social security agreement; Class 1 paid by posted workers for the first 52 weeks abroad; and Class 2 paid by volunteer development workers.

NIM33204 gives the example of Andrew, who came from New Zealand, worked in the UK for 15 months and left for Brazil for 24 months. Without 10 continuous years or enough contributions, he cannot pay for his time in Brazil.

2. Worked example: what £956.80 buys

The full new State Pension is £241.30 a week. People whose record began after April 2016 need 35 qualifying years for the full rate and usually at least 10 for any. One year is therefore worth about £241.30 ÷ 35 = £6.89 a week, or roughly £358.50 a year (52 weeks, 2026 to 2027 rates, before tax and future changes).

The £767 gap matches HMRC's policy paper. The arithmetic assumes each extra year adds pension. Someone who already has 35 years, was contracted out, or will never reach 10 years may gain nothing. GOV.UK says plainly that voluntary contributions do not always increase your State Pension.

Scenario: a person worked in the UK for eight years, September 2015 to August 2023, then moved to the US and works there. For gaps in 2023 to 2024, 2024 to 2025 and 2025 to 2026, they meet the three-year test, worked in the UK immediately before leaving and work abroad, so Class 2 is open on application. From 2026 to 2027, with eight continuous years and fewer than 10 qualifying years, they generally no longer qualify. For this person the past years matter more than the new ones.

3. Deadlines: two clocks, one date

From July 2026HMRC writes to former Class 2 payers that their arrangement has ended

Transitional protection keeps the three-year test for existing applicants. GOV.UK says all three conditions must be met: you applied by April 5, 2026 to pay Class 2 or 3 for 2024 to 2025 or 2025 to 2026; you pay or have paid those contributions by April 5, 2027; and you apply by that date for Class 3 for 2026 to 2027. NIM33205 joins the last two with "or," which does not match GOV.UK's "all." The safe course is to satisfy all three.

Existing Class 3 payers abroad can continue without reapplying. But the manual warns that a person who returns to the UK to work, or keeps paying Class 3 in the UK, and later leaves and applies again faces the 10-year test.

Rates depend on timing too. The previous year for Class 2 and the previous two for Class 3 are charged at their original rates; earlier years at the current one. Class 3 was £17.45 in 2024 to 2025 and £17.75 in 2025 to 2026, so those years are slightly cheaper.

4. Advanced moves

  • Get the forecast first. The State Pension forecast and NI record show each gap, its cost and whether filling it helps. If you are within six months of State Pension age or past it, contact the International Pension Centre.
  • Use Class 2 for past years where you can. For 2025 to 2026 and earlier, it costs about one fifth of Class 3. The key facts are work in the UK immediately before leaving and work abroad, so keep employer and date evidence.
  • Refused between November 2017 and April 2019? Ask again. GOV.UK acknowledges that HMRC guidance in that period wrongly required all conditions instead of one. People who did not apply or were refused may be able to pay at the original rates.
  • Use social security agreements to reach 10 years. Contribution periods in the EEA, Switzerland and agreement countries can count toward the 10-year minimum, though the amount is based only on UK years. The US is on GOV.UK's agreement list. Canadian residence follows different rules and generally requires you to live in the UK now.
  • Price in where you will retire. The State Pension rises each year only in the EEA, Switzerland and countries whose agreement allows it. The US is on the list. Canada and New Zealand have agreements but no increases. Mainland China and Hong Kong are not listed. The same year can be worth very different sums.

5. Common misconceptions

  • "Class 2 is gone." Only for periods abroad from 2026 to 2027. Self-employed people in the UK with profits under £7,105 can still pay it voluntarily.
  • "Ten years of visits is enough." The residence test requires continuity. The contribution test looks at qualifying years, not time spent.
  • "Pay now, apply later." GOV.UK says not to pay until HMRC's letter gives you the amount and a payment reference.
  • "More years is always better." Beyond 35 years, or where contracted out, extra years may bring nothing.

FAQ

How do I apply?

Usually with online form CF83. You need your NI number, the date you left, employment before leaving, your address and work abroad, and photo ID. People told by HMRC that their Class 2 arrangement has ended use the dedicated Class 3 form published on July 14, 2026.

What if I have lost my NI number?

Use GOV.UK's "find your National Insurance number" page first, then submit CF83. Never use someone else's number.

Does Class 3 count for working-age benefits?

No. HMRC's policy paper says Class 3 builds only State Pension entitlement, unlike Class 2, which also counts toward New Style ESA.

Reminder: rely on GOV.UK and HMRC. Whether paying is worthwhile varies by person; consult an independent financial adviser or accountant in complex cases.
