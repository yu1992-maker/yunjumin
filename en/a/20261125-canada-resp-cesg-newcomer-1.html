RESP for Newcomers to Canada: The Age-15 Gate and the December 31 $1,000

Nov 25, 20266 min readYunjumin Forum editors

CESG room builds $500 per resident year; miss $2,000 by the end of the year a child turns 15 and the last two years pay nothing.

The Canada Education Savings Grant (CESG) pays 20% on RESP contributions. Room grows by $500 a year; with carry-forward, up to $1,000 can be paid in one year on $5,000 contributed; the lifetime cap is $7,200. Newcomers tend to miss two lines. Room builds with years of Canadian residence, so pre-arrival years should not be counted on. And by December 31 of the year a child turns 15, either $2,000 must have been contributed, or at least $100 in each of four years. Otherwise contributions at 16 and 17 attract nothing.

This article relies on chapters 4, 5 and 10 of ESDC's RESP provider guide, InfoCapsule 12, the CRA's CESG and contribution pages, Canada.ca's education savings pages, and IRS Rev. Proc. 2020-17. Amounts are in Canadian dollars unless noted. Consulted November 25, 2026.

1. Three payments, three sets of rules

PaymentAmountIncome testCatch-up
Basic CESG20% of contributions; $500 a year, or $1,000 with carry-forwardNoneYes, to the end of the year the child turns 17
Additional CESG10% or 20% of the first $500, i.e. $50 or $1002026 family net income: 20% below $58,523; 10% up to $117,045No, lost each year
Canada Learning Bond (CLB)$500 in the first year, $100 a year after, to $2,000; no contribution needed$58,523 or less with one to three children (July 2026 to June 2027)Yes, for eligible past years

Basic and Additional CESG together cannot exceed $7,200. Official pages differ on the middle band: Canada.ca gives $58,523 to $117,045, while the CRA page prints $59,523 to $114,750 and then uses $117,045 as the ceiling. ESDC's determination governs.

The status tests differ too. Basic CESG requires a child who is resident in Canada when the contribution is made, holds a SIN, is named in an RESP and is 17 or younger; permanent residence is not on that list. Additional CESG and the CLB require a primary caregiver eligible for the Canada Child Benefit. A family on study or work permits that has not yet met the CCB's 18-month condition may therefore qualify for the basic grant alone. Whether a temporary resident child can obtain a SIN depends on the permit; confirm with Service Canada.

2. Do pre-arrival years count?

ESDC's guide says in one place that the grant accumulates "yearly from birth", and InfoCapsule 12 starts with the birth year, leading many articles to conclude that newcomers can catch up from birth. But the CRA says every child under 18 "who is a resident of Canada" accumulates $500, and section 5.3.1 of the guide says children do not build room while a move abroad changes their tax residency. Third-party guides such as silaws.com and bremo.io count resident years only. No official page we found answers the born-abroad case word for word. Plan on resident years, then ask the promoter to confirm actual room with ESDC.

One consequence is rarely stated. At $500 a year, $7,200 takes 14.4 years, or 15 resident calendar years. On that reading a child must arrive by the year of the third birthday to reach the cap on the basic grant alone. For later arrivals the true ceiling is resident years times $500, and the savings target should be scaled down from the usual $36,000.

3. Two arrival ages, worked through

Case one: born 2016, landed 2023, no RESP yet. On the resident-year reading, 2023 to 2026 gives $2,000 of room. The table assumes $5,000 a year and no Additional CESG.

YearCarried in + $500ContributionCESGRoom left
2026$1,500 + $500 = $2,000$5,000$1,000$1,000
2027$1,000 + $500 = $1,500$5,000$1,000$500
2028$500 + $500 = $1,000$5,000$1,000$0
2029 to 2033$500 a year$2,500 a year$500 a year$0

In total, $27,500 contributed and $5,500 granted: 11 resident years at $500. Anything above that earns no grant.

Case two: born 2011, landed 2025, turns 15 in 2026. December 31, 2026 is the only window.

  • Contribute $2,000 by year end: $400 for 2026, $600 left; $1,100 of room in 2027, so $5,000 earns $1,000; in 2028, $100 plus $500, so $3,000 earns $600. Total $2,000.
  • Fall short of $2,000, with no way to reach four years of $100: nothing on contributions in 2027 or 2028. Up to $2,000 is lost.

A child who lands in or after the year of the 16th birthday cannot have met the age-15 condition, because residence and a SIN must precede any contribution. By inference the CESG is out of reach; what remains is tax deferral and, for low-income families, the CLB.

Advanced techniques

  • Hold the age-15 gate at minimum cost. For children born in 2014 or later, $100 in each of four years starting with 2026, $400 in all, preserves eligibility at 16 and 17, provided it is not withdrawn.
  • The contribution date is what counts. Canada.ca uses the calendar year for eligibility, contributions and room. Grants usually arrive six to eight weeks later. Allow time for SIN validation.
  • Take the first $500 every year. Additional CESG is not carried forward. A $500 contribution can draw up to $200, or 40%. Income is normally from two years earlier, so 2026 looks to 2024; families first receiving the CCB between July and December may be assessed on the previous year.
  • Open an RESP for the CLB even with no money to contribute. The caregiver must have filed for each year claimed. From April 2028 the government will open plans automatically for eligible children born in 2024 or later with no RESP by age four; for older children it can be requested from 2028.
  • Grandparents abroad need not be subscribers. A subscriber needs a SIN. A gift to the parents, who open the plan, is simpler. All subscribers together are held to $50,000 per child.
  • Keep family plans to siblings. Additional CESG and the CLB can be paid only to individual plans or sibling-only family plans; adding others may force repayment.
  • US taxpayers. Rev. Proc. 2020-17 relieves eligible individuals of Forms 3520 and 3520-A for certain foreign education trusts, where local law limits contributions to US$10,000 a year or US$200,000 for life and income has been reported in the United States. The relief concerns section 6048 only; consider Form 8938, FBAR and US tax separately.

Common mistakes

  • "Withdrawing an over-contribution fixes it." Amounts above $50,000 bear 1% a month, reported on T1E-OVP. Withdrawal reduces the taxable excess, but the CRA still counts withdrawn sums toward the lifetime limit.
  • "A $20,000 deposit catches up everything." The grant is capped at $1,000 a year, matching $5,000.
  • "Grants continue after leaving." A non-resident child cannot receive contributions or build room. CESG and CLB cannot be paid out to a non-resident; accumulated income still can, for qualifying study.
  • "Studying in the US forfeits the grant." The guide notes that students abroad may remain resident in Canada. Residency at the time of payment decides.
  • "Everything can come out in September." Educational assistance payments are limited to $8,000 in the first 13 weeks of full-time study and $4,000 per 13 weeks part-time. Withdrawals of the subscriber's own contributions do not count.

FAQ

Are there provincial amounts?

Canada.ca lists a one-time $1,200 grant in British Columbia and a Quebec refundable credit with a $3,600 lifetime maximum. Check provincial conditions.

What if the child does not pursue further study?

The CESG is returned, contributions come back tax-free, and earnings may be taken as a taxable accumulated income payment or, if conditions are met, moved to the subscriber's or spouse's RRSP.

RESP, TFSA or RRSP first?

On grants alone, the slice of RESP contributions that draws the 20% match has a known return. Beyond the year's grantable amount, compare. This is not investment advice.
