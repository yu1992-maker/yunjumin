Amex 1/90 Rule Explained: How Soon Can You Reopen the Same Card

Nov 10, 20263 min readYunjumin Forum editors

1/90 means Amex approves the same card only once in 90 days. Separate limits apply: 1/5, 2/90 and the once-per-lifetime welcome bonus. Here is how they differ.

the Amex 1/90 rule means you will be approved for the same Amex card only once in any 90 days. It limits approvals, not bonus eligibility. Earning the welcome bonus on the same card again runs into a different rule: once per lifetime per card (loosened in recent years, but still not something to count on).

These rules govern different things

Amex rules are often lumped together, but they split into approval velocity and bonus eligibility. The names below are community shorthand, not official Amex policy names.

How 1/90 actually applies

  • It targets the same card. If you were approved for a card in January, applying for it again within 90 days usually results in denial.
  • Frugal Flyer notes it matters most to people chasing offers without lifetime language (NLL), since those are the only way to legitimately earn a bonus on the same card again.
  • A denial is not an approval, so it should not start the clock, according to most guides. A March 2026 FlyerTalk post claims a withdrawn application may also count, contradicting the mainstream view; accounts differ.

1/5 and 2/90: velocity for credit cards

  1. 1/5: one credit card approval in 5 days.
  2. 2/90: at most two credit card approvals in any 90 days.
  3. Charge cards are not subject to these two (Frugal Flyer), though several applications in a short span may trigger manual review.

Whether 2/90 combines personal and business cards is disputed: some sources say combined, while cardholder reports suggest separate counting. Some users have also reported two approvals on the same day, so these are not hard limits.

Can you earn the bonus again on the same card?

The traditional view: each Amex card's welcome bonus is once per lifetime; you can be approved again but without a bonus. Some guides say "lifetime" is sometimes treated as seven years; that is community lore, not official. Bankrate also notes that Amex does not always enforce it strictly and makes exceptions.

Thrifty Traveler reported on October 13, 2025 that:

  • The Platinum Card's 2021 terms said the offer was unavailable to those who have or have had the card; they now say applicants "may not be eligible", leaving room for case-by-case decisions.
  • The author closed a Platinum Card in December 2022 and was approved again in September 2025, earning 80,000 points after $12,000 of spending in 6 months.
  • Some readers were told they were ineligible. The article itself says it is too early to conclude Amex has dropped the rule, it is a single case, and the article contains affiliate content.
Conclusion: re-earning is now case-by-case. Do not assume it will work.

Pre-application checklist

  1. Use Amex's Apply with Confidence tool to preview eligibility; it does not cause a hard inquiry (per Thrifty Traveler).
  2. Read the offer terms for language excluding those who have or have had the card.
  3. Count credit card approvals in the past 90 days and past 5 days.
  4. Confirm you hold no more than 5 credit cards.
  5. If a pop-up says you are ineligible for the bonus, do not push ahead.

FAQ

1. After closing a card, can I reopen the same one once 90 days pass?

From the 1/90 standpoint, yes. The bonus depends on the lifetime rule and your eligibility; you may be approved without a bonus.

2. Are 1/90 and 2/90 the same?

No. 1/90 concerns the same card; 2/90 concerns total credit card approvals in 90 days.

3. Is the once-per-lifetime rule still in force?

Amex still references it, but the terms have softened; some people re-earn the bonus and others are refused. It is decided individually.
