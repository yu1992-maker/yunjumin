The BofA 2/3/4 rule: how many cards in two months is too many

Nov 8, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Two cards in 2 months, three in 12, four in 24: which cards count, what about business cards, and what to do if declined.

What people call BofA's "2/3 rule" is usually Bank of America's 2/3/4 rule for personal credit cards: roughly no more than 2 approvals in a rolling 2 months, 3 in 12 months and 4 in 24 months. A third card within two months is therefore likely to be declined. The rule is not published by the bank; it comes from statements by agents and community data points, and is presented here on that basis.

1. The rule

WindowMaximum approvals (BofA personal cards)
Rolling 2 months2
Rolling 12 months3
Rolling 24 months4

Doctor of Credit first reported it in September 2017 and updated the report on October 4 after confirmation from the bank: at most two cards in two months, three in 12 and four in 24. AwardWallet's October 2026 article gives the same figures and suggests planning around two months.

Sources disagree on the first window. Most say 2 months; a few, such as WalletHub, say two cards in 30 days. Two months is the safer assumption.

2. What counts

  • Only cards issued by BofA count, including co-branded cards (AwardWallet). Cards from other banks do not, unlike Chase's 5/24, which counts all issuers.
  • Business cards: there is no official statement. Data points cited by Doctor of Credit suggest business cards do not use up personal slots: someone who applied for a personal and a business card on the same day could still be approved for another personal card within two months. These are data points and can change.
  • Merrill cards: the Doctor of Credit article does not say. We found no reliable conclusion.

3. Two other rules that are easily confused

3/12 or 7/12 (based on your credit report)

According to AwardWallet, this looks at new accounts from any bank on your personal credit report. Without a BofA deposit account, approval odds drop noticeably at 3 or more new accounts in 12 months; with one, the threshold is around 7. Enforcement is inconsistent, and this too is community experience rather than official policy. Most business cards do not appear on personal reports and so generally do not matter here.

The 24-month offer restriction

Many BofA welcome offers state that you may not qualify if you have, or have had, the card in the past 24 months. AwardWallet points out that this turns on having held the card, not on when the bonus posted. Terms vary by offer, so read the current disclosure.

4. Pre-application check

  1. Count BofA personal cards opened in the past 24 months, including co-branded and closed cards.
  2. Count those in the past 2 and 12 months and compare with the table.
  3. Count new personal cards from all banks in the past 12 months against 3/12 or 7/12.
  4. Read the offer terms for a 24-month restriction.
  5. Try prequalification first. It does not affect your score, and it is not a guarantee.
  6. Apply through the original link or offer code, and do not submit several applications on the same day.

5. If you are declined or pending

  • "Pending" is not a denial. BofA may need more information. Give it time.
  • Call reconsideration. Doctor of Credit says an agent may make an exception based on your relationship, but the odds are low unless the agent sees a genuine need.
  • Wait for the window to roll. The most reliable course is to wait until the oldest card passes 2, 12 or 24 months.
  • Do not close other cards in advance. AwardWallet notes that BofA sometimes moves credit from an existing card to the new one without raising total credit; that is not a reason to close cards.

6. An approval can be reversed

Doctor of Credit reports cases in which BofA approved a card by mistake and closed it within days. Do not stake an important card on the edge of the rule.

Frequently asked questions

After two BofA cards in two months, is a third always declined?

Not always, but that is the likely result. An agent may have discretion; do not count on it.

Can cards from other banks push me over 2/3/4?

No. 2/3/4 counts only BofA cards. New cards elsewhere do affect softer rules such as 3/12 or 7/12.

Do business cards use a 2/3/4 slot?

There is no official answer. Existing data points suggest most do not, but this is unverified and may change.
