BofA secured card: is the deposit refundable, and what graduation takes

Nov 8, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

The deposit comes back on graduation or on closing a paid-off account. BofA publishes no threshold; here is what can be sourced.

Yes, it can be refunded. Bank of America (BofA) says on its website that it periodically reviews secured card accounts and, based on overall credit history and the relationship with the bank, may return the deposit to customers who qualify. Not everyone does. The other route is to pay the balance in full and close the account, after which the deposit is also returned. BofA publishes no score or number of months required to graduate, so what follows is limited to what can be found and sourced.

Bottom line: two ways to get the deposit back

RouteResultNotes
Review passed (graduation)Deposit returnedBofA reviews periodically, looking at credit history and the overall relationship
Pay off and closeDeposit returnedWalletHub mentions up to two billing cycles plus 10 days

The deposit earns no interest (Doctor of Credit's review of the BankAmericard Secured).

First, know which secured card you hold

BofA has had several secured cards with slightly different rules:

  • Customized Cash Rewards Secured (the "cash back secured" page): deposit of $200 to $5,000, no annual fee, standard variable APR of 27.74% on the website. Third-party sources list 27.74%, 28.24%, 29.24% and other figures; they disagree, so rely on your statement.
  • Unlimited Cash Rewards Secured: a Doctor of Credit headline describes 1.5% cash back and no annual fee.
  • BankAmericard Secured: Doctor of Credit's review lists a $39 annual fee. This is an older card that may no longer be open to new customers; check the website.

On deposit size, the website states a $200 minimum and $5,000 maximum. One Doctor of Credit article says $300, which conflicts with the website and is probably outdated. The credit limit depends on the deposit, income and ability to pay.

What graduation requires: no official rule, only experience

BofA's secured card page gives no specific conditions. What is available comes mainly from reviews and forums:

  • Time: Doctor of Credit says BofA used to require at least 12 months, that this has changed, and that many people now graduate sooner. One forum user was told by an agent that accounts can be considered from month 5, although the app mentioned a one-year review. Others report 9 to 12 months, or roughly 6 to 12. Accounts differ and there is no single standard.
  • Usage: in forum reports, some who paid in full each month asked again for early graduation and were approved.
  • Overall credit: the website refers to overall credit history, the relationship with the bank, and other cards and loans. Checking or savings accounts and payment history on other BofA cards may all be considered. No score cutoff could be found and we will not guess one.
The timelines above come mainly from individual forum posts and may not reflect current BofA policy.

Steps toward earlier graduation

  1. Pay on time every month, ideally in full, and avoid carrying high statement balances.
  2. Keep utilization low; do not run the card to its limit.
  3. After about 6 months (sources vary), ask through online chat or by phone whether the account can be reviewed.
  4. If declined, do not panic. One forum user applied again a week later and was approved. That is a single case and no guarantee. Ask first whether each request triggers a hard inquiry.
  5. The decision may arrive by mail or email; some were told to expect 5 to 7 days.

After graduation

  • The deposit is returned to your account.
  • The card may stay the same or be moved to another product (WalletHub says migration is possible; some forum users kept the same card, fee, APR and limit).
  • Doctor of Credit notes that a request for a higher limit may involve a hard inquiry.

If you only want the deposit back

  1. Pay off the entire balance, including unposted purchases.
  2. Call the number on the back of the card and ask how and when the deposit is refunded on closure.
  3. Think before closing. Shutting your oldest card affects account age and may lower your score. If it is your only card, your file becomes thin.

Frequently asked questions

How many months before graduation?

There is no official figure. The older answer was 12 months; some forum reports now say 5 months. Accounts differ.

Does graduation bring a new-account bonus?

The website says secured cards do not qualify for new-account bonuses or an introductory APR. Ask customer service about the rules for the upgraded card.

How long until the deposit arrives?

The website gives no number of days. WalletHub says a closure refund may take up to two billing cycles plus 10 days; we found no more authoritative figure.

Rely on official information. For complex situations such as multiple cards, credit repair or immigration status, consult a CPA, a lawyer or BofA customer service.
