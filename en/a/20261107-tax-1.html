Home Office Deduction for the Self-Employed: Simplified vs Regular Method

Nov 7, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

The simplified method allows $5 per square foot up to 300 square feet ($1,500); the regular method allocates actual costs on Form 8829. Conditions, comparison and how to choose.

Self-employed filers (Schedule C) can figure the home office deduction two ways. The simplified method allows $5 per square foot for up to 300 square feet, a maximum of $1,500. The regular method allocates actual costs such as rent or mortgage interest and utilities by the office's share of the home, using Form 8829. A small office in a low-cost home favors the simplified method; a large office or high housing costs usually favor the regular method, at the price of record-keeping and depreciation.

Eligibility is identical under both methods. The choice affects only the calculation, not whether you qualify.

First, confirm you qualify

  • The space must be used regularly and exclusively for the business.
  • It must be your principal place of business, a place where you meet clients, or a separate structure such as a studio or garage.
  • Exceptions: storage of inventory or product samples and qualifying day-care facilities need not be used exclusively.
  • W-2 employees cannot claim it. The IRS notes that employees' miscellaneous itemized deductions were eliminated from 2018.
  • The deduction cannot exceed the gross income from the home business less business expenses unrelated to the home (the income limit).

How to choose: four steps

  1. Measure. Office area divided by total home area gives the percentage; the IRS also accepts other reasonable methods.
  2. Compute the simplified method. Area (up to 300) × $5. Example: 120 sq ft = $600; 300 sq ft or more = $1,500.
  3. Compute the regular method. Total the year's rent (or mortgage interest, property tax, insurance, utilities, repairs, depreciation), multiply by the percentage, then add direct expenses for the office itself at 100%.
  4. Pick the larger, weighing the effort. When the gap is small, the simplified method is easier and leaves no depreciation.

Illustration (hypothetical figures): with $30,000 of annual housing costs and a 15% office, the regular method gives about $4,500, well above $1,500. With the same 15% but only $8,000 of costs, it gives about $1,200, so the simplified method wins.

Renters versus owners

  • Renters: Under the regular method, enter the year's rent on Form 8829 line 19 and apply the percentage. There is no depreciation, so the burden is light.
  • Owners: The regular method covers a share of mortgage interest, property tax, insurance, utilities and depreciation. Line 37 of Form 8829 uses the home's basis (or value, if lower), excluding land. The instructions give monthly percentages, for example 2.461% if first used in January 2025 and 0.107% if in December.
  • If you take the standard deduction, the business share of mortgage interest and property tax can still go on Form 8829 lines 16 and 17. Property tax is subject to the SALT cap ($40,000 for 2025, phasing down at higher incomes to a floor of $10,000).

Can you switch each year?

  • Yes. You choose for each tax year, and the method on your timely filed original return controls; it cannot be changed for that year.
  • Regular to simplified: no depreciation in the simplified year, and any unused regular-method carryover cannot be used that year; it carries forward until you return to the regular method.
  • Simplified to regular: depreciation follows the applicable table.
  • Several homes or businesses: one Form 8829 per home; a home supporting several businesses must be allocated reasonably.

When you sell the home

Depreciation claimed under the regular method may be recaptured on sale; the IRS notes the simplified method involves no depreciation and therefore no recapture. Publication 587 covers how this interacts with the home-sale exclusion. We have not verified every rule there, so have a CPA review before selling.

FAQ

My office exceeds 300 square feet. Can I still use the simplified method?

Yes, but only 300 square feet count, for a maximum of $1,500. Use the regular method to deduct more.

I used the regular method this year. Can I switch next year?

Yes. Each year is a separate choice and is not a change in accounting method, subject to the carryover and depreciation rules above.

Can I still deduct mortgage interest and property tax under the simplified method?

Yes, as personal deductions on Schedule A if you itemize, without allocating any to the business.

Can a W-2 employee working from home deduct a home office?

Not federally; the employee deduction was eliminated from 2018. State rules vary.
