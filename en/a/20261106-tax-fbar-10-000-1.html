What is the FBAR? Reporting foreign accounts above $10,000

Nov 6, 20264 min readYunjumin Forum editors

FBAR (FinCEN 114) thresholds, deadlines, filing steps, how it differs from Form 8938, and penalties in one place.

The FBAR, the Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts, is FinCEN Form 114. US persons (citizens, green-card holders and other residents) with a financial interest in or signature authority over foreign financial accounts must file if those accounts together exceeded $10,000 at any time during the year. It is filed separately through FinCEN's BSA E-Filing system, not with your tax return, by 15 April of the following year, with an automatic extension to 15 October.

Note the aggregation: three accounts of $4,000 each total $12,000, so you file. A single moment above the threshold counts; the year-end balance is irrelevant.

Who must file

  • US citizens, green-card holders and other qualifying tax residents.
  • Corporations, partnerships, LLCs, trusts and estates, which the IRS lists as US persons. Readers who later form an LLC should note that foreign accounts held by the LLC may be reportable.
  • Anyone with a financial interest or signature or other authority, even if the money is not theirs, for example managing an account for family abroad.

Which accounts count

The IRS says, in principle, any account at a financial institution outside the US counts, whether or not it produces taxable income, such as bank accounts in mainland China, Hong Kong or Canada.

Exceptions listed by the IRS include:

  • correspondent or nostro accounts;
  • accounts owned by governmental entities or international financial institutions;
  • accounts at US military banking facilities;
  • accounts held in an IRA or retirement plan.

We found no clear official answer on other types, such as foreign insurance, funds or Alipay/WeChat balances; check the FinCEN instructions or ask a CPA.

Filing steps

  1. List your accounts: institution name, address, account number and type.
  2. Find each account's maximum value for the year: per FinCEN instructions, use the largest amount on periodic statements; without statements, the maximum at any point.
  3. Convert to dollars at the year-end Treasury Fiscal Service rate; if unavailable, use another verifiable rate and state the source.
  4. Go to BSA E-Filing (bsaefiling.fincen.gov); individuals can file without registering an account.
  5. Complete and submit FinCEN 114, keeping the confirmation number and receipt.

Paper filing requires a prior FinCEN exemption. The IRS no longer accepts TD F 90-22.1 or printed Form 114.

Deadlines

ItemDate
Regular deadline15 April of the following year
Automatic extension15 October (no request needed)

For 2025, the deadline was 15 April 2026, extended to 15 October 2026. As of 6 November 2026 that has passed, so anyone who has not filed is late and should catch up promptly. FinCEN also posts disaster-related extensions.

Spouses and joint accounts

  • Joint accounts are not split pro rata; each owner reports the full value.
  • Spouses can file one FBAR if specific conditions are met, with the other spouse completing Form 114a; otherwise each files separately.

FBAR versus Form 8938

Many people assume they are the same. They are separate regimes, and you may need both.

The IRS comparison page also lists higher Form 8938 thresholds for those living abroad: single $200,000 at year-end or $300,000 at any time; joint $400,000 / $600,000.

Penalties

FBAR violations carry civil and criminal penalties; the IRS says amounts depend on facts and circumstances, with civil maximums adjusted for inflation each year.

  • Pre-1 August 2016 figures: non-wilful up to $10,000; wilful up to the greater of $100,000 or 50% of the balance (IRS comparison page).
  • Current adjusted figures: several unofficial sources say that from 17 January 2025 the non-wilful maximum is about $16,536 and the wilful maximum is the greater of $165,353 or 50% of the balance.

Sources differ on whether non-wilful penalties apply per report or per account; several cite the Supreme Court's 2023 Bittner decision for per-report. We did not verify the official text; rely on 31 CFR 1010.821 and the latest IRS figures. Form 8938 has its own penalties: $10,000, plus $10,000 per 30 days after IRS notice, up to $60,000.

FAQ

1. Must green-card holders file?

Yes. "US person" includes residents; green-card holders who meet the $10,000 threshold must file. Ask a CPA if your residency status is unclear.

2. My year-end balance was $3,000. Do I still file?

Possibly. The test is the aggregate at any time in the year, not the year-end balance.

3. Is the FBAR attached to my tax return?

No. It is filed separately through BSA E-Filing; Form 8938 is the one attached to the return.
