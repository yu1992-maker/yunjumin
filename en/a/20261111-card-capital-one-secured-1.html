Capital One Platinum Secured: Deposit Tiers and the Path to an Unsecured Card

Nov 11, 20263 min readYunjumin Forum editors

Deposits are $49, $99 or $200, with up to $1,000 in credit line by adding more. There is no fixed upgrade timeline; conditions, deposit refunds and real-world reports.

The Capital One Platinum Secured deposit is $49, $99 or $200, depending on your credit, for an initial credit line of at least $200. There is no fixed timeline for graduating to an unsecured card; Capital One reviews accounts periodically. Most user reports cite 6 to 12 months, and some receive only a credit-line increase without graduating.

Data from Capital One's product page and official learning-centre articles (checked 11 November 2026). Terms change; check the site before applying.

Note that the $49 or $99 tiers reportedly still come with a $200 line, but this depends on credit and is not guaranteed.

Getting the deposit back

Capital One describes two ways:

  1. Graduate to an unsecured card: the deposit may be returned; the FAQ mentions a statement credit.
  2. Pay off the balance and close the account: the deposit may be returned.

The official wording is "may". Confirm the amount and timing in your account or on the next statement.

Graduating: the official position

  • Capital One sets no fixed timeline; it reviews accounts periodically and notifies eligible customers.
  • An official article says graduation "may happen within a year", depending on payment history and credit score.
  • Capital One publishes no specific criteria (score or number of months).
  • Graduation usually involves no hard inquiry and generally does not affect your score.
  • The site also says you may be automatically considered for a higher line in as little as 6 months, without an additional deposit.

User reports: mixed

With no official timeline, user experience is the guide, though it guarantees nothing:

Improving your odds

From Capital One's own articles:

  1. Pay on time every month, ideally with autopay; a single late payment sets you back.
  2. Pay as much as possible, ideally in full, to avoid 29.24% interest.
  3. Keep utilisation under 30% (citing CFPB guidance). On a $200 line, keep each statement below $60.
  4. Monitor via CreditWise or AnnualCreditReport.com for errors and progress.
  5. Avoid frequent new applications to keep your score stable (general practice, not a stated Capital One rule).

If graduation does not come

  • Raise the line first: watch for an automatic increase after 6 months, or add deposit up to a $1,000 line.
  • Call and ask: an agent can say whether you are under review, but treat the answer as informal.
  • Apply for an unsecured card elsewhere: once your score improves, consider an entry-level unsecured card, accepting a hard inquiry.
  • Keep the old account: its age helps your credit history; do not rush to close it.

FAQ

Q1: $49 or $200 deposit, which is better?

A smaller deposit ties up less cash, though the line often starts at $200 either way. Capital One lets you add deposit later up to a $1,000 line, so starting low is reasonable.

Q2: Is the deposit refunded automatically after graduating?

Capital One says it "may" be, via statement credit per the FAQ. Check your next statement and contact customer service if unclear.

Q3: Does graduation involve a credit check?

Official articles say graduation usually involves no hard inquiry and generally will not lower your score. Your notice and account records prevail.
