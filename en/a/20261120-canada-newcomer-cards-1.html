Credit Cards for Newcomers to Canada with No Credit History: Big Five Banks vs Secured Cards

Nov 20, 20263 min readYunjumin Forum editors

RBC, TD, Scotiabank, CIBC and BMO all run newcomer card programs that don't require Canadian credit history; if declined, secured cards like Neo are the fallback. Limits and conditions compared.

you can get a credit card in Canada without local credit history. All Big Five banks (RBC, TD, Scotiabank, CIBC, BMO) run newcomer card programs whose official pages say Canadian credit history is not required; if you're declined, a secured card is the fallback.

How to choose: for higher limits, start with Scotiabank, RBC or TD (all advertise up to $15,000)—but “up to” isn't a promise; your actual limit depends mostly on verifiable income. If you have good credit history abroad, look at Scotiabank's Nova Credit partnership.

Declined? Secured cards

A secured card needs an upfront deposit that roughly equals your limit—made for people with no credit history. For example:

  • Neo Secured Mastercard: Ratehub and Neo say deposits can start around $50 with no credit check; NerdWallet's review of the Tim Hortons co-branded version lists a $500 minimum. Versions differ—check Neo's site.
  • BMO also says you can get a secured card if not approved for an unsecured one.

Deposits are generally returned only when you close the account or graduate to an unsecured card, so only deposit money you won't need soon.

FAQ

Can I apply without a SIN?

It varies. TD and CIBC list a SIN on their application pages; RBC says a SIN is optional for opening an account. Get your SIN first if you can.

Can international students apply?

Scotiabank StartRight explicitly covers international students; CIBC has separate student cards with lower limits. Check other banks' sites.

Should I apply to several banks at once?

Each application usually triggers a credit inquiry. When starting out, get one card, pay on time for 6+ months, then consider a second.
